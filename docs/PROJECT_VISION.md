# Project Vision

> Decisions referenced here are recorded in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md). If this document and the register ever disagree, the register is authoritative.

## The ambition

We want to create an immersive, illustrated storytelling experience built on the tales of **Vikram and Betal**, the collection known as *Betal Pachisi* (the "twenty-five tales of the Betal"). (D-001)

We take storytelling seriously. The aim is not to put old stories online. It is to let a reader **enter** a story: to be narrated to, to see it, to hear it, and to feel the night, the forest, and the weight of each riddle the Betal poses to the king. (D-004)

## The intended reader experience

**It should feel like entering a story, not visiting a website.** Everything the reader meets should belong to the telling: the narration, the illustrations, the atmosphere, and the sound. Navigation and controls should serve that experience and not interrupt it. *How* this is achieved is still OPEN. See [OPEN_QUESTIONS.md](OPEN_QUESTIONS.md). (D-004)

**It should work on two levels.** (D-005)

- *The surface story.* Anyone can follow and enjoy the tale with no philosophical background: the characters, the strangeness, the suspense, and the riddle at the end.
- *The deeper story.* A more attentive reader finds layers of meaning, human psychology, and perspectives that invite reflection and reward rereading. These layers should **emerge from the story**. They should not be attached to it.

**It should be narrated, not lectured.** The narrator's voice changes with the story. Sometimes it is quiet, sometimes playful, sometimes tense. It is never a fixed sage delivering commentary. (D-007, D-008; see [EDITORIAL_GUIDE.md](EDITORIAL_GUIDE.md))

**It should be seen.** Illustrations follow a cinematic rhythm. They are held through a scene, and dedicated artwork marks turning points. They should be unmistakably Indian in character. (D-023, D-024; see [ART_DIRECTION.md](ART_DIRECTION.md))

**It should be felt in its environment.** Time of day, weather, setting, emotional atmosphere, and dramatic intensity shape the reading experience. The story leads these changes, and they happen at natural moments. (D-026)

**It should be heard, gently.** Ambient sound, instrumental music, sparing sound accents, and deliberate silence support the reading and never compete with it. (D-027; see [AUDIO_DIRECTION.md](AUDIO_DIRECTION.md))

**It should be readable in Hindi and English.** Hindi is written first. English is a literary adaptation in its own right. (D-010 to D-013)

## Scope

| In scope now | Not in scope now |
|---|---|
| The 25 Betal Pachisi tales (D-001) | Other Indian folk story collections, which are possible later (D-002) |
| Hindi and English at launch (D-010) | Marathi, Kannada, and other languages. The design should eventually allow them, but they are not launch requirements (D-014). |
| Research, editorial process, and creative direction | Choosing a technology stack or architecture, which has not been approved (D-030) |

The exact frame of the experience is still OPEN. That includes how the outer story of King Vikram and the Betal is presented around the 25 tales, and in what order the tales are produced. See [OPEN_QUESTIONS.md](OPEN_QUESTIONS.md).

## Guiding principles

1. **The story comes first.** Every element (text, image, sound, layout, interaction) is judged by whether it serves the telling.
2. **Broad imagination, disciplined relevance.** Writers have wide freedom to deepen a story, but every addition must stay faithful to the story's identity and essential events. Do not turn a small moment into an unrelated epic. (D-009)
3. **No forced morals.** Many of these tales end in a dilemma, not a lesson. Keep that openness, and allow several interpretations where the story supports them. (D-006)
4. **Respect the sources.** Know which source and edition a story comes from, what the variants say, and which parts are our own invention. (D-016, D-017)
5. **Quality through revision.** No story is final after one pass. At least three substantial drafts, independent critique and verification, and the user's approval are required. (D-019 to D-022)
6. **Indian at its core.** The visual, literary, and sonic identity should come from Indian traditions. It should not be a generic "exotic" layer over a Western template. (D-024)
7. **Simple where possible.** The eventual application should be simple and maintainable, with that simplicity serving the experience. (D-030)
8. **Content is protected.** Approved story texts are creative work and must be kept safe from engineering changes. (D-015)

## What this project is

- A literary retelling of the Betal Pachisi, researched against its textual traditions.
- An illustrated, atmospheric, sound-supported reading experience.
- A bilingual project in which Hindi leads and English is crafted afterwards.
- A long-term creative work, with the user acting as creative director.

## What this project is not

- **Not a conventional website** with stories placed on pages.
- **Not a moral-of-the-story collection.** It does not reduce each tale to a lesson.
- **Not a philosophy lecture** wrapped in a story, and not a platform for commentary unconnected to the tale.
- **Not a word-for-word translation** between Hindi and English.
- **Not mass-produced content.** One-shot generated stories are not acceptable as final work.
- **Not an imitation of any person.** The project may learn storytelling techniques from others, including Osho, but it does not imitate anyone's identity or writing. (D-018)
- **Not a copy of a reference design.** References inform the project. They are not templates. (D-031)
- **Not yet an application.** No technology has been chosen, and nothing in these documents describes a feature that exists.
