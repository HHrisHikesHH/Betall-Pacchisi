The existing evidence (every core tale read in full in KSS, the questions and answers of Ś and the Hindi, J 21–23 in full, and the Hindi XXII/XXIV comparison) is enough to understand each candidate's story and dilemma and to compare them. ## N4: Other Jambhaladatta tales

1. **What is unknown.** The content of most J tales. The repository has read J 10 (report §8.1) and J 13 and J 21–23 (N3). J 25's reading status is unresolved: the comparison report gives inconsistent accounts of its J coverage (§2.2, §7, §15), as catalogue §1.4 records. Unread: J 24 (TC-016; N3 saw its opening in passing, Emeneau Tr p. 141); J's answers for TC-007 and TC-009, which the report gives without a documented reading (catalogue §1.4); and J's other tales, whose details Emeneau says "differ widely" from the other versions (catalogue §10.1).
2. **Candidates affected.** TC-016 (J 24); TC-007 (J 7); TC-009 (J 9); TC-024 (J 25's reading status is unresolved; J's omission of the key marriage detail is recorded through Emeneau §6, per Report §7, which does not establish a full reading of J 25); in principle every core family J contains (all except TC-012 and TC-014).
3. **Could it change eligibility, distinctness, source choice or selection?** **Eligibility and distinctness: no.** A J variant is a variant inside a family; it would not merge two families or create a new one. **Source choice: yes**, for any tale where J's version might be preferred under D-034. **Selection: only marginally**: a striking J variant (as with TC-010's reversed answer) could make a tale more attractive, but it would not make a tale ineligible.
4. **Needed before choosing 25?** No.
5. **Could it wait for a shortlist?** Yes.
6. **Smallest useful investigation.** For shortlisted tales only: read J's question and answer passages (and the immediately preceding narrative where the answer seems to differ) in Emeneau. Order of value: **J 24 (TC-016)**, because N3 noted that it opens, like J 23, with a weeping mother, which bears on group 8 in [COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md](COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md); then **J 7 and J 9** to document or withdraw the report's undocumented answers. Each is a few pages of an edition already used by N3.

**Priority: Targeted check only if a candidate is a serious contender.** Choosing a primary source (Q-401) would not by itself change this: under D-034 the candidate pool is multi-recension, and a primary-source choice does not remove candidates from it. That J's Bengali text lacks TC-012 and TC-014 bears on **source availability** (those tales would have to be taken from another tradition, with the borrowing recorded) and would matter only under a strict single-recension approach; it is not an automatic change to candidate eligibility under the current rules. N4 would rise in importance if the owner wanted to use J's own versions of the chosen tales.

## N5: Specialist Hindi/Braj reading

1. **What is unknown.** How the printed Hindi wording reads to a specialist at passages where the evidence rests on AI readings of one printed text (Barker 1855 page images) and on Platts's English: Hindi IX (TC-010: the husband "showed her no affection"), XV (TC-016: the caste grounding of the hero's "practice"), XX (TC-021: *adhik kāmī* against "greatest love"), XXIV (TC-023: whether the father actually acquires the art, *sīkhkar*), plus XIX (TC-020: the boy's death; "laughed, then wept"), XVI (TC-017: Unmādinī), XIV (TC-015: the pregnancy) and VII (TC-009: *śāstra*/*śastra*) (Report §15; N1 §5.3).
2. **Candidates affected.** TC-009, 010, 015, 016, 017, 020, 021, 023.
3. **Could it change eligibility, distinctness, source choice or selection?** **Eligibility: no.** **Distinctness: no.** TC-023's count is settled by the owner and must not be reopened; N2 noted that a specialist reading of *sīkhkar* could strengthen the case for XXIV as a separate story, but that would now be relevant only to how the one slot is told. **Source choice: yes**, if the Hindi lineage is used for a tale's details. **Selection: unlikely**; each of these variants is a nuance of an ending or reasoning, and the KSS version of every affected tale is read in full.
4. **Needed before choosing 25?** No.
5. **Could it wait?** Yes: until the 25 are chosen and N8/Q-401 decide whether the Hindi lineage is used, and then only for the chosen tales whose Hindi wording is to be relied on.
6. **Smallest useful investigation.** A Hindi/Braj specialist's reading of the specific sentences already listed, for chosen tales only. This needs a human specialist (Q-405); an AI re-reading would not meet the purpose (D-020).

**Priority: Defer until after the 25 are chosen.**

## N6: Verify the Kṣemendra concordance

1. **What is unknown.** Whether BKM's 25 *Vetālakathā* headings correspond tale by tale to KSS's numbering. The BKM column is provisional for 22 of 24 tales; only BKM 10 and the Unmādinī story (KSS 17) were checked on page images (catalogue §1.4).
2. **Candidates affected.** All 24 core families in their BKM column; nothing else.
3. **Could it change eligibility, distinctness, source choice or selection?** **No** for eligibility and distinctness: every core family is already attested in KSS (read in full), Ś and the Hindi, so its existence does not depend on BKM. **Source choice: marginally**: BKM is described as shorter and balder than KSS (Report §4.A), so it is unlikely to be a preferred source for a tale's telling. **Selection: no.**
4. **Needed before choosing 25?** No.
5. **Could it wait?** Yes; it may not be needed at all unless a chosen tale is to draw on Kṣemendra.
6. **Smallest useful investigation.** If ever needed: page images of the BKM headings for the chosen tales only (catalogue §10.1 already suggests "at least for tales under serious consideration").

**Priority: Not necessary for this selection stage.** One exception is folded into N7: the two embedded stories listed after the second heading.

## N7: The unique 25th story in Ś MS *c*; Kṣemendra's two embedded stories

1. **What is unknown.** (a) The content of the "further, unique 25th story" in Śivadāsa manuscript *c*, which Uhle notes but which the repository has not read (Report §4.C). (b) The content of the two embedded stories (on the wickedness of men and of women) that the BKM contents list after the second *Vetālakathā*, and their relation to TC-003 (Report §4.A).
2. **Candidates affected.** (a) None directly; it could add an outside candidate. (b) Probably TC-003, whose KSS telling contains two embedded stories of exactly that kind (N1 TC-003); this is an inference.
3. **Could it change eligibility, distinctness, source choice or selection?** (a) **Possibly selection**, if the story proved to be a distinct, complete Betal-narrated tale and the owner wanted it. It is the only lead in the register with a plausible route to a new candidate ([SUPPLEMENTARY_MATERIAL_REGISTER.md](SUPPLEMENTARY_MATERIAL_REGISTER.md) item 1). (b) **Unlikely**; if they are TC-003's embedded stories, they are part of an existing candidate.
4. **Needed before choosing 25?** **No, under present conditions.** The pool has 27 candidates for 25 places. The item becomes needed only if **the owner excludes more than two candidates from the 27, leaving fewer than 25 acceptable candidates** (for example, if all three J-only candidates are ruled out, since only 24 candidates are core), or if the owner identifies a **genuine gap** that an outside tale could fill ([SELECTION_OVERVIEW.md](SELECTION_OVERVIEW.md) §5).
5. **Could it wait?** Yes, until the owner's first pass shows whether the 27 can supply 25.
6. **Smallest useful investigation.** (a) Locate in Uhle 1881 (already used by N1) whether he prints, summarises or only mentions MS *c*'s story; a page-level search, not a reading of the whole edition. If he prints it, read that one story. (b) Page images of the BKM headings around the second *Vetālakathā* and the opening verses of each embedded story, enough to see whether they are TC-003's two tales.

**Priority: Not necessary for this selection stage, with a named trigger** (fewer than 25 acceptable candidates, or an owner-identified gap). If triggered, it becomes the one item needed before selection.

## N8: Which Hindi text should be authoritative

1. **What is unknown.** If the Hindi (*Baitāl Pachīsī*) lineage is used, which text governs: the 1805 Calcutta print (not located), Barker 1855, Forbes (the text Platts translates), a later edition, or the Braj original (not located) (Report §14 Q8; N1 §5.1).
2. **Candidates affected.** Every candidate whose Hindi variant might be used; in practice the eight listed under N5 and TC-023 (both Hindi variants).
3. **Could it change eligibility, distinctness, source choice or selection?** **Source choice only.** It does not affect which stories exist or how they compare.
4. **Needed before choosing 25?** No. It follows Q-401 (primary tradition) and Q-408 (source hierarchy), both open and both the owner's.
5. **Could it wait?** Yes, until the owner decides whether the Hindi lineage is a source at all.
6. **Smallest useful investigation.** First an owner decision (is the Hindi lineage used, and for what). Then a bibliographic check of the available Hindi texts for the chosen tales, followed by N5 where wording matters.

**Priority: Defer until after the 25 are chosen.**

## N9: Moralising and gendered passages

1. **What is unknown.** A complete list, per shortlisted tale, of moralising and gendered passages **across versions**. The KSS layer is listed (N1 §4.4: TC-003's answer and the thief's remark; TC-014's answer; TC-021's narrator aside; TC-009's caste reasoning; TC-024's line on a helpless woman) and the J layer for TC-025–027 (N3 §6.4). Known but **unread** in their narrative context: Ś's maxims in Ś 3, 9 and 13; the Hindi maxims in XIII, XVII and XIX (N1 §4.4; catalogue §10.1). Ś and Hindi narrative bodies were mostly not read (N1 §1.1).
2. **Candidates affected.** All, in principle; most clearly TC-003, 009, 010, 014, 017, 020, 021, 024, 025 (each bucket's §7 lists what is known).
3. **Could it change eligibility, distinctness, source choice or selection?** **Eligibility: no**: D-006 does not make difficult material a reason to exclude, and the owner has said so in the brief for this dossier. **Selection: possibly**, if the owner weighs **handling difficulty** in choosing; but the known passages, already summarised in each bucket, are enough for that weighing. **Source choice: yes**: some moralising sits in one version only (for example the lover's verses on women in Ś 9 and Hindi IX, TC-010), and D-034 lets a retelling choose among versions.
4. **Needed before choosing 25?** No. The existing lists cover the KSS and J layers, which are the main texts.
5. **Could it wait?** Yes, for a shortlist.
6. **Smallest useful investigation.** For the owner's shortlist: (a) consolidate the KSS/J items already recorded (done in each bucket's §7); (b) read the Ś and Hindi passages already identified as moralising (Ś 3, 9, 13; Hindi XIII, XVII, XIX) in context, for shortlisted tales only. Do not read every Ś and Hindi narrative body.

**Priority: Targeted check only if a candidate is a serious contender.**
