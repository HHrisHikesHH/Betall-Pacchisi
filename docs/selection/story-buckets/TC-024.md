# TC-024: The father who marries the daughter and the son who marries the mother (the final riddle)

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Viable core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record and §6.4](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md), [comparison report](../../research/SOURCE_TRADITIONS_COMPARISON.md).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** The father who marries the daughter and the son who marries the mother (Tawney's title as a catalogue label, not a project title).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 24 (12.31.1–75, Tawney ch. 98) · BKM 24 (provisional) · Ś 24 · J 25 · Hindi XXV · Ryder 22. Match **E** (the report notes Vikrama falls silent).
- **Essential events** (KSS base, N1 TC-024):
  1. A southern feudal king, Dharma, his wife Candravatī and their daughter Lāvaṇyavatī are deposed by kinsmen and flee at night toward the queen's father in Mālava. In the Vindhya forest they reach a settlement of *śabara* tribesmen (a forest people) who rush to rob them. The king fights, kills many and is killed (5–20).
  2. The queen and daughter flee deeper and sit weeping by a lotus pond (20–23).
  3. A local chief, Caṇḍasiṃha, hunting with his son Siṃhaparākrama, sees two lines of footprints. The father tells the son to follow them and take the woman he likes; the son says the small-footed one is young and suits him, the large-footed one suits his father. The father objects that his wife has only just died; the son presses him with a verse and makes him swear (24–34).
  4. They find the women, reassure them and take them home. The queen accepts "as if in another birth" (35–50).
  5. Since the queen has the smaller feet, the son takes the mother, Candravatī, and the father the daughter, Lāvaṇyavatī, keeping the word given at the footprints. Each woman is now the other's daughter-in-law and mother-in-law (51–54). Both couples have children, who in turn have children (55–56).
- **Central characters and relationships:** the dead king; a mother and daughter; a chief and his son, now their husbands.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** the cross-generational marriages and the riddle about what the children are to each other.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** Of the children born to the son and the mother, and to the father and the daughter, what are they to each other? (KSS 12.31.58–59).
- **Vikrama answers:** **None in KSS.** The king thought it over in many ways but did not know (*nājñāsīt*) and walked on in silence (60). The Vetāla laughs inwardly (61–62) and then reveals the monk's plot and warns the king (63–74). [Primary: KSS e-text, AI reading]
- **Reasoning:** None recorded. N1 notes the text gives ignorance as the reason; "unanswered by design" is an interpretation of the narrative, not a statement of the text (N1 TC-024; Cat. TC-024).
- **Across versions:** Ś 24: the king gives no reply; no reason is stated in Ś 24, but the Vetāla's opening words in Ś 25 (Uhle p. 61) may give one (OCR, provisional) [Primary, Uhle via OCR]. Hindi XXV: the king "held his tongue through ignorance"; the Vetāla, "in great glee", praises him and warns him about the ascetic, inside the same numbered tale [Translation, Platts]. J 25: omits the key marriage detail (Emeneau, per Report §7) [Secondary]. Same outcome (silence) in the three versions examined.
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** Not a choice but a puzzle: tangled kin relations have no ready word. If Vikrama cannot name the relation, what does that say about how families and categories hold together? [Inference]

## 5. Storytelling potential [Inference throughout]

- **Hook:** A comic-sounding puzzle: whose children are cousins, siblings and more all at once?
- **Story engine:** Exile and a forest attack, followed by a promise made over footprints.
- **Stakes:** The family's survival; the later puzzle is intellectual.
- **Characters:** A king who dies protecting his family; a mother and daughter in shock; a chief and son bound by a hasty promise.
- **Emotional experience:** Grim and ironic (N1): the humour of the footprints beside the women's helplessness; then the king's puzzled silence.
- **Distinctive device:** The only riddle left unanswered in KSS; the bafflement is the point, and the Vetāla's reaction opens the frame's last act.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** As a puzzle, yes, but it has no answer to argue for; the interest is the tangle and the king's silence rather than a contested choice.
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Possibly: the reader can try to untangle the relation and fail.
- **Retelling difficulty:** J omits the marriage detail the puzzle needs; the puzzle requires precise statement of relations; the frame's conclusion follows immediately, so the hand-over needs care. The women's lack of choice (see below) is central to events.

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- **Coerced remarriage and women's helplessness:** the queen accepts "what can a helpless woman do, fallen into misfortune in a foreign land?" (KSS 12.31.50; N1 §4.4 lists it). Source-backed; needs later examination (N9).
- **Marriage across generations:** a mother and daughter marry a father and son. They are not related to the men, so this is a kinship tangle, not incest; but the riddle's wording will read oddly to some audiences. Inference.
- **A forest people (*śabara*) shown as robbers** (13–20). Source-backed; ethnic and caste portrayal needs later examination.
- **Death of a king in defence of his family** (13–20). Source-backed.
- None excludes the tale by itself (D-006).

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)
