# TC-021: Anaṅgamañjarī, Maṇivarman and Kamalākara

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Strong core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md), [comparison report §8.4](../../research/SOURCE_TRADITIONS_COMPARISON.md).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** Anaṅgamañjarī, Maṇivarman and Kamalākara (catalogue label, not a project title).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 21 (12.28.1–97, Tawney ch. 95) · BKM 21 (provisional) · Ś 20 · J 18 · Hindi XX (Barker p. 332) · Ryder 19. Match **P**: the Hindi reframes the question; same answer in KSS, Ś and Hindi.
- **Essential events** (KSS base, N1 TC-021):
  1. In Viśālā the merchant Arthadatta marries his only daughter Anaṅgamañjarī to Maṇivarman, a merchant's son from Tāmraliptī, but keeps the couple at home. She finds her husband hateful "like bitter medicine"; he loves her more than life (3–10).
  2. While he is away visiting his parents she sees Kamalākara, the king's chaplain's son, from her window; they fall in love at a glance (11–24). In despair she goes to the garden tank, prays to the goddess Caṇḍikā for him in another birth and ties a noose; her friend Mālatikā cuts it (25–34).
  3. Mālatikā arranges a tryst in the garden. When Kamalākara enters, Anaṅgamañjarī runs and embraces him and dies on the spot, her breath stopped by joy. He faints, revives, embraces her body and his heart bursts from grief (35–78).
  4. At dawn the kin find them. Maṇivarman returns, sees his wife dead with another man and, still loving her, dies at once (79–84).
  5. The goddess Caṇḍī, petitioned for her devotee Arthadatta, orders that all three live "with passion stilled" (*śāntānaṅgāḥ*). They rise as if from sleep, free of desire; Kamalākara goes home ashamed; Arthadatta takes his daughter and son-in-law home and celebrates (85–91). [N1 correction: the catalogue and the report's earlier summaries stopped at the deaths.]
- **Central characters and relationships:** a wife, her unloved but loving husband, her lover; the confidante Mālatikā; her father and the goddess.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** a triple death from love, then a revival, and a question about who was most overcome.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** Among these, deluded by passion, whose delusion was greater? (KSS 12.28.93, *adhiko moho 'nurāgamūḍheṣu*).
- **Vikrama answers:** Maṇivarman, the husband (94).
- **Reasoning:** The lovers' passion was mutual and ripened over time, and their deaths are understandable. The husband, seeing his wife dead with another man, had reason for anger but instead, still in love, gave up his life: "excessively deluded" (95–96). [Primary: KSS e-text, AI reading]
- **Across versions:** Same answer in all three read. The **question's framing** differs: KSS "deluded by passion"; Ś 20 "who was blind with desire?" (*kāmāndhaḥ*); Hindi XX (Barker p. 332): *adhik kāmī*, roughly "most full of desire", where the English translators render it as love ("whose love was greatest"; Platts: "the deepest lover"). The report calls the difference "real but partial, and partly a translation effect", not an opposite moral framing; the connotation of *kāmī* in this register is a question for a specialist (Report §8.4) [Translation, Platts and Barker; Hindi wording provisional, AI reading of one printed text]. J 18: not documented. Whether Ś 20 and Hindi XX also narrate the revival was not checked (N1).
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** The surface question is who was most blinded by passion. Underneath it asks whether love that does not stop for betrayal is devotion or folly, and whether dying of grief for someone who never loved you is a greater delusion than dying for mutual love. The shifting question words (deluded, blind, desirous, loving) change which of those a reader hears. [Inference]

## 5. Storytelling potential [Inference throughout]

- **Hook:** Three people die of love in one night.
- **Story engine:** Forbidden love, a go-between's plan, a tryst that ends in joy-death, then the husband's arrival.
- **Stakes:** Emotional and total (life), softened by the revival.
- **Characters:** A wife who cannot bear her husband, a husband whose love will not stop, a young lover, a quick-thinking friend.
- **Emotional experience:** Intensity bordering on tragicomedy; N1 calls it "almost tragicomic in its triple death and sober revival".
- **Distinctive device:** Deaths from emotion (joy, grief, grief), each causally linked, then a divine reset.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Yes: it ranks three kinds of excess, and the husband's answer is counter-intuitive.
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Yes; the reader will probably feel the husband's pathos before being told it.
- **Retelling difficulty:** The revival, "with passion stilled", undercuts the tragedy and may feel abrupt. The question's wording must be chosen (deluded, blind, loving), since it steers the reader. The narrator's aside against women (see below) is in the source.

## 6. Contribution to the collection

- **Offers:** A love-triangle with a three-way comparison, a tonal mix of tragedy and irony, and a built-in choice of how to frame the question (D-005, D-006; the report notes this bears on layered meaning).
- **Nearest overlaps:** TC-010, TC-014, TC-005/009 and TC-006 also centre on lovers and spouses; a shared theme, not a shared dilemma. TC-012 (a heart bursts from grief) shares a death from grief and the "reaction" territory in a loose sense; a motif only. TC-021's question form is a superlative comparison, the commonest in the pool (8 of 24, N1 §4.1).
- **Variety:** Adds a pure love-and-passion tale; risks adding to the cluster of wives, lovers and faithfulness (Cat. §6.3).
- **Comparison buckets:** none in [COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md](../COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md).

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)

**Provisional label:** **Strong core contender**

- **Strongest reason to include:** A distinct dilemma with the same answer across KSS, Ś and Hindi, a vivid chain of events, and a question that can be heard in several ways without changing the plot.
- **Strongest reason to hesitate:** Passion-deaths followed by a quick revival may feel melodramatic, and the story belongs to the large cluster of love and fidelity tales; the narrator's aside needs handling.
- **What could change this assessment:** A specialist reading of *kāmī* and of the Hindi passage; reading J 18 and the revival in Ś and Hindi; the owner's weighing of the love cluster.
- **Confidence:** Moderate. The events and question are fully read in KSS and Ś Q&A; the Hindi nuance rests on one AI reading of a printed page.
