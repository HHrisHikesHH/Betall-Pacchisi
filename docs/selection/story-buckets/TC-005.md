# TC-005: Somaprabhā and her three suitors

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Strong core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md) (N1 TC-005).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** Somaprabhā and her three suitors (a catalogue label).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 5 (12.12.1–50, Tawney ch. 79) · BKM 5 (provisional) · Ś 5 · J 6 (J places it sixth) · Hindi V · Ryder 5. Match E.
- **Essential events** (N1 TC-005, KSS 12.12.5–45):
  1. In Ujjayinī, the brahman minister Harisvāmin has a son and a beautiful daughter, Somaprabhā. Proud of her beauty, she says she must be given only to a hero, a man of knowledge or a man of skill (5–9).
  2. Three brahmans appear. A man of skill builds a flying chariot and is promised her by her father; a man claiming heroism is promised her by her brother; a man of knowledge, claiming to know past and future, is promised her by her mother. Each promise is for the same day and made without the others' knowledge (11–27).
  3. On the wedding day Somaprabhā has vanished. The man of knowledge reveals that the Rākṣasa Dhūmaśikha has carried her to the Vindhya forest. The man of skill builds an armed flying chariot and takes them there; the hero kills the Rākṣasa with an arrow (28–38).
  4. Back home, the three quarrel at the auspicious hour, each claiming that without his part she could not have been recovered (39–45).
- **Central characters and relationships:** Somaprabhā (bride; she sets the conditions and says nothing after her abduction); her father, mother and brother, who each promise her; three brahman suitors (hero, seer, craftsman); the Rākṣasa Dhūmaśikha (a demon).
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** a bride promised three times to three suitors with different gifts, and a rescue in which all three are needed.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** to whom should she be given? (KSS 12.12.46).
- **Vikrama answers:** to the hero, who won her at the risk of his life by killing the Rākṣasa (12.12.48).
- **Reasoning:** the man of knowledge and the man of skill were made by the Creator as instruments, like an astrologer and a carpenter, who exist to serve others (12.12.49).
- **Across versions:** Ś 5 [Primary, OCR]: same answer; the Vetāla objects that all were equal and the king replies that the other two were instruments, with a verse on daring and valour. Hindi V [Translation, Platts]: same answer; the others "simply conferred favours, for which they were recompensed", a slightly different reason. J 6: not recorded in the repository evidence as to answer.
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** the surface question is who wins the bride; the deeper conflict is whether the one who risks the most deserves the most when the others' contributions were also indispensable. [Inference]

## 5. Storytelling potential [Inference throughout]

- **Hook:** a proud bride, three impossible promises, and a bride who vanishes on the wedding day.
- **Story engine:** a farce of triple promises that turns into a rescue adventure, then back into a quarrel.
- **Stakes:** a bride's abduction and a fight to the death; clear and compelling, though little interior emotion is recorded (N1).
- **Characters:** three specialists, each with a showpiece (chariot, weapons, foresight); the bewildered father is a ready comic note.
- **Emotional experience:** comic confusion, then adventure, then the anticlimax of a petty dispute.
- **Distinctive device or turn:** the three skills are used in sequence in the rescue, so the dilemma emerges from the plot.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Yes: all three were necessary, which is what makes it hard.
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Yes, through the family's own tangle of promises and the reader's sense of what each did.
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** Somaprabhā is a passive prize after the first scene; the answer ranks risk above knowledge and craft, which a retelling may want to keep contestable.

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- A woman is promised by each family member without her say and is later fought over (KSS 12.12.22–45); she sets her own conditions at the start (8–9). Source-backed.
- Violence is limited to an epic-style fight (36–38). The answer's ranking of a warrior above a seer and a craftsman reflects a hierarchy of roles in Vikrama's own words (49). No moral verdict; no gendered generalisation recorded. N9 examination pending. Not a reason for exclusion (D-006).
