# TC-018: The brahman's son who failed to gain the magic power

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Strong core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md) (TC-018), [comparison report §7](../../research/SOURCE_TRADITIONS_COMPARISON.md), [N3 §6.1](../../research/JAMBHALADATTA_ADDITIONAL_TALES_EVIDENCE.md).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** The brahman's son who failed to gain the magic power (Tawney's title; a catalogue label). N1 names him Candrasvāmin.
- **Recorded in:** KSS 18 (12.25.1–87, Tawney ch. 92) · BKM 18 (provisional) · Ś 17 · J 15 · Hindi XVII · Ryder 17. The Nepali J manuscript (W) omits it (absence from one manuscript only). Emeneau notes carelessness in J's riddle solutions for J 5, 15 and 16 (Emeneau Intro. §6, p. xix, as cited in N3 §4), so J 15's answer should be read with that caution.
- **Essential events (KSS, N1 TC-018):**
  - *Frame episode:* at the tree the king finds many identical corpses, each possessed by a Vetāla; he resolves to enter the fire if the night fails; the Vetāla withdraws the illusion (12.25.3–7). This is unique in the cycle (N1).
  1. Candrasvāmin, a learned brahman's son addicted to gambling, loses everything, is beaten and left for dead, and shelters in an empty Śiva temple (13–30).
  2. An ascetic summons a wish-granting science that creates a golden city for him each night; it vanishes at dawn (31–47).
  3. He begs to be taught. The ascetic warns it is gained only by reciting under water, which creates an illusory life: birth, marriage, children. Only one who, roused by the teacher at twenty-four, remembers his true life and still enters a fire within the illusion succeeds; an unworthy pupil costs the teacher his own science (48–58).
  4. In the river he lives an illusory life. The ascetic awakens him; he faces the pyre, sees his parents, wife and children weeping, wavers, then decides to enter (59–75). The fire feels cold; he comes out of the river (76).
  5. The teacher is puzzled and calls on the science; it does not appear to either; both have lost it and leave dejected (77–81).
- **Central characters and relationships:** Candrasvāmin; the ascetic teacher; the illusory family; the gamblers.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** a rite performed as instructed that fails, in an illusory lifetime.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** why was the science lost to both although the rite was done as instructed (*vihite 'pi yathoddiṣṭe karmaṇi*, KSS 12.25.83)? [Primary]
- **Vikrama answers:** success is not gained by an act merely correct in form; it needs a steady mind, free of wavering (*vikalpa*) and of pure resolve. The pupil wavered even after he was awakened; the teacher lost his science by giving it to an unworthy recipient (12.25.84–86). [Primary]
- **Reasoning:** the failure is placed in the moment of inner hesitation, not in the outward act. [Primary]
- **Across versions:**
  - Ś 17: a yakṣiṇī (the science's presiding power) does not come; question: why did she not come? Answer: the adept was double-minded (*dvidhācitta*); he went to visit his family at the time of the practice; the yogin should not have given the science to such a pupil; a closing verse says a man's intellect follows his *karma* (N1) [Primary, OCR; PROVISIONAL].
  - Hindi XVII: the same reply; ends that a man obtains only what fate has recorded [Translation].
  - Catalogue corrected by N1: "fate" is not a Hindi-only addition relative to Ś; it is already in Ś's closing verse and absent from KSS 12.25.85–86.
  - J 15: not read.
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** surface: why did a correct rite fail. Deeper: whether one can be blamed for a wavering that came from love for a family, even an illusory one; whether sincerity or resolve is the true price. The text asks Vikrama to explain an outcome by an inner state (N1). [Inference]

## 4. Common story spine and variant differences

**Common spine:** a pupil performs a magic rite and loses its power by a wavering mind (as far as Q&A shows). In KSS the body (gambling, illusion, fire) is read fully; Ś is read at the question, answer and fire episode; the Hindi at ending only.

## 5. Storytelling potential

- **Hook:** a gambler beaten and left for dead gets a wish-granting city each night. [Inference]
- **Story engine:** a rule-governed ordeal: conditions are stated in advance (12.25.50–57) and then tested.
- **Stakes:** the pupil's lifetime of illusion, the teacher's power.
- **Characters:** the pupil is flawed and sympathetic; the ascetic is a stern teacher.
- **Emotional experience:** shame, wonder, pathos at the weeping family (12.25.72–74), then deflation.
- **Distinctive device or turn:** a life within an illusion; a failure revealed only at the end by a cold fire. N1 describes the "centre" as a lifetime compressed into an illusion and a single moment of doubt.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Yes: a causal puzzle about inner states. [Inference]
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Yes: the wavering moment is dramatic; the answer's maxim (success needs a steady mind) is a lecture risk.
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** Nested reality needs clear handling; the many-corpses frame episode is unique and is a frame device (the frame is not counted in the 25, D-035); *vikalpa* has several senses.

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- Violence: a beating with sticks and being left for dead (12.25.19–20) [Primary]. A sorcerer-ascetic (a *mahāvratī*) uses a lure of women in an illusory city (31–43).
- Religious: a Śaiva temple and a science that a teacher can lose; a wife and children as illusion.
- Not much gendered or moralising material is recorded beyond the maxims in Ś and Hindi XVII (N9 would examine them).

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)

**Provisional label:** **Strong core contender**

- **Strongest reason to include:** a distinct, imaginative device (an illusory lifetime) and a dilemma about inner steadiness.
- **Strongest reason to hesitate:** the answer is abstract and can tip into a maxim; the nested illusion needs skilful telling; J 15 is unread (and Emeneau calls J 15's riddle solution careless). The question form sits close to the four reaction riddles (group 3), which adds to that cluster's weight.
- **What could change this assessment:** reading J 15; the owner's view on TC-025's similar question form.
- **Confidence:** moderate. KSS full; other versions partial.
