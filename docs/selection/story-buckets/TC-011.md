## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** King Dharmadhvaja's three delicate wives (Tawney's title; a catalogue label, not a project title).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 11 (12.18.1–39, Tawney ch. 85) · BKM 11 (provisional) · Ś 10 · J 11 (content not read by the repository) · Hindi X · Ryder 10 (Ryder's numbering is one lower from here because it omits KSS 10).
- **Essential events (KSS, N1 TC-011):**
  1. King Dharmadhvaja of Ujjayinī has three beloved queens: Indulekhā, Tārāvalī and Mṛgāṅkavatī (12.18.3–5).
  2. At a spring festival a blue lotus falls from Indulekhā's ear and wounds her thigh; she cries out and faints (6–14).
  3. That night, moonbeams through a lattice fall on Tārāvalī's uncovered body; she wakes burned and blistered (*visphoṭa*) and is laid on wet lotus leaves (15–21).
  4. Mṛgāṅkavatī, on her way to the king, hears a pestle pounding grain far off, cries that she is dead, and is carried back; marks (*kiṇa*, "calluses, weals"; Penzer "bruises") are found on her hands (22–28).
  5. The king reflects that their very refinement has turned into a fault through fate; by morning physicians have them well (29–34).
- **Central characters and relationships:** the king and three co-wives, not shown as rivals.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** three escalating extreme sensitivities, the last caused by a mere sound.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** which queen was the most delicate (*abhijātatarā*, KSS 12.18.36)? [Primary]
- **Vikrama answers:** **Mṛgāṅkavatī**, because marks rose on her hands from the mere sound of the pestle without its touching her; the other two were hurt by actual contact with the lotus and moonbeams (12.18.37–38). [Primary]
- **Reasoning:** injury without contact ranks above injury by contact. [Primary]
- **Across versions:** the answer is the third queen in Ś 10 and Hindi X [Primary (OCR) / Translation]. The injuries differ: in Ś the third queen gets pain in her hand; in the Hindi a headache and a faint; in both Ś and the Hindi the lotus injures the first queen's foot (N1).
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** the surface question is a ranking of three hyperbolic sensitivities. There is little deeper conflict beyond how to measure "delicate" (by cause, by contact, by effect). [Inference] N1 describes the tale as "hyperbolic and courtly".

## 5. Storytelling potential

- **Hook:** three queens each hurt by something absurdly slight; the escalation is the draw. [Inference]
- **Story engine:** a triple structure, each test lighter in cause and greater in effect (N1).
- **Stakes:** low. The injuries heal quickly (12.18.34); the king's distress is mild.
- **Characters:** courtly figures; N1 records no individual character beyond the three names.
- **Emotional experience:** light, hyperbolic, perhaps comic or whimsical; some pathos in the king's sleepless night (12.18.33).
- **Distinctive device or turn:** the answer rests on injury without contact, a small clever inversion.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Modestly. It is a ranking puzzle with a neat reason. [Inference]
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Only lightly; the reflection is about the king's remark that refinement has become a fault.
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** Sustaining a very short, low-stakes tale; the Jaina prelude is long and arguably separate.

## 6. Contribution to the collection

- **Offers:** a light, courtly, near-comic episode, and in Ś/Hindi a Jaina-coloured prelude (N1 TC-011). Of the TC-008/TC-011 pair, this is the shorter and the more static as a story ([COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md](../COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md) group 6).
- **Nearest overlaps:** **TC-008**. N1 §4.6 says the two are "close in form and tone": the same "who was most refined?" question, a triple test structure, and answers resting on how the effect was produced. Whether that is the same dilemma under D-035 is the owner's call. A shared motif of refined sensitivity is not by itself duplication; here the form and the dilemma are both close.
- **Variety:** risks repeating the lightness and ranking form of TC-008.

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- Wives are described through their bodily fragility; there is no gendered judgement in the record [Primary]. Courtly eroticism (moonlit terrace, sleeping queen) is mild.
- Ś/Hindi prelude contains verses against wine and meat and a Jaina conversion with a banished minister [Primary (OCR) / Translation]; religious framing would need examination (N9).
