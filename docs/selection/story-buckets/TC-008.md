## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** The three fastidious men (food, women, bed) (a catalogue label).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 8 (12.15.1–52, Tawney ch. 82) · BKM 8 (provisional) · Ś 23 · J 5 · Hindi XXIII · Ryder 8. Match E. Position varies widely: 8 (KSS, BKM, Ryder), 23 (Ś, Hindi), 5 (J) (Cat. §2.1).
- **Essential events** (N1 TC-008, KSS 12.15.3–47):
  1. A wealthy brahman sacrificer, Viṣṇusvāmin, sends his three sons to the sea to fetch a turtle for a sacrifice. Each refuses to touch the slimy creature: the eldest is fastidious about food, the second about women, the youngest about beds. They abandon the errand and quarrel (3–18).
  2. They go to King Prasenajit to have their claims judged (15–18).
  3. **Food:** the eldest will not eat the fine rice, saying it smells of corpse-smoke; inquiry shows it was grown beside a cremation ground (19–26).
  4. **Women:** a courtesan sent to the second makes him nearly faint; he says she smells of goat. She reveals she was reared on goat's milk as an infant (27–37).
  5. **Bed:** the youngest is given a bed of seven mattresses and rises crying in pain after half a watch; a red curved mark is seen on his side and a single hair is found under all seven mattresses (37–45).
  6. The king rewards each and they stay on, forgetting the turtle, "having earned the sin" of obstructing their father's sacrifice (46–47).
- **Central characters and relationships:** three brahman brothers; their father Viṣṇusvāmin (whose sacrifice they neglect); King Prasenajit (judge and tester); the courtesan.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** three tests of extraordinary sensitivity, the last a hair under seven mattresses.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** of these three, fastidious about food, women and bed, who is the most fastidious? (KSS 12.15.49).
- **Vikrama answers:** the one fastidious about beds (12.15.50).
- **Reasoning:** the hair's imprint was seen with one's own eyes on his body, whereas the other two could have learned their facts in advance from someone else (12.15.51).
- **Across versions:** Ś 23 [Primary, OCR]: the bed-connoisseur, no reason in the answer. Hindi XXIII [Translation, Platts]: the hair is "in the seventh fold of the bedding"; the bed-connoisseur, no reason stated. J 5: not recorded in the repository evidence as to answer; the catalogue notes J 5 is one of three J riddle solutions Emeneau calls careless (report §4.D, as reported in Cat. TC-008) [Secondary, via catalogue]. Only KSS gives the evidential reason.
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** the surface question is who is the most delicate; the deeper question is what counts as proof of a sense, observed evidence or a claim that might be prepared. [Inference]

## 5. Storytelling potential [Inference throughout]

- **Hook:** three brothers who will not touch a turtle, so a sacrifice fails.
- **Story engine:** a triple test, each a small detective story ending in a hidden cause.
- **Stakes:** low in life-and-death terms, but the father's sacrifice is neglected; the real stakes are the brothers' pride.
- **Characters:** three rival eccentrics; the king as test-setter; the courtesan, whose origin is revealed.
- **Emotional experience:** comedy and irony (a man fainting at a perfumed courtesan's goat smell); the report's note of humour in this tale is supported by the events (N1; Cat. TC-008).
- **Distinctive device or turn:** the ending is evidential rather than moral; the father's sacrifice and the turtle are never resolved (N1).
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Moderately: the reasoning (direct proof against possibly prepared knowledge) is clever, but the stakes are low.
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Possibly, through the unfinished errand and the narrator's note about the neglected sacrifice (12.15.47), whose reading as irony is the compiler's (N1).
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** The answer's logic (prior knowledge) is explained only in KSS; a modern reader may see the courtesan's test as demeaning; the frame errand is never completed.
