# TC-012: King Yaśaḥketu, his Vidyādharī wife, and his faithful minister

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Viable core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md) (TC-012), [comparison report §7](../../research/SOURCE_TRADITIONS_COMPARISON.md).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** King Yaśaḥketu, his Vidyādharī wife, and his faithful minister (Tawney's title; a catalogue label).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 12 (12.19.1–171, Tawney ch. 86; 171 verses, the second-longest KSS tale by verse range) · BKM 12 (provisional) · Ś 11 (cross-checked against Emeneau's stated equivalence Bṛhatkathā 12 = Ś 11) · J — (absent from Emeneau's Bengali text; present in the Nepali manuscript W) · Hindi XI · Ryder 11. Absence from J's Bengali text is an absence from one text only, not from the tradition.
- **Essential events (KSS, N1 TC-012):**
  1. King Yaśaḥketu of Aṅga leaves the kingdom to his able minister Dīrghadarśin and gives himself to pleasure. People say the minister is "eating up" the kingdom. On his wife's advice the minister leaves on a pilgrimage so the rumour dies and the king must rule (12.19.4–26).
  2. Voyaging with a merchant, the minister sees a maiden on a jewelled couch in a wishing-tree rising from the sea; she sings of acts and their fruit, then sinks (27–50).
  3. Back home, the king hears of her, becomes lovesick, leaves the kingdom in the minister's hands and goes secretly as an ascetic, forbidding the minister to follow (51–70).
  4. A sage sends him to sea; the maiden rises and sings again; the king leaps after her (71–85).
  5. Under the sea he finds her, Mṛgāṅkavatī, a Vidyādhara king's daughter. She agrees to marry him on condition that on certain nights she may go without being asked (86–116).
  6. He follows her with a sword, sees a Rākṣasa swallow her, beheads it, and she emerges alive. She explains a curse by her father, now lifted (117–145).
  7. He brings her back to earth; the minister and citizens welcome them (146–161).
  8. That night the minister dies suddenly, his heart bursting (162).
- **Central characters and relationships:** the pleasure-loving king; the faithful minister and his wife Medhāvinī; the Vidyādharī and her father; two merchants; a sage; a Rākṣasa.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** a minister's sudden death at the moment of his master's triumph.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** at his master's success, why did the great minister's heart burst: grief that he had not won the divine woman himself, or because he wanted the kingdom and grieved at the king's return? (KSS 12.19.164–167). The penalty formula is expanded: Vikrama's merit as well as his head would be lost. [Primary]
- **Vikrama answers:** **neither** (12.19.168). [Primary]
- **Reasoning:** the king had already neglected the kingdom for ordinary women; what hope now that he was attached to a divine one? The minister's pains had only made the fault grow, and his heart burst (12.19.169–170). [Primary]
- **Across versions:** Ś 11 (question: why did the minister die during the great festival) and Hindi XI give the same answer: he feared the king would neglect the kingdom; the Hindi adds that no one would heed what the minister said (N1) [Primary (OCR) / Translation].
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** the surface question is a motive-reading puzzle with two discreditable options. The deeper conflict is the minister's loyalty to a kingdom against loyalty to a master who is happy at the kingdom's expense. N1 notes that the narrative gives the minister's unhappiness (12.19.69) and his reflection on fate (154–156), but only the answer states a motive. [Inference]

## 5. Storytelling potential

- **Hook:** a sea-borne maiden singing from a wishing-tree; a king abandons his throne for her. [Inference]
- **Story engine:** two journeys along the same path (minister, then king); a lovesick quest, a curse and a swallowing demon (N1).
- **Stakes:** the king's life and love, a kingdom left without a ruler; the final death is sudden.
- **Characters:** a stubbornly loyal minister is the quiet centre; the king is passive and pleasure-driven. The Vidyādharī is marvellous but described mostly by her situation.
- **Emotional experience:** wonder, romance, adventure, then abrupt grief.
- **Distinctive device or turn:** most of the tale is a romance of marvels; the riddle reframes it around a minister whose sorrow the text states in only two verses (69, 162) (N1).
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Yes, as a puzzle about hidden motive, but the clue lies outside the main plot. [Inference]
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Yes: the song on the fruit of acts and the minister's foreboding give the reader material to weigh.
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** Length (171 verses is mostly romance, not the riddle); the minister's motive is offstage; many episodes (sea voyages, curse, Rākṣasa) compete.

## 6. Contribution to the collection

- **Offers:** a marvel-romance frame with a political, loyalty-based riddle; an undersea city (shared with TC-007) and a curse-story told by its victim.
- **Nearest overlaps:** the reaction-riddle form (TC-014, TC-020, TC-023; a shared question device, with different content). TC-007 also has the king making the same journey a second time and an undersea city (N1 §4.5): a shared motif, not a shared dilemma. The master-and-servant relation (TC-004, 007, 017) is a similar relationship, with a different question.
- **Variety:** adds the minister as protagonist and a death of heartbreak.
- **Comparison buckets:** reaction riddles ([COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md](https://github.com/HHrisHikesHH/Betall-Pacchisi/blob/58d00606394f38801e94e4c258c431b02457f7aa/docs/selection/COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md) group 3); the undersea journey made twice, with TC-007 (group 13).

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- Little gendered or moralising material is recorded. The minister's wife Medhāvinī advises him; the Vidyādharī's condition (go without being asked) and the Rākṣasa swallowing are source-backed events (N1).
- A maiden "abandoned" by her father for a reason she does not know, and a curse imposed for lingering at worship, are narrative facts, not evaluated. [Primary]
