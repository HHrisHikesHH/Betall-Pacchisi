# TC-022: The four brothers who revived a lion

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Strong core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md), [comparison report](../../research/SOURCE_TRADITIONS_COMPARISON.md).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** The four brothers who revived a lion (Tawney's title as a catalogue label, not a project title). In J and the Hindi the animal is a tiger.
- **Recorded in:** KSS 22 (12.29.1–51, Tawney ch. 96) · BKM 22 (provisional) · Ś 21 · J 19 · Hindi XXI · Ryder 20.
- **Essential events** (KSS base, N1 TC-022):
  1. In Brahmasthala, in King Dharaṇīvarāha's realm, the brahman Viṣṇusvāmin dies and his wife follows him; kinsmen seize the property of their four sons (3–7).
  2. The brothers beg their way to their mother's father's village; their maternal uncles shelter them but come to treat them with contempt (8–11).
  3. The eldest tells how he tried to hang himself in a cremation ground but the rope broke and a stranger revived him and told him that suicide leads to hell. The others argue that wealth is fickle and that they should acquire a skill that will win it (12–28).
  4. They part in four directions. Later each shows his skill: one can grow flesh on a fragment of bone, the second skin and hair, the third the limbs, the fourth can give the finished creature life (29–35).
  5. In a forest they find a lion's bone without knowing what animal it is. Each applies his skill in turn. The lion rises, kills all four and goes off satisfied (36–41). The narrator comments that skill gained with effort brings ruin when fate is adverse (42–44).
- **Central characters and relationships:** four orphaned brahman brothers; their contemptuous maternal uncles; a stranger who saves the eldest; the lion.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** four skills cooperate to revive an animal that kills its makers.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** Which of the four was guilty in making the lion that killed them? (KSS 12.29.45–46, *aparādhyati*).
- **Vikrama answers:** The one who gave it life (48), after an aside in which he thinks to himself that the Vetāla wants to leave and he can fetch him back (47).
- **Reasoning:** The others, who made flesh, skin, hair and limbs without knowing the animal, acted in ignorance and are not at fault; but he who gave it life, seeing it had a lion's form, did so out of eagerness to show off his science and so committed the brahman-murders (49–50). [Primary: KSS e-text, AI reading]
- **Across versions:** Ś 21 and Hindi XXI ask "who is the **fool**?" (*mūrkha* in Ś; "the greatest fool" in Platts) rather than "who is guilty?"; the answer (the reviver) and the maxim (intelligence is better than learning) are the same [Primary, Uhle via OCR; Translation, Platts, N1]. The animal is a lion in KSS and Ś, a tiger in J and the Hindi (Cat. TC-022; the J basis is not documented beyond the catalogue and report). In the Hindi the brothers obtain the bones from a Kanjar (Platts, N1).
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** On the surface, who is to blame in a joint act that backfires. Underneath, whether knowledge changes responsibility, and whether acting from vanity is a different kind of fault from acting in ignorance; the shift from "guilty" to "fool" asks whether the issue is morality or wisdom. [Inference]

## 5. Storytelling potential [Inference throughout]

- **Hook:** Four young men bring a lion to life from a bone.
- **Story engine:** Poverty and dispossession drive the search for skill; the skills combine in a demonstration that spirals into disaster.
- **Stakes:** Their own lives, set against a vivid, escalating build-up.
- **Characters:** Four brothers distinguished by their skill, with the eldest as a survivor of suicide; contemptuous uncles. Roles are clear but lightly sketched.
- **Emotional experience:** Dread, dark irony, a sense of hubris; a quick, violent end. N1 calls it a grim cautionary tale.
- **Distinctive device:** Sequential four-step creation followed by an immediate reversal; and the unexpected release of Vikrama's aside (47).
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Yes, and compact: everyone helped, but one knew.
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Yes, through the sequence itself: the reader watches each brother not ask what the animal is.
- **Retelling difficulty:** The opening (half the tale) is long and misfortune-heavy before the skill episode. The narrator's moral on fate may duplicate the dilemma. The choice between "guilty" and "fool" must be made. The eldest's suicide attempt needs care.

## 6. Contribution to the collection

- **Offers:** A short, sharp tale with a clear causal chain and a blame question that rests on knowledge, with a memorable image.
- **Nearest overlaps:**
  - **TC-002** (three brahmans revive a dead girl): a shared motif, skills that revive the dead (N1 §4.6 item 5). Different dilemma (who is the girl's husband or brother) and different outcome. In TC-022 the revival kills.

- **Variety:** Adds a cautionary, supernatural-science tale with a dark comic arc; risks adding to the blame-form cluster (four tales with TC-027).

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)

**Provisional label:** **Strong core contender**

- **Strongest reason to include:** Compact, vivid and clearly distinct; the dilemma concerns knowledge and responsibility, with a clear answer that is the same in all read versions.
- **Strongest reason to hesitate:** Half the tale is misfortune setup before the central image, and the blame form recurs in three other candidates (TC-001, 013, 027).
- **What could change this assessment:** The owner's view of the blame-form cluster; reading J 19 and the Ś and Hindi narrative bodies; a decision between the "guilty" and "fool" framings.
- **Confidence:** Moderate to high for the KSS tale; moderate overall because J is unread and the Ś and Hindi readings are passage-level.

*This label is an analyst's recommendation for discussion, not a selection decision. The owner decides (D-022, Q-411).*
