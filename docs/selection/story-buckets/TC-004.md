# TC-004: Vīravara, who sacrificed his family for King Śūdraka

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Strong core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md) (N1 TC-004).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** Vīravara, who sacrificed his family for King Śūdraka (a catalogue label).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 4 (12.11.1–132, Tawney ch. 78) · BKM 4 (provisional) · Ś 4 · J 4 · Hindi III (swaps order with TC-003; the king is "Rupsen") · Ryder 4. Match E.
- **Essential events** (N1 TC-004, KSS 12.11.5–120):
  1. A brahman, Vīravara, serves King Śūdraka of Śobhāvatī with his wife, son and daughter for 500 dīnāras a day, and spends it on his family, worship and gifts, standing guard at the lion-gate day and night (5–31).
  2. A woman is heard weeping outside the city. Vīravara goes to find out; the king secretly follows. The weeper is the Earth, mourning because the righteous king will die on the third day unless Vīravara offers his son to the goddess Caṇḍikā (32–54).
  3. Vīravara's wife and the boy agree at once. At the shrine the father beheads the boy; the daughter dies of a broken heart; the mother enters the fire; Vīravara beheads himself (55–93).
  4. The hidden king, who has watched everything, resolves to repay the deed and offers his own head. A heavenly voice stops him and the family revives unharmed (94–103).
  5. Vīravara, asked by the king, says only that the woman vanished. Next morning the king tells his ministers and gives Vīravara and his son a kingdom (104–120).
- **Central characters and relationships:** King Śūdraka (master); Vīravara (brahman servant and guard); his wife Dharmavatī, son Sattvavara, daughter Vīravatī; the Earth (personified); the goddess Caṇḍikā (via a heavenly voice).
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** a servant who sacrifices his family on a goddess's demand for his king, and a king who then offers his own life.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** among all these, who was the greatest hero? (KSS 12.11.122).
- **Vikrama answers:** King Śūdraka (12.11.123). Betal challenges: is Vīravara not greater, or his wife who consented to her son's sacrifice, or the boy, so young and courageous? (124–127).
- **Reasoning:** Vīravara's vow is to protect his master with his life, family included; the wife followed her husband; the son is like his parents, "as the threads, so the cloth"; but kings protect themselves with their servants' lives, so the king who was ready to give his body for a servant excels (12.11.128–131).
- **Across versions:** Ś 4 [Primary, OCR]: same answer and reasoning (servants give lives for masters, not masters for servants); the king is of Vardhamāna and the servant asks a thousand gold pieces; the OCR names the answering king Vikramāditya here and Vikramasena elsewhere. Hindi III [Translation, Platts]: five persons compared; same answer, since a servant's duty is to die for his master while the king gave up his throne for his servant. J: not recorded in the repository evidence.
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** the surface question names the most heroic; the deeper conflict is whether greatness lies in an act's cost or in how far it exceeds what the role demands. [Inference]

## 5. Storytelling potential [Inference throughout]

- **Hook:** a faithful guard, and a king who secretly follows him into the night.
- **Story engine:** a chain of escalating sacrifices, each answered by the next.
- **Stakes:** life and death, family and kingdom; the intensity is high.
- **Characters:** the family members each have a distinct act (the boy's calm consent, the daughter's grief, the wife's regret at not offering herself first); the king's unseen presence gives dramatic irony.
- **Emotional experience:** pathos and awe, then relief at a divine reversal, and Vīravara's silence after.
- **Distinctive device or turn:** the king's secret witnessing runs through the plot (N1 narrative features) and turns the final act into a reply.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Yes, because Betal's challenge makes Vikrama defend an answer many readers would resist.
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Yes, through the reader's own resistance to the verdict.
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** A father beheads his son, a daughter dies, a wife self-immolates; the escalation can read as repetitive; the divine reversal can seem to cancel the stakes.

## 6. Contribution to the collection

- Offers the strongest self-sacrifice and loyalty narrative in the batch and a famous reversal.
- **Nearest overlaps:** TC-017 (king and subordinate): N1 §4.6 records the same argument (a servant's duty against a king's exceptional act), so this is a similar dilemma with a different plot; TC-007 asks a king-and-servant question on a different basis (knowledge, not role). TC-020 (child sacrifice for a king): N1 §4.6 records both sacrifice a brahman boy for a king with the parents' consent; whether that is a repeated plot or a shared device is for the owner's judgement under D-035. - **Variety:** adds the heroic-extremity register; risks repeating the sacrifice register if TC-020 and TC-016 are also chosen.
- **Comparison buckets:** TC-004/TC-017 (king, subordinate, loyalty, duty); TC-004/TC-020 (sacrifice of a child or family for a king). See [COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md](../COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md) groups 2 and 4; both are judged related stories with distinct central dilemmas.

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- Child sacrifice with parental consent, a daughter's death of grief, a widow's self-immolation (KSS 12.11.55–93). Source-backed, with a divine reversal (94–103). Handling matter.
- Caste and role: Vīravara is a brahman serving as guard; the answer rests on duty by role (128–131). The rule that a wife's duty is to follow her husband (130) is stated in Vikrama's reasoning. Source-backed; N9 examination pending. Not a reason for exclusion (D-006).
- Supernatural demand (the goddess) that a king's life be bought with a child's: no moral verdict made here.

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)

**Provisional label:** **Strong core contender**

- **Strongest reason to include:** a powerful, well-documented story with the same answer in KSS, Ś and Hindi, and an engaging Betal challenge.
- **Strongest reason to hesitate:** it sits in two comparison groups. TC-017 is resolved by the same argument (N1 §4.6), so choosing both repeats Vikrama's reasoning. TC-020 shares the image of a brahman boy given up by his parents for a king. Neither is a repeated plot, but both bear on variety, and the material is among the most violent in the pool.
- **What could change this assessment:** the owner's view on whether the collection can carry the same king-against-servant argument twice, or two child-sacrifice tales; an editorial view on handling the chain of deaths.
- **Confidence:** moderate for the story; moderate for the overlap judgement (the KSS texts of TC-017 and TC-020 are read in full; J and the Ś and Hindi narrative bodies are not).
