# TC-015: The magic pill (Manaḥsvāmin as a woman)

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Strong core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md) (TC-015), [N3 §3.1](../../research/JAMBHALADATTA_ADDITIONAL_TALES_EVIDENCE.md), [comparison report §7](../../research/SOURCE_TRADITIONS_COMPARISON.md).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** The magic pill (Manaḥsvāmin as a woman) (Tawney's title; a catalogue label).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 15 (12.22.1–115, Tawney ch. 89) · BKM 15 (provisional) · Ś 14 · J 13 (Emeneau Tr pp. 79–87, read by N3) · Hindi XIV · Ryder 14.
- **Essential events (KSS, N1 TC-015):**
  1. In Śivapura, Princess Śaśiprabhā and the young brahman Manaḥsvāmin fall in love at a spring festival. A mad elephant breaks loose and he carries her to safety; she does not know his name (12.22.3–19).
  2. He goes to his teacher, the master-rogue Mūladeva, who has a friend Śaśin. Mūladeva takes a pill and becomes an old brahman; he gives Manaḥsvāmin a pill that turns him into a girl (20–26).
  3. The "old brahman" asks the king to guard his "son's betrothed"; the king, fearing a brahman's curse, places the "girl" with the princess (27–33).
  4. The princess confides her lovesickness; Manaḥsvāmin reveals himself and they marry by the *gāndharva* rite (informal marriage by mutual consent); he lives as a girl by day, a man by night (34–56).
  5. At a cousin's wedding the minister's son falls lovesick for the "girl"; the king gives her to him. She imposes a six-month delay, he goes on pilgrimage, and Manaḥsvāmin seduces his bride Mṛgāṅkavatī and flees with her (57–99).
  6. Mūladeva returns as the old brahman with Śaśin disguised as the "son" and demands his daughter-in-law; the king, afraid of a curse, gives Śaśiprabhā to Śaśin with proper rites (100–105).
  7. Manaḥsvāmin and Śaśin dispute: first marriage, by the teacher's favour, or the father's gift before the sacred fire (106–110).
- **Central characters and relationships:** the princess; Manaḥsvāmin; Mūladeva and Śaśin; the king; the minister's son and his bride.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** a lover disguised as a woman and a "whose wife is she?" dispute between a secret husband and a public one.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** whose wife is she properly (*kasyopapadyate bhāryā sā*, KSS 12.22.111)? [Primary]
- **Vikrama answers:** **Śaśin's**, because her father gave her to him openly by the proper rite; Manaḥsvāmin took her by stealth through the *gāndharva* form, and a thief has no right to another's property (12.22.113–114). [Primary]
- **Reasoning:** the public, paternal gift outranks the secret union. [Primary]
- **Across versions:**
  - Ś 14 and Hindi XIV add that she is pregnant by the lover and that the child will perform Śaśin's funeral rites; the answer is again Śaśin. Ś gives deceit (*chadmanā praviṣṭaḥ*) and public marriage; the Hindi says no one knew of the pregnancy and Śaśin married her before ten arbitrators (N1) [Primary (OCR) / Translation].
  - J 13: same answer, Śaśideva, because her father gave her to him; what Manaḥsvāmin did is forbidden in this world and the next (Emeneau Tr p. 87; N3 §3.1) [Primary: Emeneau's translation].
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** surface: which marriage is valid. Deeper: love and consent against law and public ritual, in a case where the princess's own wishes are not consulted (N1) and where every "marriage" has been engineered by tricksters. [Inference]

## 4. Common story spine and variant differences

**Common spine:** a princess loves a brahman who changes into a woman with Mūladeva's help; she is given to Mūladeva's friend by her father; the rivals dispute; the lawful husband wins. Established in KSS and J 13 at plot level; in Ś and Hindi at the level of the dispute and answer only.

## 5. Storytelling potential

- **Hook:** a lover turns himself into a girl to live with the princess. [Inference]
- **Story engine:** a master-rogue's disguises (an old brahman twice) and a chain of deceptions (N1).
- **Stakes:** the lovers' union against the king's deception; a minister's son's collapse.
- **Characters:** Mūladeva, the rogue puppet-master, is the standout; Manaḥsvāmin more instrumental.
- **Emotional experience:** comic intrigue and trickster humour (N1; report §9), with the princess's shame and wonder at the revelation (12.22.54).
- **Distinctive device or turn:** sex-change by pill or charm, "a girl by day, a man by night"; an embedded tale (Ilā and Budha) used as seduction.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Yes: a legal-moral puzzle where the "thief" is the lover the reader likes. [Inference]
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Yes, since the reader can hold both claims. 
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** The seduction of the second wife Mṛgāṅkavatī and her unresolved fate (N1); consent and deception are central; the princess's voice is absent from the dispute.

## 6. Contribution to the collection

- **Offers:** a rare comic trickster tale with magical disguise and a legal-moral riddle; N1 records humour here and in a few others.
- **Nearest overlaps:** **TC-026 (J 22)**: N3 §3.1 compares them in detail. Shared: the rogue pair Mūladeva and Śaśideva, a woman removed from guardians, a disguised claim before a king. Different: a princess's secret love against a married woman's adultery; a legal question (whose wife) against a cleverness comparison; a second trickster. N3's observation is that J 22 is not a repetition of J 13 at the level of events or dilemma; whether the dilemma is genuinely different is the owner's judgement. TC-001 and TC-026 share schemer devices (N3 §6.2). TC-002 and TC-006 also ask "whose wife?", and TC-005 and TC-009 "to whom should she be given?" (allocation forms), on different bases.
- **Variety:** the allocation form of the riddle appears also in TC-002, 005, 006, 009, 019 (N1 §4.1).
- **Comparison bucket:** Mūladeva and tricksters, with TC-026 ([COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md](../COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md) group 7).

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- **Consent and deception:** the king is deceived through a false brahman's claim; the minister's son's bride is seduced by the disguised lover (12.22.83–99) [Primary]; the princess is not asked in the final dispute (N1).
- **Gendered/legal framing:** the answer calls the lover a thief of another's property (12.22.114); the pregnancy variant (Ś, Hindi) adds that the child will perform Śaśin's funeral rites [Primary (OCR) / Translation]. Not a reason for exclusion (D-006); needs later examination (N9).
- A *brahman's curse* is the king's chief fear (12.22.30, 102) [Primary].

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)

**Provisional label:** **Strong core contender**

- **Strongest reason to include:** the best-evidenced comic trickster tale in the batch, with a distinct legal-moral dilemma, evidence in four traditions, and J 13 read in full.
- **Strongest reason to hesitate:** the intrigue is long; consent issues in the seduction; overlap with TC-026 as a Mūladeva tale.
- **What could change this assessment:** the owner's view on TC-026's distinctness; a specialist reading of Ś 14 and Hindi XIV.
- **Confidence:** moderate to high for the KSS and J plot; moderate overall since Ś and Hindi are Q&A only.
