# TC-026: Mūladeva wins a bride for Śaśideva

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Viable core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N3 §3, §3.1 and §5.3](../../research/JAMBHALADATTA_ADDITIONAL_TALES_EVIDENCE.md).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** Mūladeva wins a bride for Śaśideva (Emeneau's title, "How Mūladeva obtained a Bride for Śaśideva"; a catalogue label, not a project title).
- **Recorded in:** KSS — · BKM — · Ś — · J **22** (Emeneau T pp. 124–132, Tr pp. 125–133; **P and V only**, H and O lack it) · Hindi — · Ryder —. Also Newari 22 and W. The dashes mean the examined texts lack it, not that the tradition does.
- **Essential events** (J base, N3 §3):
  1. In Kuṇḍinapura the merchant Śaṅkaradatta, a friend of the pious King Śvetaketu, leaves his wife Anaṅgasenā under guard and goes abroad. Twelve years pass and she wastes away (Tr p. 125).
  2. At a festival the rogue Śaśideva sees her and falls in love. His friend Mūladeva proposes a bawd, Kāmikalpalatā, who can "disturb even Arundhatī" (a model of wifely fidelity) (Tr p. 127).
  3. The bawd goes to Kuṇḍinapura and, by worship and a show of power, becomes known as an all-knowing ascetic. The wife's mother consults her about the absent husband; the bawd falsely says he has married abroad. The parents beg for a remedy and she prescribes a "charm": in a hidden bower the daughter must whisper it while looking at a man brought to her and make love with him daily, until he takes on her husband's appearance and the husband returns. The father builds the bower (Tr pp. 127–129).
  4. The man is Śaśideva. The two become lovers and she forgets her husband (Tr p. 129).
  5. Mūladeva decides they must leave with her. In Śaśideva's guise, he seizes her at the king's gate as his runaway wife; the king threatens to kill him; he says he mistook her and promises to produce his real wife. A woman's corpse in her ornaments is laid in her house and the house is burned, so that no one will say she ran off with another man. The lovers take her away (Tr pp. 129–133). The parents grieve and the king sends "her" bones to pilgrimage places.
  6. Later Mūladeva presents Anaṅgasenā as his own wife, who "looks like" her. The king exclaims she is Anaṅgasenā in person and lets them go; her parents give "Anaṅgasenā's" jewels to her and weep (Tr p. 133). Whether they knew her is not stated.
- **Central characters and relationships:** the wife Anaṅgasenā; the lover Śaśideva; the strategist Mūladeva; the bawd Kāmikalpalatā; her parents; the absent husband (never returns in the narrative); the deceived king.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** the rogue pair, a bawd posing as an ascetic with a false "charm", and a faked death.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** Who showed the greater intelligence (*mahatī buddhiḥ*), Kāmikalpalatā or Mūladeva? (J 22, T p. 132; Tr p. 133).
- **Vikrama answers:** Mūladeva, the great rogue, because his wit was ready on every occasion (*sarvatrotpannabuddhitvāt*) (T p. 132; Tr p. 133). [Primary: J Sanskrit in Emeneau; one manuscript, P, plus V]
- **Reasoning:** Only the phrase above; no further argument.
- **Across versions:** **Newari 22 reverses the answer**: the king answers **the bawd**, because she was the occasion of all the cleverness (Jørgensen p. 236, page image) [Translation, German; Newari text not read]. The Newari also redistributes the actions (Śaśideva plans the escape and fakes the death; Mūladeva is Śaśideva's elder brother and only impersonates a brahman). W: names only.
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** On the surface, which of two tricksters was cleverer. Underneath, whether cleverness lies in starting a scheme or in finishing it. The two versions' answers (strategist, go-between) show that reasonable readers disagree. [Inference]

## 5. Storytelling potential [Inference throughout]

- **Hook:** A false ascetic and a love "charm" designed to seduce a guarded wife.
- **Story engine:** A chain of deceptions, each making the next possible: the false oracle, the charm, the staged mistaken identity, the substituted corpse and fire, the lookalike wife.
- **Stakes:** Exposure and punishment (a king's threat to kill), but the tone is comic.
- **Characters:** Two tricksters and a lonely wife; the deceived are the virtuous parties (parents, king, absent husband). N3 sees an edge of pathos in the parents' final scene.
- **Emotional experience:** Comic intrigue with a dark undertow; delight in cleverness, discomfort at those deceived.
- **Distinctive device:** A corpse substitution and fire to erase a woman; a mistaken-identity claim planted early so it can pay off.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Moderately: a comparison of two helpers' wit is light, and the answer given in J has almost no argument, but the two traditions' opposite answers make the question live.
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Yes, by letting the reader weigh the go-between's reach against the strategist's improvisation.
- **Retelling difficulty:** The text is partly corrupt (T p. 130); the king's oath and Mūladeva's excuse are uncertain (N3 §3); the husband's fate is untold; the faked death and the wife's willingness need delicate handling; the question compares helpers, not lovers.

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- **Adultery and deception of a wife's family:** the parents are tricked into arranging the affair; her agency after the "charm" is ambiguous in the text (J 22, Tr p. 129). Source-backed; needs later examination (N9).
- **Religious fraud:** the false ascetic (J); in the Newari, the bawd preaches the Prajñāpāramitā and claims mantras succeed in proportion to intercourse (N3 §6.4, §5.3) [Translation]. Source-backed; needs later examination for religious sensitivity.
- **Faked death and a corpse** burned in a house (Tr p. 133). Source-backed.
- **Gender:** the wife is guarded, then "won"; the bawd's power to disturb "even Arundhatī" (Tr p. 127). Presented without judgement in J (N3 §6.4).
- None is a reason for exclusion by itself (D-006).

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)

**Provisional label:** **Viable core contender**

- **Strongest reason to include:** A lively comic intrigue with a distinctive question about cleverness, plus a recorded second answer that gives the owner a real variant to work with (D-034).
- **Strongest reason to hesitate:** Its cast and devices are close to TC-015, its dilemma is lighter than most, and its Bengali text rests on one manuscript and a poor print with a corrupt passage.
- **What could change this assessment:** The owner's judgement of TC-015 / TC-026 under D-035; a Sanskritist's check of T pp. 130 and 132; a reading of W's narrative.
- **Confidence:** Low to moderate; the tale was read in full, but on thin witnesses and by an AI reader.
