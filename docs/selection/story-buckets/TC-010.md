# TC-010: Madanasenā and her rash promise (the "promise tale")

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Strong core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md) (TC-010), [comparison report §8.1](../../research/SOURCE_TRADITIONS_COMPARISON.md).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** Madanasenā and her rash promise (a catalogue label, not a project title).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 10 (12.17.1–68, Tawney ch. 84) · BKM 10 (provisional; one of only two BKM tales checked on a page image, names match KSS 10) · Ś 9 · J 10 (Emeneau pp. 66–73) · Hindi IX · Ryder — (Ryder omits it; why is unverified, so the absence says nothing about the tale's standing in the wider tradition).
- **Essential events (KSS, N1 TC-010):**
  1. A merchant's daughter, Madanasenā, is betrothed to the merchant Samudradatta. Her brother's friend Dharmadatta falls desperately in love with her and pleads with her (12.17.6–20).
  2. Fearing he may use force, she promises to come to him after her wedding, before going to her husband, and swears it. She first asks him to let the wedding happen so that her father gains the merit of giving a maiden; he refuses a woman who has first belonged to another, so she promises to come to him, already married, before going to her husband, and binds it with an oath (12.17.21–26).
  3. On the wedding night she turns from her husband and weeps. He says she may go to whoever she loves. She tells him of her vow, which she says she cannot break; he is stunned, then releases her (12.17.27–41).
  4. A thief stops her on the night road. She offers her jewels; he wants her to stay; she tells her story and asks to keep her word and return. He lets her go and waits (12.17.42–52).
  5. Dharmadatta hears that her husband freed her, says he is pleased with her truthfulness but wants nothing to do with another man's wife, and sends her back (12.17.53–54).
  6. The thief lets her pass again, with her jewels; she reaches home unharmed. Her husband sees no change in her and lives happily with her (12.17.55–61).
- **Central characters and relationships:** Madanasenā (promise-keeper); Samudradatta (husband); Dharmadatta (would-be lover); the thief (stranger). Her father and brother are setting only.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** a bride's wedding-night promise to another man, and three men in a row who release her. Question: who was the *tyāgin*, the one who gives up or is generous?

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** which of the thief and the two merchants was truly generous (*kas tyāgī*)? (KSS 12.17.62–63) [Primary]
- **Vikrama answers:** **the thief** (12.17.64). [Primary]
- **Reasoning:** the husband, of good family, could hardly keep a wife known to be attached to another; the lover's ardour had been worn by time and he feared the husband would report him to the king; the thief, an evil-doer with no such constraints, gave up a jewelled woman (12.17.65–67). [Primary]
- **Across versions:** this is the tale in which question, answer and ending change most.
  - Ś 9: the question is who was *sattvādhika* (greatest in strength of character); the answer is the thief, with the lover's fear of the king's punishment as a reason [Primary, OCR; N1 TC-010].
  - Hindi IX: same answer (the thief), after the lover's speech on women [Translation: Platts].
  - J 10: the question names four figures (Kandarpa, the thief, a brahman-ogre, Dharmadatta) and the answer is **the husband, Kandarpa**, who released her for the lover's sake and then rejected her; the others' nobility is called "conditioned" (Emeneau p. 73) [Primary: Emeneau's text and translation].
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** on the surface, which release is most generous. Underneath, whether a gift of freedom counts when the giver has little else to do, and whether generosity is measured by cost, by absence of pressure, or by what the giver then does with it. In KSS the thief's disinterested restraint is the answer; in J 10 the same set of events is re-weighed around the husband's sexual honour. [Inference]

## 4. Common story spine and variant differences

**Common spine (as far as the read versions show):**
- A betrothed bride has sworn to another man to come to him first; she tells her husband on the wedding night and he lets her go; a thief meets her on the road and lets her pass; the lover declines to keep her; the Vetāla asks which was noblest.
- Established passage-level in KSS, J 10 and Hindi IX (report §8.1); in Ś 9 the question, answer and the lover's and husband's actions were read, but most of the body was not.

**Variants:**

| Story element | KSS | Ś 9 | Hindi IX | J 10 | Why the difference matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Added figures | none | none | none | a brahman-ogre also stops her and lets her go | adds a fourth candidate and a second night-road encounter |
| Lover's response | refuses; pleased with her truthfulness, wants no other man's wife | refuses with verses on women's untrustworthiness | as Ś, with the speech | bows to her and sends her back | Ś and Hindi add a gendered speech (see §7) |
| Husband's reception | sees her unchanged, honours her, lives happily (12.17.59–61) | embraces her affectionately | shows her no affection (translation; AI reading of Hindi) | rejects her as defiled | decides how the ending feels: reunion, chill, or rejection |
| Answer | the thief | the thief | the thief | the husband | the moral centre moves (report: significance High for J, Moderate to high for Hindi) |
| Reasons | each man's release judged by his constraints | husband knew her mind was elsewhere; lover feared the king | husband gave her up; lover feared the king | husband acted despite desire; others' nobility "conditioned" | the same events support opposite answers |

## 5. Storytelling potential

- **Hook:** a bride promises herself to a stranger before her own wedding; the promise is kept under pressure. [Inference]
- **Story engine:** a chain of three releases on a single night, each testing a man's restraint; the return journey repeats the outward one (thief, lover, thief again) (N1 notes this structure).
- **Stakes:** immediate and clear: her safety on a night road, her honour, her marriage. Textual fear of force (12.17.22) gives the promise its weight.
- **Characters:** a woman who acts on a principle of truthfulness that the narrator stresses throughout (N1 cites 26, 38, 50–51, 54, 57); a thief who is more trusting than the respectable men.
- **Emotional experience:** suspense on the night road, tenderness and shock in the bridal chamber, irony as the outlaw proves the most restrained. Warm resolution in KSS and Ś; colder in the Hindi and J.
- **Distinctive device or turn:** the "three releases" structure, and an answer that overturns who looks most honourable. In J 10 the turn changes direction entirely.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Yes: it is arguable, and the versions themselves argue it differently. [Inference]
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Probably: the three men can be left to speak by their actions, and the reader asked to weigh them. The answer's reasoning is explicit in all versions and need not be restated.
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** The variant choice (the ending's emotional register depends on it); the husband's reaction needs a decision that is the owner's, not the analyst's; Ś and Hindi lover's verses on women; the wedding-night and extracted-oath setup needs careful handling.

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- **Gendered material.** Ś 9 and Hindi IX add the lover's verses on women's untrustworthiness [Primary (OCR) / Translation]. J 10's husband rejects the returning wife as defiled (Emeneau p. 71). KSS's husband inspects her face for signs of intercourse (12.17.59–61), a detail the record gives as the basis of his trust [Primary]. These are recorded, not reasons for exclusion (D-006); how they would be framed is a later editorial matter (N9).
- **Coercion in the setup.** The oath is extracted from fear of force (12.17.22). [Primary]
- **Sensitive framing of the husband.** The Hindi reading of his coldness needs a specialist (report §15). [Needs later examination]

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)

**Provisional label:** **Strong core contender**

- **Strongest reason to include:** a clear, distinct dilemma with strong tension and a good evidence base across four traditions; the story engine is compact and visual.
- **Strongest reason to hesitate:** the answer and tone depend on which variant is used, and the gendered ending (the husband's response) needs a considered choice.
- **What could change this assessment:** a specialist reading of Hindi IX, or an owner decision that the J ending changes the story too far.
- **Confidence:** moderate. Strong KSS and J evidence; Ś and Hindi are thinner and BKM is only a name check.

*This label is an analyst's recommendation for discussion, not a selection decision. The owner decides (D-022, Q-411).*
