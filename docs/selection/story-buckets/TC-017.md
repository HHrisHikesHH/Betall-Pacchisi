# TC-017: The beautiful Unmādinī

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Viable core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md) (TC-017 and §4.6), [comparison report §7](../../research/SOURCE_TRADITIONS_COMPARISON.md).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** The beautiful Unmādinī (Tawney's title; a catalogue label).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 17 (12.24.1–61, Tawney ch. 91; maxim at 12.24.61) · BKM 17 (provisional; this story was found at about the 17th heading on one BKM page image checked by the reviewer) · Ś 16 · J 14 · Hindi XVI · Ryder 16. The Nepali J manuscript (W) omits it; that is the absence of one manuscript, not of the tale in the tradition.
- **Essential events (KSS, N1 TC-017):**
  1. In Kanakapura, the virtuous King Yaśodhana is "impotent towards others' wives". A merchant's daughter, Unmādinī, maddens all who see her; her father offers her to the king first (12.24.7–12).
  2. Brahmans sent to examine her marks are overwhelmed and falsely report bad marks, fearing the king's infatuation would ruin the kingdom; the king declines. She is married to the general Baladhara and resents the slight (13–19).
  3. At the spring festival she shows herself from her roof; the king is smitten, faints, and banishes the brahmans (20–28).
  4. He wastes away but refuses his confidants' pleas to take her (29–34).
  5. The general offers her: she is his servant's wife, or he will abandon her at a temple so there is no fault. The king refuses angrily, preferring death (35–43).
  6. The king dies of lovesickness; the general, unable to bear his master's death, enters the fire (44–45).
- **Central characters and relationships:** King Yaśodhana; Unmādinī; the general Baladhara; the deceiving brahmans; her father.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** a king who refuses a woman he desires and dies, and a general who offers her and follows him in death.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** which of the two, the king or the general, was greater in courage (*sattvenābhyadhikaḥ*, KSS 12.24.47)? [Primary]
- **Vikrama answers:** **the king** (12.24.48). The Vetāla objects that the general, who gave up his own wife and burned himself, seems greater (49–51). Vikrama laughs and replies: servants' vow is to protect the master even with their lives, so it is no wonder; kings, swollen with pride, break dharma; this king, sole ruler, was not deluded by a fickle woman, and died without setting foot on the wrong path (52–60). [Primary]
- **Reasoning:** each act is measured against what is expected of the actor's role: servant's duty against the exceptional restraint of a king. [Primary]
- **Across versions:** in Ś 16 and Hindi XVI Unmādinī also enters the fire, after asking a teacher about a wife's duty, and the question compares **three**. The answer is the king in both; a servant's duty is to die for his master and a wife's for her husband (N1) [Primary (OCR) / Translation]. J 14: not read.
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** surface: which of the two was greater in *sattva* (rendered in N1 as "courage"; KSS 12.24.47). "Self-command" is this dossier's interpretive gloss, since the king's act is a refusal. Deeper: whether a role's expected sacrifice counts less than an act that exceeds one's role, and where the woman at the centre fits in an argument about two men. In KSS her feelings appear only as resentment and display (N1). [Inference]

## 5. Storytelling potential

- **Hook:** a woman so beautiful she maddens all who see her. [Inference]
- **Story engine:** a deception (the brahmans' false report) starts a tragedy that the king refuses to remedy by wrongdoing (N1).
- **Stakes:** the king's life; a kingdom; the general's life.
- **Characters:** the king is restrained and consumed; the general is devoted; Unmādinī is largely seen from outside (resentment, display).
- **Emotional experience:** a slow tragedy of restraint; pathos; admiration.
- **Distinctive device or turn:** Vikrama must defend his answer against the Vetāla's challenge, which is the central argument of the tale; extended verses on the vices of kings (54–58).
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Moderately, since the answer depends on a role-based argument; N1 notes it repeats TC-004's argument shape. [Inference]
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** The reflection is made explicit by Vikrama's long answer, so a retelling would need to avoid that.
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** The woman's thin agency; the Ś/Hindi self-immolation ending; the repeated argument shape of TC-004.

## 6. Contribution to the collection

- **Offers:** a king's tragic restraint and a political-moral argument about role and exception.
- **Nearest overlaps:** **TC-004**. N1 §4.6 and N1's note say the two "use the same argument (a servant's duty against a king's exceptional act)". TC-007 asks the same sort of question with a different argument. This is a **similar dilemma form** and a similar relationship (king and subordinate); the plots differ (TC-004: a servant sacrifices his family; TC-017: a king refuses a woman). Whether that is a genuine duplication under D-035 is the owner's call.
- **Variety:** it risks repeating the king-versus-servant argument.
- **Comparison bucket:** king, subordinate, loyalty and duty ([COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md](https://github.com/HHrisHikesHH/Betall-Pacchisi/blob/58d00606394f38801e94e4c258c431b02457f7aa/docs/selection/COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md) group 2). The lead's reading there: related stories with distinct central dilemmas (devotion to death against restraint of desire), but the pool's clearest repetition of **reasoning**.

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- **Gendered:** a woman is a prize and a danger; her husband offers her to his king (12.24.35–37); in Ś/Hindi she follows her husband into the fire as a wife's duty (sati) [Primary (OCR) / Translation]. Recorded, not a reason for exclusion (D-006). Needs later examination (N9).
- **Mind-altering beauty:** the text presents a woman whose appearance maddens all; the brahmans' lie is motivated by fear for the kingdom.
- **Violence:** death by lovesickness and self-immolation [Primary].

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)

**Provisional label:** **Viable core contender**

- **Strongest reason to include:** a strong tragic situation (a king who dies rather than do wrong) with a Vetāla challenge that gives the answer drama.
- **Strongest reason to hesitate:** Vikrama's answer uses the same argument as TC-004's (N1 §4.6), so if both are chosen he makes the same case twice. The plot is not a repeat, which is why this is not labelled "contested". Unmādinī is mostly an object of the men's debate.
- **What could change this assessment:** the owner's view on whether the shared argument matters if both TC-004 and TC-017 are considered (and, if so, which to keep); how the Ś/Hindi sati ending is weighed; reading J 14.
- **Why TC-004 and TC-017 carry different labels** (both face the same group-2 overlap, which the lead judges not a serious one for either): the difference rests on evidence and handling, not on the overlap. TC-004's answer and reasoning are stable across KSS, Ś and the Hindi (N1 TC-004). TC-017's ending and question change in Ś and the Hindi (Unmādinī's sati; three compared, N1 TC-017), so the dilemma depends on the version chosen, and its central woman is largely an object of the men's debate. Neither tale is the fixed point against which the other is judged; if the owner wants only one, which one is open.
- **Confidence:** moderate. The KSS reading is complete; the overlap is a judgement, not an evidence gap.
