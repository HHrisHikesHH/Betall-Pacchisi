# TC-006: The woman who switched her husband's and brother's heads

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Strong core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md) (N1 TC-006).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** The woman who switched her husband's and brother's heads (a catalogue label; N1 names her Madanasundarī).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 6 (12.13.1–54, Tawney ch. 80) · BKM 6 (provisional) · Ś 6 · J 8 (J places it eighth) · Hindi VI · Ryder 6. Match E.
- **Essential events** (N1 TC-006, KSS 12.13.4–50):
  1. At a festival of the goddess Gaurī in Śobhāvatī, a young washerman, Dhavala, sees Madanasundarī, a washerman's daughter, bathing. He falls lovesick and they marry (4–17).
  2. Her brother invites them to a festival at her father's house. Passing the Gaurī temple, Dhavala goes in alone and, moved "by fate", cuts off his own head as an offering (18–31).
  3. The brother goes in to look for him and, distraught in the same way, beheads himself (32–33).
  4. Madanasundarī finds both dead, reproaches the goddess and prepares to hang herself. A voice from the sky stops her: she should join each head to its body and both will live (34–45).
  5. In joyful haste she joins her husband's head to her brother's body and her brother's head to her husband's body. Both rise alive with heads exchanged; after exchanging accounts they go on, and she, seeing what she has done, is bewildered (46–50).
- **Central characters and relationships:** Madanasundarī (wife and sister); Dhavala (husband); her brother (unnamed); the goddess (as a heavenly voice); the two fathers.
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** a woman who rejoins two dead men's heads to the wrong bodies and is left asking which man is which.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** of the two mixed men, who is her husband? (KSS 12.13.51).
- **Vikrama answers:** the one on whom her husband's head is set (12.13.53).
- **Reasoning:** the head is the chief of the limbs and personal identity (*pratyabhijñā*, "recognition") resides in it (12.13.53).
- **Across versions:** Ś 6 [Primary, OCR]: same answer, given by a verse ending "among all the limbs the head is chief". Hindi VI [Translation, Platts]: the same principle cited from "the book of law". J 8: not recorded in the repository evidence as to answer.
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** the surface question is which man is the husband; the deeper conflict is whether a person is the body, the face, or something else, and what a marriage attaches to. [Inference]

## 5. Storytelling potential [Inference throughout]

- **Hook:** a happy marriage, then two sudden self-beheadings at a temple.
- **Story engine:** shock, grief, miracle, then a mistake made in haste that produces the puzzle.
- **Stakes:** life and death turn into a problem of identity that cannot be undone.
- **Characters:** Madanasundarī is the actor: she grieves, argues with the goddess, tries to die and, in her joy, makes the error. The men act at once and then are passive.
- **Emotional experience:** horror, grief, relief, then absurd comedy and unease.
- **Distinctive device or turn:** the miracle is spoiled by the helper's own haste (N1). The tale does not narrate how the three live afterwards.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Yes: a clean puzzle of identity with an answer that can be argued either way.
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Yes, through the physical absurdity and the reader's intuition about who the men now are.
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** The motives for the self-beheadings are given only as "fate" and "delusion" (N1; KSS 12.13.28, 33), which can seem unmotivated; tonal balance between horror and comedy.

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- Two men kill themselves as offerings; a woman prepares to hang herself (KSS 12.13.28–43). Source-backed; divine reprieve. Handling matter.
- The goddess's grace is attributed to the wife's courage (*sattvotkarṣa*, 12.13.44, N1).
- Social setting: the characters are washermen (*rajaka*, 12.13.7–8, N1). The woman's mistake is the cause of the puzzle; the framing of that mistake is a retelling choice. No gendered generalisation recorded. N9 examination pending. Not a reason for exclusion (D-006).

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)

**Provisional label:** **Strong core contender**

- **Strongest reason to include:** a clear, memorable, genuinely distinct dilemma and a vivid, darkly comic plot, with the answer consistent across KSS, Ś and Hindi.
- **Strongest reason to hesitate:** the self-beheadings' motivation is thin and the tonal balance is demanding.
- **What could change this assessment:** reading of J 8 and the Ś and Hindi bodies for a different cause or ending.
