# TC-009: Anaṅgarati and her four suitors

> **Selection dossier, story bucket.** Evidence summary and qualitative assessment for the owner. Not a selection, ranking or adaptation. Provisional assessment: **Viable core contender**. Sources: [catalogue record](../../research/CANDIDATE_TALE_CATALOGUE.md), [N1 record](../../research/SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md) (N1 TC-009).

## 1. Identity and narrative summary

- **Working title:** Anaṅgarati and her four suitors (a catalogue label).
- **Recorded in:** KSS 9 (12.16.1–39, Tawney ch. 83) · BKM 9 (provisional) · Ś 7 · J 9 · Hindi VII · Ryder 9. Match E. Ś and the Hindi place it seventh (Cat. §2.1).
- **Essential events** (N1 TC-009, KSS 12.16.5–31):
  1. In Ujjayinī, King Vīradeva and Queen Padmarati, after austerities to Śiva, are promised a heroic son and a matchless daughter. A son, then a daughter, Anaṅgarati, are born (5–13).
  2. No portrait of any king seems worthy of her; offered a *svayaṃvara* (a ceremony where a woman chooses), she declines from modesty and asks to be given to a handsome young man who knows some unique skill (14–18).
  3. Four men from the south each describe a skill: a Śūdra weaver who makes five fine pairs of garments daily and distributes them; a Vaiśya who understands the speech of all beasts and birds; a Kṣatriya, Khaḍgadhara, unrivalled with the sword; a brahman, Jīvadatta, who can restore the dead to life (19–30).
  4. The king, seeing all four of divine appearance, hesitates "as if on a swing" (31). Nothing further happens in the tale.
- **Central characters and relationships:** Anaṅgarati (princess; sets the condition); King Vīradeva (father and decider); four suitors, one from each of the four *varṇas* (the traditional social classes).
- **What makes it recognisable as this family:** four suitors from four classes, each with a special skill, and a ruler who must choose.

## 2. The central dilemma

- **Betal asks:** to which of the four should the girl be given? (KSS 12.16.33).
- **Vikrama answers:** the Kṣatriya Khaḍgadhara, her equal (12.16.38). He opens by saying the Betal makes him break silence only to waste time, since the question is not hard (34–35a).
- **Reasoning:** a Kṣatriya woman cannot be given to a Śūdra or a Vaiśya; animal speech is of no practical use; the brahman, a "conjurer" proud of his practice, has fallen from his own caste duty (12.16.35b–37).
- **Across versions:** Ś 7 [Primary, OCR]: same answer, "by being of her own caste", with a verse on marrying in an equal family. Hindi VII [Translation, Platts]: same answer, "of her caste"; Platts suspects a *śāstra*/*śastra* confusion in his text. J 9: the report §7 gives the answer as the warrior-suitor in KSS, Ś, J and Hindi, but the basis for J's answer is not documented (the report documents a reading of J 10, and its account of whether J 25 was read is inconsistent, catalogue §1.4; J 9 was not read, so this is a report statement without a documented reading) [Secondary].
- **What the reader is really asked to judge:** the surface question is which skill is best; the answer does not compare skills but rules out three on caste and usefulness. The deeper question is whether a marvellous skill can outweigh social suitability. [Inference]

## 4. Common story spine and variant differences

**Common spine (as far as read):** four suitors of four classes; the Kṣatriya is the answer in KSS, Ś, Hindi (and J per report). Shared at the level of the suitors in KSS, Ś and Hindi; J's body is unread.

| Story element | KSS | Ś | Hindi | J / other | Why the difference matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Princess's requirement | a handsome young man with a unique skill | beauty, strength and knowledge | not recorded | not documented | changes what is being tested |
| Brahman suitor | restores the dead to life; "fallen from caste duty" | "knows the *śāstra* well" (uncertain OCR) | studied the learned writings | not documented | **material variant: no reviver of the dead in Ś or Hindi** |
| Kṣatriya | swordsman, invincible | swordsman, invincible in battle | archer who hits a target by sound | not documented | detail |
| Vaiśya | understands all beasts' speech | knows the speech of all creatures | knows languages | not documented | detail |
| Princess's response | declines the *svayaṃvara* from modesty | silent from modesty | not recorded | not documented | agency |

Whether the brahman in Ś is "learned" rather than "fallen" depends partly on OCR; the absence of a reviver does not (N1). The difference between the KSS brahman's "conjurer" status and the learned brahman changes why Vikrama rejects him.

## 5. Storytelling potential [Inference throughout]

- **Hook:** a princess who will marry only a man with a unique skill, and four strangers who each claim one.
- **Story engine:** a parade of four self-presentations; there is little action (N1: "short and static").
- **Stakes:** a choice of husband; low action, no danger.
- **Characters:** four types and a hesitant king; the princess sets the terms and then says nothing.
- **Emotional experience:** mostly curiosity and amusement; Vikrama's impatient opening is unusual (12.16.34).
- **Distinctive device or turn:** the answer ignores the glamorous skills and falls back on caste, which can play as an anticlimax or a puncturing of the setup.
- **Is the dilemma genuinely interesting?** Weakly as a story; more interesting as an argument, because the reader may expect the revival skill to win.
- **Can reflection emerge without a lecture?** Difficult: the recorded reasoning is a statement of caste rules.
- **What could make a retelling difficult?** Little plot to adapt; the answer rests on explicit caste hierarchy (see §7); the princess's agency is thin; the cast changes materially between KSS and Ś/Hindi.

## 7. Editorial and sensitivity considerations

- **Caste.** The answer rests on *varṇa* and *svakarma* (duty proper to one's class) at KSS 12.16.35–38, and the princess is described as a Kṣatriya woman who cannot be given to a Śūdra or Vaiśya. Ś adds a verse on marrying in an equal family, and the Hindi answer is "of her caste" (Platts). Source-backed. This is caste-based reasoning in the recorded answer; N9 examination is pending. Retelling implications, neutrally: the verdict can be kept as a character's view, reframed, or contested. Not a reason for exclusion by itself (D-006).
- Skills are assigned to class (a Śūdra weaver, a Vaiśya linguist). Source-backed.
- The woman's choice is replaced by the king's and then Vikrama's judgement (KSS 12.16.31, 38). Source-backed.

## 8. Selection assessment (provisional, for discussion)

**Provisional label:** **Viable core contender**

- **Strongest reason to include:** a short, clean dilemma that surprises the reader by overriding the marvellous skills, with agreement on the answer across KSS, Ś, Hindi and (per report) J.
- **Strongest reason to hesitate:** very little plot, a caste-based answer that needs careful framing, and a shared suitor setup with TC-005 that the owner may treat as one slot.
- **What could change this assessment:** the owner's D-035 judgement on TC-005/TC-009; a documented reading of J 9; specialist reading of Ś 7 and Hindi VII (the *śāstra*/*śastra* point).
- **Confidence:** moderate for the family's identity; low-to-moderate for the overlap judgement.
