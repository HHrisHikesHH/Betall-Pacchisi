# Selection Overview: the 27 Candidate Families

> **Selection dossier. Open this document first.** It summarises all 27 candidate story families and looks at the pool as a whole. **It does not select, rank or score anything.** The owner chooses the 25 (D-022, Q-411). The labels are an analyst's provisional assessments for discussion. Compiled 2026-11-18 by an AI session (Claude Code) from the repository's existing research; no new primary reading. Not independently reviewed.

**Where the detail is:** one dossier per family in [story-buckets/](story-buckets/); overlaps in [COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md](COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md); research triage in [RESEARCH_IMPACT_AUDIT.md](RESEARCH_IMPACT_AUDIT.md); outside leads in [SUPPLEMENTARY_MATERIAL_REGISTER.md](SUPPLEMENTARY_MATERIAL_REGISTER.md); the owner's worksheet in [SELECTION_DECISION_SHEET.md](SELECTION_DECISION_SHEET.md).

## 1. The pool in brief

- **27 candidate families:** 24 core families (TC-001 to TC-024), found in Somadeva (KSS), Śivadāsa (Ś), the Hindi *Baitāl Pachīsī* and (mostly) Jambhaladatta (J), plus 3 Jambhaladatta-only families (TC-025 to TC-027). TC-023 is one family with two Hindi variants (owner's decision; not reopened).
- **Margin of two.** 27 candidates for 25 places. Every exclusion beyond two needs a candidate from outside the pool, which the owner would have to authorise (D-001, D-002; [register](SUPPLEMENTARY_MATERIAL_REGISTER.md)).
- **At least one J-only tale is unavoidable.** Because only 24 candidates are core, any 25 drawn from this pool must include at least one of TC-025 to TC-027, and more if core tales are excluded (see §5).
- **Evidence.** Every core tale was read in full in the KSS Sanskrit (N1); Ś and the Hindi were read mainly at their questions and answers; J 21–23 were read in full in Emeneau (N3); Hindi XXII and XXIV were read in full (N2). All readings are AI readings, not a specialist's. Kṣemendra (BKM) is a provisional concordance only.
- **Provisional assessments:** 12 **Strong core contender**, 13 **Viable core contender**, 2 **Distinctness or overlap contested** (TC-008 and TC-011, as a pair), 0 **Evidence insufficient to judge**. These labels are not a ranking: "Viable" candidates are not ranked below "Strong" ones for selection, and several Viable tales carry exactly the variety the collection may need.

## 3. The pool through the seven proposed lenses

The lenses are the catalogue's **proposed** method (catalogue §9.1), not an approved rule. Each is described in words; there are no scores.

### A. Type of central dilemma or riddle

| Form | Candidates |
|---|---|
| Superlative comparison ("who was most…?") | 004, 007, 008, 010, 011, 016, 017, 021, 026 |
| Allocation or rightful claim ("whose wife? to whom?") | 002, 005, 006, 009, 015, 019 |
| Blame ("who is guilty?") | 001, 013, 022, 027 |
| Reaction ("why did X react so?") | 012, 014, 020, 023 |
| Causal ("why did it fail?") | 018, 025 |
| Categorical generalisation | 003 |
| Kinship puzzle, unanswered | 024 |

- **Well represented:** superlative comparisons (nine), allocation (six), blame and reaction (four each).
- **Repetition risk:** the four **reaction riddles**, plus two causal riddles of a similar kind, make "explain this person's inner state" the largest family of question after the superlative (group 3). **Allocation** questions concentrate on who gets a woman (002, 005, 009, 015; also 006). **Two answers repeat an argument:** 004 and 017 (group 2).
- **Gaps:** there is only one unanswered riddle (024) and one categorical question (003). No question asks Vikrama to predict an outcome or to choose a course of action; all ask him to judge what has already happened. That is the cycle's nature, not a gap to fill.
- **Confidence:** high for KSS; the forms are read directly from the questions (N1 §4.1; N3 §6.1).

### B. Characters and relationships

- **Well represented:** kings and servants or ministers (004, 007, 012, 017; seen from the other side in 027); spouses, lovers and rival suitors (most of the pool); parents and children (004, 019, 020, 023, 024); brothers (008, 022, 025).
- **Distinctive:** a courtesan at the centre (025); tricksters and a bawd (015, 026); a thief as a moral figure (010, 014); two talking birds (003); a young child as the mind the riddle explores (020); an old man's relation to his own body (023).
- **Gaps:** friendship as a central relationship appears only as a helper's role (001, 015, 026). Siblings beyond brothers are almost absent (a brother in 006). Few tales centre on old age (023) or childhood (020).
- **Confidence:** high for KSS; J's versions of the core tales are mostly unread.

### C. Conflict and stakes

- Life and death dominate: most tales put a life at stake, and divine or magical revival reverses death in 002, 004, 006, 014, 016, 021 (N1 §4.4). **Revival is a pattern** a collection may want to space out.
- Other stakes are well represented: honour and marriage (010, 015, 017), justice and blame (001, 013, 027), knowledge and its misuse (018, 022), identity (006, 023).
- **Lower-stakes tales** are few and comic: 008, 011, and the frame of 005. If 008 and 011 compete for one slot (group 6), the collection's lightest register narrows to one tale.
- **Confidence:** high.

### D. Emotional experience

- **Well represented:** pathos and devotion (004, 016, 017), dread and horror (002, 020, 027), wonder and marvel (007, 012, 016, 018), intrigue (001, 015, 026).
- **Humour is scarce.** Clear comic or ironic tales are 008, 011, 015, 026, with comic elements in 005, 006, 023 and 024 (N1 §4.4; N3 §6.3). Most of the comedy depends on trickery or hyperbole. Choosing only one of 008/011 and one of 015/026 would leave two strongly comic tales.
- **Tenderness** is rare and usually brief (TC-010's bride and husband; TC-016's lovers).
- **Confidence:** moderate. Emotional character is an interpretation of the events (D-036 puts it first, but the texts record events, not effects).

### E. Narrative form and structure

- **Varied.** Embedded tales and a frame within the frame (003; a false story in 001; Ilā and Budha in 015); triple or repeated tests (008, 010, 011); journeys made twice (007, 012); a nested illusion (018); a chain of rebirths (025); stories that stop at their climax (020); an unanswered riddle that opens the frame's ending (024); a long caper of deceptions (026).
- **Length varies greatly:** from 39 KSS verses (009) to 207 (016). Short tales (006, 009, 011, 013) and very long ones (012, 016, 025) are both represented.
- **Repetition:** the Vetāla's challenge to Vikrama's answer appears in 001, 004 and 017 in KSS (N1 §4.2); with 004 and 017 it carries the same argument.
- **Confidence:** high for KSS structure.

### F. Setting and atmosphere

- **Well represented:** royal courts and cities; forests and hunts (001, 007, 020, 024); cremation grounds (002, 023; the frame's own setting); temples (004, 006, 018).
- **Distinctive:** undersea cities (007, 012); a Himalayan and seashore Vidyādhara world (016); a city under a monster's siege (027); a funeral well at Gayā (019); an illusory lifetime (018).
- **Gap:** village and working life appear only incidentally (the washermen of 006; the weaver suitor of 009). The pool's settings lean royal and brahmanical.
- **Confidence:** moderate (settings are read from KSS and N3; Ś and Hindi settings mostly unread).

### Gender portrayal and agency

- **Women drive many plots** (001's Padmāvatī, 010's Madanasenā, 014's Ratnavatī, 021's Anaṅgamañjarī, 025's Rūpavatī, 006's Madanasundarī, who causes the puzzle). But the questions are mostly **about men** (N1 §4.3), and several women are prizes or objects of men's dispute (002, 005, 009, 015, 017, 024).
- **Gendered generalisations** in answers or narration: 003 (answer), 014 (answer), 021 (narrator), 024 (a helpless woman's line), 025 (the courtesan called "very wicked"); maxims added in Ś and the Hindi to 003, 010, 014, 020 (N1 §4.4; N3 §6.4). Under D-006 none is a reason to exclude; each needs a handling decision, which N9 would support for the shortlist.
- **Women's voices against men** are rare and valuable: Rūpavatī's account of male abandonment (025), the mynah's tale in 003, Madanasundarī's reproach to the goddess (006), Malayavatī's to Gaurī (016).
- **Caste** grounds the answer in 009 (and in the Hindi reasoning of 016); a class hierarchy is implicit in 005's answer. The *śabara* robbers of 024 and the Kanjar of Hindi XXI (022) carry community labels.
- **Confidence:** moderate; the KSS and J layers are listed, the Ś and Hindi layers only partly.

## 4. Does the pool give enough variety?

**Mostly yes.** Even after the hardest overlaps are resolved one way, the pool still spans every lens. No two candidates were found to be the same story (see [COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md](COMPARISON_BUCKETS.md)). The three J-only tales add things the core lacks: a courtesan's own story and rebirths (025), a pure caper with a disputed answer (026), and city-scale horror (027).

**The pressure points are question forms and tone, not duplicate plots:**

1. **Reaction riddles:** 012, 014, 020, 023 (plus causal 018, 025). Keeping all of them makes "why did he react so?" a recurring ending.
2. **King-against-servant reasoning:** 004 and 017 resolve with the same argument.
3. **Refinement comedies:** 008 and 011 are the closest pair, and also two of very few light tales.
4. **Child or family sacrifice for a king:** 004 and 020, with willing self-offering also in 016.
5. **Revival as a reset:** six tales reverse a death by divine or magical means.
6. **Suitor and "whose wife?" questions:** 002, 005, 009, 015.

## 5. The hardest trade-offs

- **The margin is two.** If the owner wants to leave out more than two of the 27 candidates (for example one of 008/011 and two reaction riddles), the pool falls below 25. Then there are only two options: keep a tale the owner had doubts about, or authorise a bounded look outside the pool. The only plausible outside lead is the unique 25th story in Śivadāsa MS *c* (N7; register item 1), whose content is unknown.
- **The 25th narrated tale.** KSS and Ś count the frame's conclusion as their 25th unit and narrate only 24 tales; D-035 excludes the frame from the count. So a KSS- or Ś-based collection still needs a 25th narrated tale from elsewhere in the pool; the J-only tales can supply it (catalogue §6.4). **This is separate from TC-024**, which is itself a Betal-narrated tale, distinct from the frame conclusion that follows it, and a full candidate under Q-411. Q-002 concerns only how the outer frame (including the hand-over after TC-024) is presented. **Inherited catalogue wording (for later attention, not changed here):** catalogue §6.4 says TC-024's place among the 25 "is therefore connected to Q-002 and Q-411". Under D-035, which takes precedence, TC-024 is a Betal-narrated tale distinct from the frame conclusion that follows it, so its candidacy depends on Q-411 alone; Q-002 concerns only how the outer frame is presented.
- **The J-only tales.** TC-025 to TC-027 are complete, distinct and add variety, but rest on one recension (TC-026 and TC-027 on one Bengali manuscript plus a poor print). Their evidence base therefore matters to every possible selection, not just to some:

**The J-only arithmetic.** The pool has 24 core candidates (TC-001 to TC-024) and 3 Jambhaladatta-only candidates (TC-025 to TC-027). If the 25 come entirely from this pool, **any selection of 25 includes at least one J-only candidate**. If the owner excludes *k* of the 24 core candidates, the selection needs at least *k* + 1 J-only candidates:

| Core candidates excluded | J-only candidates needed |
|---|---|
| 0 | at least 1 of the 3 |
| 1 | at least 2 of the 3 |
| 2 | all 3 |

If all three J-only candidates are ruled out, the pool supplies only 24 acceptable candidates, and a bounded outside-candidate investigation would be needed to find a 25th (N7; [RESEARCH_IMPACT_AUDIT.md](RESEARCH_IMPACT_AUDIT.md)). This is arithmetic, not a recommendation: it says nothing about *which* J-only tale, if any, is suitable.

- **Variant choice is part of selection for some tales.** TC-010, 020 and 026 are different stories depending on the version used (lens G). The owner may wish to note the intended version alongside the choice, under D-034, even though Q-401 stays open.
- **Handling difficulty.** The most demanding material (003's verdict, 009's caste answer, 020's child sacrifice, 024's coerced marriages, 025's verdict on the courtesan) sits in tales that also offer something distinctive. D-006 makes this a handling question, not an exclusion rule.

## 6. Questions for the owner (not decisions)

1. Do you want to apply the proposed lenses (catalogue §9), or to judge variety informally? The lenses are not approved.
2. How many reaction riddles, and how many "whose wife?" questions, can the collection carry?
3. Should TC-008 and TC-011 share one slot?
4. Is TC-024 one of the 25? (A Q-411 choice like any other; its frame transition is a Q-002 presentation matter.)
5. Which J-only tales are admissible on their evidence base? (At least one must be chosen for a 25 drawn wholly from this pool; see §5.)
6. If your choices leave fewer than 25, do you want a bounded investigation of the Śivadāsa MS *c* story (N7)?

## 7. What this overview cannot tell you

- Anything about J's telling of most core tales, Ś and Hindi narrative bodies, or BKM's text.
- Any specialist judgement: all readings are AI readings (D-020; Q-405).
