### 8.2 Recommended base (for approval): KSS structure, with charter adaptations
- **Why KSS:** it is the best-read witness in this repo. It keeps the warning *late* (most suspense), gives the clearest penalty rule (knowing and staying silent is the crime), and its ending, where the king asks that the tales be remembered, fits a reading app.
- **Adaptations (all recorded as our composition):**
  1. **The ascetic** keeps his name (Kṣāntiśīla: "whose nature is patience", a fine irony) and his role as the person who performs the rite, in a cremation ground. The plan to use a human sacrifice to gain power is **reported by Betal** (KSS 12.31.68–73; in every witness the king learns it only from Betal), not shown as independently established fact; the ambiguity is kept. His **sectarian label is dropped**: not Buddhist, not Jain, not a named Śaiva order, and he carries no sect-specific role word. He is described **by function only, as the person who performs the rite**, defined by his ambition, not his community. ~~He becomes a sādhak of the burning ground.~~ *[Withdrawn, D-055 R1: the phrase "sādhak of the burning ground" is retired.]* *[Decided, D-055 R1, 2026-11-21: the name is provisional only pending a Hindi sensitivity read; if that reader finds it reads as Buddhist, the user decides a replacement.]* The challenger warns that "tantrik" is the stock villain of Hindi film and TV, and Tantra is a living tradition, so the word is avoided, as is Bhairava or skull iconography that would signal Kāpālika anyway. Essential: his specialised ritual knowledge. Incidental: his sect.
  2. **No caste-coded prehistory**: the Hindi oilman and potter backstory is not used. Betal's own past stays a mystery he reveals on his own terms. That makes it a character asset, not a lineage.
  3. **The ending without servitude.** This is **source-supported**: KSS has no servitude (only Ś, J and the Hindi do). KSS shows no captivity or servitude here. Betal departs by his own power; the adaptation does not describe him as freed or liberated (D-055 R5; D-058 item 4). ~~What is our invention is any liberation of Betal ("freed from the tree"), and it is recorded as such.~~ *[Withdrawn, D-055 R5, D-058 item 4: no liberation is used.]* The king's request that the tales be told stays, from KSS. The divine coronation is reduced to a single image or left out. *Interpretive choice.*
  4. **No offering, no reward-by-deity for the killing.** J's offering of the ascetic to Devī for universal rule is not used. *[Factual note, §8.6 F4: this refers to J's Devī offering. KSS itself has the king offer the monk's head and heart to Betal, 12.32.19. Whether that is kept is open for the frame's A3 packet; this item does not decide it.]* *[Decided, §8.7: kept, restrained, D-055 R4; the heart and offering are the king's own further acts, D-056, D-058 item 3.]* If Betal's past is ever touched, J's oil-miller is a working man exploited by a teacher, not a monstrous origin (caste reviewer).
  5. **Vikram's killing of the ascetic** stays, and it stays **morally live**. My draft called it "self-defence". The challenger showed that KSS frames it as a pre-emptive killing by ruse, done on Betal's instruction **to win the sovereignty the ascetic sought**. We keep that, drop the sovereignty *reward*, and **do not clean the act**. The night ends with Vikram **examined, not vindicated** (§3 principle 6, applied to him). Did he kill to survive, or did he take the prize? The frame's final pages leave that honestly open. The honest-difficulty test applies to the frame too.

### 8.3 The arc: what changes over the night
- **Why Vikram goes.** He goes out of obligation for years of unexplained gifts, the gem-filled fruit. *Charter reading:* patronage-debt is the "loyalty" being examined (§3.4.5, principle 6). He goes because he owes, not because he has asked what for.
- **What Betal wants.** On the surface: to pass the road *[§8.6 F3: KSS gives "to ease the road"; "to escape" has no KSS basis]*. Underneath, there are **two candidate motives, and the frame blueprint chooses between them**:
  - (a) Our characterisation: Betal is weighing whether this king is worth warning. KSS supports only a *late* evaluation (63–65: the king "cannot be deceived", praised for courage), so this would be an extrapolation.
  - (b) Ś 25's motive (provisional, OCR): Betal has deceived the king many times, and the warning comes when the game is played out.

  Either way, Betal is **not** a vehicle for the charter. He is mischievous, self-interested and unknowable. *[Refined, §8.7: D-055 R2 chose a layered, unresolved motive; D-056 records the source ledger and what may be inferred.]*
- **Rules.** Vikram must answer if he knows (KSS penalty). At tale endings where Vikram answers (e.g. 12.8.196, 12.9.41, 12.14.114), KSS narrates Betal's departure after he breaks silence; in the first instance the king then goes to the *śiṃśapā* tree to retrieve the corpse (departure at 12.8.196, retrieval at 12.9.1), and this one cited retrieval is not offered as a general rule for every tale ending. The opening sequence at 12.8.54–55 places the first return during Vikram's speech but does not explicitly state whether the speech causes it. The additional frame-only loop, in which Betal deliberately provokes Vikram to speak before a further return, is an adaptation mechanic (D-057). ~~The moment he speaks, Betal is free and returns to the tree.~~ *[Withdrawn, 2026-11-23: not a source statement and not an adaptation rule; "free" is not used for Betal, and KSS 12.8.196 does not say he returns to the tree. Kept as history only.]* *[Amended, §8.7: the loop is shown once, by use, and is not stated as a rule in the adaptation.]* The trap is that his virtue (honest judgment) is exactly what keeps undoing his task.
- **Escalation, in four movements.** This is a *working shape for the frame blueprint*, not a template. The challenger warns that a schematic growth arc is itself lecture-prone, and that "his answers are changing him" is a modern arc nobody attested. Movements are felt through events (fatigue, weather, the walk, Betal's provocations), never announced, and Vikram is not "improved".
  1. *Contract* (opening tales, including TC-001 in KSS order): mechanics, humour and irritation. Vikram answers quickly and confidently. TC-001's "the king is guilty" lands early, as a first sting.
  2. *Friction* (middle): more tales turn back on rulers (016's Vāsuki, 020, 027). The walks get shorter and the dialogue gets sharper. ~~The burning ground's people are seen in passing as *people*, not scenery.~~ *[Withdrawn, §8.7: D-058 item 5.]*
  3. *Doubt* (late): Vikram begins to ask Betal questions back. Whether he changes is left to the reader.
  4. *Silence* (TC-024): the one riddle he cannot answer. **The text says he did not know** (N1, *nājñāsīt*). We keep that honestly: it is not a "wise refusal". His not-knowing is what earns the warning. The warning, the confrontation, the ending.
- **Transitions.** Each tale is bracketed by a **short frame segment** (½ to 2 pages). Each segment varies one or two of: the walk, the weather or hour, a fragment of Betal's past, a reaction to the last answer, a hook for the next. Nothing is repeated as a formula. The tree, shoulder, tale and question loop is *shown* fully twice (the first and the last) and *elided* in between. *[Amended, §8.7: the frame-only first loop of D-057 is also shown, so the loop is shown at the frame-only loop, at the first shipped tale and at the climax.]*
- **Order (Q-413, recommendation):** use the KSS sequence for the slate's tales (it is the base), with TC-024 last. TC-025 to TC-027, which are J-only, are placed by the lead for variety and pacing in the frame blueprint. Final order is approved with the frame blueprint.
- **Gender in the frame (gender reviewer).** The documented frame has no women, and every judge and questioner is male. **No token woman is invented.** Instead, the frame's own mechanisms make each verdict *a judgement under pressure*, not the collection's last word: Betal's objections (attested in KSS 1, 4, 17), Vikram's strategic answers (TC-022, 12.29.47), and his not-knowing (TC-024). Not using the Hindi prehistory also avoids the Bharthari fruit episode, which in common tellings turns on a queen's infidelity. That is *not verified in this repo*; N9 should check it if the Hindi frame is ever revisited.
- **Tale-to-frame echoes, not themes.** TC-020 (a king lets a child die for his safety) sits shortly before *Betal's claim* that the ascetic means to sacrifice *Vikram* *[amended, §8.7: D-058 item 2; the frame never confirms the claim]*. TC-022's aside (he answers knowing it lets Betal go back to the tree) *[wording amended, §8.7]* marks the turn into *Doubt*. TC-001 (early) and 027 (later) both answer "the king is guilty". These are placement choices; no tale is rewritten to fit a frame theme.
- **Narrator.** Betal tells the tales within the story world (D-035). **Who narrates the frame stays open (Q-005).** The frame blueprint drafts its segments so that they work with an unnamed narrating voice *or* with other options the user may choose. My draft wrote "an unnamed narrator tells the frame", which would have quietly settled part of Q-005 (challenger). Withdrawn.

### 8.5 Built for additions after launch
- The frame's **climax is fixed**: TC-024, then the warning and the ending. Every tale added after launch is **inserted before TC-024**, so the ending never has to be rewritten.
- Each tale is bracketed by **its own** short frame segments (§8.3 transitions). Adding a tale means writing its segments through the normal workflow. No other segment changes, except where a neighbouring transition line must be amended, and that goes through the frame's own revision cycle with your approval.
- The four movements are loose enough to absorb insertions, so pacing is re-checked at each addition and not re-planned.
