# Critique of the 19-story production plan (independent ethical challenger)

I checked the plan against the dossiers, N1, N3, DECISIONS.md and the PR #9 branch. I found five misreported or overclaimed source facts, one structural hole in the verdict protocol, and a frame that ends up glorifying Vikram, which the charter says it must not do. Plan line numbers below are prefixed `plan:`.

## 1. Grading errors

**TC-026: REJECT, or move it to the suspended pool.** The evidence is P plus a poor print, with a corrupt passage (`TC-026.md:34`) and Low–Moderate confidence (`:92`). Its dilemma overlaps TC-015. The adaptation (plan:337) changes almost everything:
- It invents Anaṅgasenā's knowing complicity.
- It swaps in the Newari answer but keeps J's plot. In the Newari, the actions are divided differently: Śaśideva plans the escape (`TC-026.md:30`). So "the bawd was the occasion of everything" is weakest on exactly the plot we keep.
- It replaces the corpse.

What survives of J 22 is the name. Calling this ADAPT is under-grading. If it is kept, it is a MAJOR REWRITE.

**The grades are applied inconsistently.**
- TC-027 has *new* reasoning that "must be supplied" (plan:345) and is graded ADAPT, yet TC-025 with new reasoning is a MAJOR REWRITE.
- TC-015 adopts a variant and is a MAJOR REWRITE, while TC-026 adopts a variant and invents the heroine's motive, and is ADAPT.
- Pick one rule. Under it, 027 and 026 are MAJOR REWRITE.

**TC-019 should be MAJOR REWRITE (plan:263–273).** The verdict *is* a purchase rule. The begetter and the king are excluded because each was paid (`TC-019.md:22`; Ś: "both were bought"). But the thief also paid, 1,000 gold for the girl. You cannot "drop field ownership" and keep the answer "the thief": nothing else grounds his claim. Either Betal presses the contradiction (which makes the tale better and harder), or the reasoning is rebuilt. That is not "substantial ADAPT".

**TC-007 is ADAPT in name only (plan:188–189).** The plan makes the hero a commoner, has the princess choose, and turns the king's repayment into "the dive itself". That is three structural changes. It also drops the ambiguity in KSS 113b that N1 flagged.

**TC-006 is not "light" (plan:179).** See §3.

**TC-003: REJECT unless a hard comparison exists.** See §2.

## 2. Over-correction

**The same "she chooses" fix appears in 7 tales:** 001, 002, 007, 015 (Mṛgāṅkavatī), 019, 024 and 026. This is the over-correction the charter itself forbids (§3.3.5). Worse, where the fix lands on coercion, it launders it:
- **TC-019:** Dhanavatī "makes the bargain herself" (plan:270). This erases the mother's sale of her daughter and turns destitution into choice.
- **TC-015:** Mṛgāṅkavatī "chooses to run" (plan:240). This turns sex obtained by deception into consent.
- **TC-024:** "Line 50 becomes her own clear-eyed sentence" (plan:318). This makes the helplessness line sound like agency.

Agency the plot cannot honour is decoration.

**Betal's objection has become the charter's voice.** The plan uses it in 003, 015, 020, 025, 027 and 001. KSS attests it in 1, 4 and 17 only (plan:424).
- In TC-025, Betal is given the source answer purely so it can be knocked down, which strawmans the source.
- In TC-027, Vikram's line "a king who sells his people… owns every death" (plan:345) is a thesis, not a judgement.

This is the back-door lecture the North Star rules out (`STORYTELLING_NORTH_STAR.md:20–21`: "may support several honest readings").

**Vikram is too obviously right in TC-003.** "Dhanadatta" against Vasudattā is murdering his rescuer against framing her husband (plan:160). That is not a dilemma. Placed inside a men-versus-women wager, it reads as "men are worse": the same generalisation with the sign flipped. The plan says "then it is a real dilemma" (plan:166). It isn't.

**TC-008: the plan breaks its own §3.2.8.** That clause says "do not make a different community… the fool". Keeping "brahman brothers" as the butt (plan:202) does exactly that. To satirise purity practice without caricaturing the caste, the labels must not carry the joke. This is a caste MAJOR REWRITE, not ADAPT.

**TC-006 rejects reasoning that KSS itself gives.** KSS 53 says "the head is chief of the limbs *and* identity resides in it" (`TC-006.md:22`). Rejecting "head is chief" as a body-hierarchy echo (plan:176) over-reads it, and misattributes it to Ś and the Hindi only.

**TC-018** recasts wavering as sympathetic ("is it failure to hesitate…?", plan:259). That quietly reverses the verdict.

**Selective label-dropping skews the cast.** Labels are dropped in 002, 013, 018, 020, 021, 022 and 027 but kept in 008 and 019. The result is that brahmans appear mostly as fools or sellers. It also erases a real injustice: who had access to learning. TC-002's "learned men, not a varṇa" (plan:142) hides that the revival spell sat behind varṇa-gated learning. Principle 3 cuts the other way here.

## 3. Under-correction

**TC-006.** KSS gives the motives as "fate" and "delusion" (N1:534 list, *vidhipreraṇa/vyāmohita*). Inventing "a vow for their happiness" (plan:176) makes self-beheading *more* valorised: a deliberate, loving sacrifice that a goddess rewards. Her attempted hanging is not addressed at all. With two suicides and a suicide attempt at a temple, ending in divine reward, this is not a light pilot.

**TC-020.**
- The boy's own speech persuading his parents ("the body is impure, worth only the merit it earns", `TC-020.md` events 3) is the real valorisation, and the plan never touches it.
- A seven-year-old's "willingness" has to be shown as manufactured by the proclamation and the reward.
- "The only power left to a child" (plan:283) romanticises again.
- The demon is a *brahmarākṣasa*, which the plan never mentions.

**TC-024.** The frame's climax uses two coerced women as a kinship puzzle, and the next beat is Betal's glee and the reward. The women's "decisive moment" is cosmetic. Their cost should weigh on the silence through events, not a choice.

**TC-010.** The plan keeps the KSS reasoning for the husband: he "could hardly keep a wife known to be attached to another" (`TC-010.md:23`). That is sexual-honour logic, and it goes unflagged.

**TC-013.** Harisvāmin's dying threat ("she will bear the sin of killing a brahman") is what causes the expulsion. Changing the vocabulary of the question alone misses it.

**TC-015.** The recommended reasoning, "the lie that came first was the lover's" (plan:239), is wrong on the facts: both deceptions were Mūladeva's. It also awards her to a stranger *against the one consent she gave*. The tale where she actually chose is the one where the plan overrides her choice.

**Mūladeva (015 and 026).** He arranges a deception-seduction and adultery and fakes a death, and is never examined. Only placement is discussed.

**The frame killing.** See §6.

## 4. Misreported facts or invention presented as source

- **Ś 3 (plan:159, plan:383).** Ś asks "whose sin is greater?" and answers *women, never men*, with a verse (`SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md:132`). "Which of these two" is the Hindi IV wording, and the Hindi also answers "the woman". The attested comparative form only ever yields the misogynist answer. Narrowing it to two individuals is entirely our composition and must be labelled that way.
- **TC-007 (plan:183).** "Answer agrees in… J 7" is false: J's answer is "not independently documented" (`TC-007.md:24`). The plan's own S3 (plan:373) contradicts it.
- **TC-006.** Misattributed reasoning (see §2).
- **Frame (plan:400, plan:411).** The KSS ending row omits that the Vetāla tells the king to behead the monk *so as to gain the Vidyādhara sovereignty* (`SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE.md:442`). Calling it "self-defence" is our reading.
- **Checked and correct:** TC-024 *nājñāsīt* (60), the TC-020 outcome not narrated (KSS 124–125), and the Newari TC-026 answer (the bawd).
- **TC-013 "the husband *is* the hasty judge".** This is our narrowing. KSS's judge is anyone who blames without reflection, a self-referential turn (`TC-013.md` §2).

## 5. The Ambedkar charter

**What is right.** The quotations look accurate and the unverified women-progress quote is honestly flagged.

**What is overclaimed.**
- Context says the texts were "read online in primary form"; they were reproductions.
- Principle 6 is cut down. The full sentence contrasts *politics* with religion ("Bhakti in religion may be a road to salvation"). Applying it to fictional kings is our extension, and column B should say so.

**What is missing.**
1. **AoC's attack on the sanctity of the śāstras.** This is the principle most relevant to verdicts that rest on dharmaśāstra: *kṣetraja*, *brahmahatyā*, the validity of *gāndharva* marriage, Hindi VI's "book of law".
2. **Documented gender grounds** to replace the dropped quote: the Hindu Code Bill and his resignation over it, and "The Rise and Fall of the Hindu Woman".
3. **Why the KSS Buddhist *bhikṣu* villain matters specifically.** Ambedkarite Buddhists are a living community.
4. **Principle 6 is never applied to Vikram** (see §6).
5. **Human readers.** An AI-administered charter "informed by Ambedkar", with no human reader from caste-affected communities, risks appropriation. Recommend a human sensitivity reader for 008, 019, 020 and the frame.

**One misapplication.** D-039 says the charter governs content where it conflicts with the North Star. That changes D-036's standing, so "D-036 is unchanged" is not true.

## 6. The frame

**The "test" motive.** KSS 63–65 supports only a late evaluation: the king "cannot be deceived" and is praised for courage. "Every tale tests whether Vikram judges by acts or station" (plan:415) projects the charter onto Betal. Ś 25 offers a rival motive: Betal says he has deceived the king many times, so the king fell silent. The test idea should be one option, not the arc.

**The arc contradicts the principle meant to govern it.** Betal tests the king, the king proves worthy, Betal is "freed", and the king kills cleanly in self-defence. That is hero-worship. KSS's pre-emptive killing by ruse, done to win the sorcerer's sovereignty, should stay morally live. Delete the reward if you like, but don't sanitise the act.

**The four movements are schematic.**
- "Doubt: his answers are changing him" (plan:420) is a modern growth arc nobody attested.
- There is an internal error. TC-001 is tale 1 in KSS order, so it falls in "Contract", yet it is listed under "Friction" (plan:419).
- TC-016 and TC-027 are adjacent in J: J 24 opens straight after J 23 (`TC-016.md:75`). "Far apart" ignores a possible source pairing.

**"Unmarked" is not unmarked.** "*Tantrik sādhak*" (plan:407) is the stock evil-tantrik of Hindi film and TV, and Tantra is a living tradition. Use "a sādhak of the burning ground" and avoid Bhairava and skull iconography, which signals Kāpālika anyway. The TC-018 ascetic is *mahāvratī*, Pāśupata-like (N1:350), and the plan doesn't address him.

**The "no servitude" ending.** KSS has no servitude, so this is *source-supported*, not an overreach. What is invented is the liberation: "freed from the tree".

**Q-005 is partly fixed despite the claim.** "Betal tells the tales, an unnamed narrator tells the frame" (plan:426) settles part of what North Star line 43 leaves open.

## 7. Process risks

**Silent promotion of the count.** Superseding the "25" in D-001 and D-035 is the lead's inference from "active production slate". Six tales are only *suspended*. The North Star (D-036, line 73) also states 25, and the README hook would change. Ask the user explicitly.

**Who drops a tale.** "A slate story that fails… is dropped" (plan:26) must say *by the user* (`STORY_WORKFLOW.md:42`).

**Loophole in D-041.** For causal "why" questions (TC-020, TC-025), the reasoning *is* the answer. Classing these as "no dilemma change" skips the escalation. Require user approval for any composed reasoning.

**Bulk approval.** §12 item 3 approves all 19 treatments at once. Approve the frame and the charter; take the treatments one by one at A3.

**IDs.** D-038 and later is fine: gaps have a precedent (D-032). "SUPERSEDED in part" has a precedent only on unmerged PR #9. P-015 to P-017 should receive D-IDs when approved. Q-412 and Q-413 are free.

**Sequencing.** A docs PR that edits DECISIONS "Superseded decisions: None yet" will conflict with PR #9's edit of the same lines. Land R0 first, or base the docs branch on PR #9.

**Workload.** Roughly 15–20 sessions per unit across 20 units, plus 40 user packets. Reserve the challenger and the dual A3 review for MAJOR REWRITE tales.

**Pilots.** TC-006 is not light. TC-025 is J-only, has no Hindi witness to calibrate register against (Q-402), has the lowest confidence, and its verdict is wholly ours. Use TC-013 or TC-001 (well-attested across KSS, Ś and Hindi; 001 also tests the frame's opening). Run 025 third.

## 8. Application roadmap

**R1 is premature.** PR #9's CLAUDE.md says plain text stays "until a real tale needs formatting", and PROJECT_PLAN M3 says app work is "likely none beyond what the text reveals". The plan also says it avoids speculative schema. Derive the grammar from the pilot drafts.

**Code-level problems.**
- `frontmatter.ts` says "should get a real parser, not an extension of this one". Write a new body module.
- `:::frame` duplicates the frame segments interleaved by the manifest.
- There is no construct for nested narrative levels (TC-003, TC-025).
- `*emphasis*` in Devanagari falls back to a synthetic oblique; only Noto Serif Devanagari 400 and 600 are loaded.

**`approved_ref`.**
- A commit SHA breaks under squash or rebase merges and shallow CI clones.
- Use a sha256 of the normalised body, recorded in `status.md`.
- Add a CI rule that no `approved.md` is ever modified.

**Missing pieces.**
- `order` is currently required, and `ui.storyNumber(order)` drives numbering, so the manifest needs a migration and frame-segment numbering rules.
- `/kathaen/:slug` → `/:lang/…` needs redirects.
- `<html lang>` is static `hi`.
- There is no error boundary for a failed lazy-chunk load after a redeploy (only Suspense).
- Q-111 accessibility is unaddressed: question, answer and verse semantics; reduced motion.

**R0.** The advice is right but incomplete. The HANDOFF "Current status" bullets ("no application code exists on main" against the foundation-stage text) must be *rewritten*, not merged as "both histories". Drop PR #9's "after a final independent review" line. CLAUDE.md and D-037 still say "25".

## 9. The single most important change

Stop resolving each problem by adding an answer: a woman's choice, a corrective objection from Betal, a cleaner Vikram. Each adapted tale must pass an **"honest difficulty" gate** at A3: after the change, could a thoughtful reader defensibly reach a different verdict than Vikram's?
- **Fails today:** TC-003, TC-015's reasoning, TC-027's line, and the frame's self-defence framing.
- **Fixable by pressing the source's own contradictions instead of inventing agency:** TC-019's purchase logic and TC-006's "fate/delusion".

Apply that gate to the frame too, so the night ends with Vikram examined, not vindicated.
