# Art Direction

This document records the visual direction for the project's illustrations. It applies decisions D-023 to D-026 and D-031 in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), and notes the layout preference PREF-001. **The final art style and reading layout are not decided.** The questions are tracked in [OPEN_QUESTIONS.md](OPEN_QUESTIONS.md) (`Q-1xx`, `Q-2xx`).

## 1. Purpose of illustration

**APPROVED (D-023)**

Illustrations exist to **strengthen the reader's mental imagery and the storytelling**. They are never generic decoration. An illustration earns its place by:

- showing what the words make the reader imagine, in a way that deepens it and does not replace it,
- marking an important event or emotional turning point,
- holding the mood of a scene while the reader moves through it.

## 2. Cinematic pacing

**APPROVED (D-023)**

Illustrations follow a **cinematic rhythm**, not a fixed one-picture-per-page pattern.

- An illustration **may stay visible through a longer passage or a whole scene**, the way an establishing shot holds while action unfolds.
- **Important events and emotional turning points may get dedicated artwork.**
- Quiet passages may need fewer images. Climactic moments may need a deliberate visual beat.

*How often illustrations appear, and exactly how they change on screen, is OPEN: see `Q-103` and `Q-104`.*

## 3. Visual identity: unmistakably Indian

**APPROVED (D-024)**

The artwork is **AI-generated** and must be **unmistakably Indian** in visual character. It should evoke the richness of Indian visual traditions. The user has named these as possibilities to research:

| Tradition to research | Status |
|---|---|
| Indian cave paintings and murals | RESEARCH REQUIRED |
| Indian miniature painting traditions | RESEARCH REQUIRED |
| Traditional Indian portraits and illustration | RESEARCH REQUIRED |
| Temple art and sculptural traditions | RESEARCH REQUIRED |
| Other appropriate Indian artistic references | RESEARCH REQUIRED |

**These are inspirations to research, not a decision to combine every style.** Research should establish, for each tradition, its characteristic palette, line, composition, treatment of figures and space, and depiction of night, forest, and the supernatural, and how well it suits these stories. Mixing styles without a rationale is to be avoided.

### Things to avoid

PROPOSED. These follow from the approved direction, but the user has not separately approved them.

- A generic "fantasy" look with Indian costume added on top.
- Clichéd or exoticised imagery that does not come from Indian traditions.
- Inconsistent style from one illustration to the next within a story.
- Images that contradict the text (wrong time of day, wrong number of characters, wrong setting).

## 4. Coherent art direction

**APPROVED (D-025)**

The project must eventually establish **one coherent art direction**. Within it, style and composition **serve each story's setting and emotional atmosphere**. A tale of court intrigue and a tale set in a cremation ground may differ in treatment and still clearly belong to the same work.

**OPEN (`Q-201`):** the final art style. **OPEN (`Q-202`):** how far the style may vary between stories.

## 5. Character consistency

**APPROVED (D-025)**

**Vikram, Betal, and any recurring characters must keep visual consistency** across all illustrations: face, build, costume, colouring, and characteristic posture or presence.

A character who appears in only one tale must still look consistent throughout that tale.

**PROPOSED (P-003):** before producing illustrations at scale, create a **character reference ("model sheet")** for each recurring character, approved by the user, and use it for every later illustration of that character. *How Betal should look is itself OPEN (`Q-203`). How the sources describe the Betal is RESEARCH REQUIRED as well.*

## 6. Scene relevance and atmosphere

**APPROVED (D-026)**

Each illustration must match the scene's:

- **time of day** (dusk, deep night, pre-dawn, and so on),
- **weather** (clear, rain, storm, mist),
- **physical setting** (forest, cremation ground, palace, village, riverbank),
- **emotional atmosphere** and **dramatic intensity**.

A rainy night in dense forest should look different from a clear, quiet night in the same forest. These visual changes should agree with the atmosphere of the reading experience as a whole (see [AUDIO_DIRECTION.md](AUDIO_DIRECTION.md) for sound), and they should happen at natural story moments.

## 7. Layout and composition

**OPEN**

- **Recorded user preference (PREF-001), not a decision:** the user currently likes text on the left with one or two relevant illustrations on the right, arranged with deliberate composition. No layout has been approved.
- The reference `https://visualsarkarsm.com/work-detail/118` is useful for illustrated storytelling and composition. **It is not a design to copy.** (D-031)
- Undecided: continuous scroll, page-turn, or a hybrid (`Q-101`); mobile behaviour (`Q-106`); typography and visual treatment (`Q-105`).
