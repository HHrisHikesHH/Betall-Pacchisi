# Source and Research Policy

This policy governs how the project identifies, compares, cites, and verifies its sources. It applies decisions D-016, D-017, and D-018 in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md). Where research sits in the story process is described in [STORY_WORKFLOW.md](STORY_WORKFLOW.md).

## 1. Core principle

**APPROVED (D-016)**

Betal Pachisi exists in several textual traditions. **Do not assume they are identical**, and do not treat the familiar modern version as the oldest or definitive one by default. The project's primary literary foundation will be chosen **only after** the traditions have been researched and compared.

### What to compare

For each tradition, and for each tale where it appears, record:

- **Story structure**: framing, order of tales, and how each tale opens and closes.
- **Plot details**: events, their sequence, and any that are added or missing.
- **Characterisation**: how characters are presented and what motivates them.
- **The riddle or dilemma**: how the Betal frames the question.
- **The conclusion**: Vikram's answer, its reasoning, and what follows.
- **Notable differences** from the other traditions, and whether they change the meaning.

## 3. Source hierarchy

**PROPOSED** until the user approves a ranking after research.

When sources conflict, give more weight to:

1. **Primary texts in their original language**, in a named critical or scholarly edition where one exists.
2. **Scholarly translations** of those texts, with the translator and edition identified.
3. **Peer-reviewed or recognised scholarly studies** of the traditions.
4. **Historical adaptations** (for example in Braj or Hindi), treated as traditions of their own and not as stand-ins for older texts.
5. **Modern popular retellings**, useful for understanding familiar versions but never authoritative on what the "original" says.
6. **Unattributed web content, summaries, and AI-generated answers.** These are never acceptable as evidence. They may only suggest leads to check.

Which tradition becomes the **primary foundation** for the project's retellings is **OPEN** (`Q-401`).

## 4. Citation and bibliographic requirements

**APPROVED (D-017)**

Every source actually consulted must be recorded with enough detail for someone else to find the same text. *The field list below is PROPOSED.*

- author, compiler, or translator,
- title, in the original script and transliterated where relevant,
- edition, editor, publisher, place, and year,
- the volume, chapter, tale number, page, or verse used,
- language, and whether the text was read in the original or in translation,
- how it was accessed (physical copy, a named digital library, and so on).

**Never fabricate a citation.** If a detail cannot be confirmed, write "unverified" and do not guess.

**PROPOSED (P-002):** keep one project bibliography with one entry per edition consulted, and cite entries from story notes by a short key.

## 5. Variant tracking

For each story, the research notes must include a **variant record** covering:

- which traditions contain the tale, and its number in each,
- the significant differences between them,
- which version or versions our retelling follows, and why,
- any points where we combine details from more than one tradition, stated explicitly.

## 6. Separating source from invention

**APPROVED (D-017)**

Creative freedom is approved (D-009), but **our additions must always be distinguishable from the source**. For each story, record:

- **Source events**: present in the sources, with citation.
- **Interpretive choices**: where the sources are ambiguous or conflict and we chose a reading.
- **Creative additions**: material that is ours, such as sensory detail, invented dialogue, inner thoughts, and atmosphere.

This record is internal. Whether any of it is shown to readers (for example as "about this tale" notes) is **OPEN** (`Q-404`).

## 7. Historical and textual verification

**APPROVED (D-017)**

Before any of the following appears in a final story or in reader-facing material, it must be verified against a cited source:

- claims about the age, authorship, or history of a text,
- historical, geographical, or cultural details presented as fact (place names, customs, rituals, titles, objects),
- claims about what a particular version "originally" says,
- any quotation.

Detail that is clearly invented atmosphere does not need a citation. It must still not contradict the setting, and it must be recorded as an addition (Section 6).

## 8. Copyright caution

*PROPOSED general guidance. The approved copyright requirement for Osho material is in §9 (D-018).*

- Older primary texts may be in the public domain, but **modern editions, translations, commentaries, and critical apparatus may still be protected**. Check before reproducing any of them. (RESEARCH REQUIRED, case by case.)
- Do not reproduce substantial passages of any modern translation or retelling in project content.
- Studying a protected work for structure and technique is different from copying its expression. The project does the former only.
- When in doubt, record the question and ask the user before using the material.

## 9. Researching Osho

**APPROVED (D-018)**

Osho's storytelling and discourses **may be researched** for:

- storytelling techniques,
- ways of layering interpretation,
- what makes narration effective and engaging.

The following are **not allowed**:

- impersonating Osho or writing "in his voice",
- attributing newly written passages, opinions, or interpretations to him,
- fabricating or paraphrasing-as-quotation anything he said,
- implying that he or his estate endorses this project.

The following are **required**:

- Keep **genuine discourse references**, cited to a specific discourse, book, or recording, clearly distinct from **our own independent interpretation**.
- **Check copyright and usage permissions** before reproducing any modern transcript, recording, translation, or other protected material. (RESEARCH REQUIRED.)

The aim is to learn what makes storytelling effective, not to copy anyone's identity or writing. This principle applies to every storyteller the project studies, not only Osho.

## 10. Use of AI in research

*The rule that AI output needs independent review is APPROVED (D-020). The specific practices below are PROPOSED.*

- AI tools may help find leads, summarise *verified* material, and draft comparison tables.
- AI output is **not evidence**. Every factual or textual claim must be traced to a cited source by a human or an independent verification step (see the verifier role in [STORY_WORKFLOW.md](STORY_WORKFLOW.md)).
- If an AI-produced claim cannot be traced to a source, drop it or mark it "unverified".
