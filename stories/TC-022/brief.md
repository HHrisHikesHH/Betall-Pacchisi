# TC-022 brief: The four brothers and the lion

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no (D-081 list). Verdict reasoning edited lightly (caste-weighted sin removed); see §2.

## 1. Source beats
1. In Brahmasthala (Dharaṇīvarāha's realm) the brahman Viṣṇusvāmin dies and his wife follows him; kinsmen seize the four sons' property. [KSS 12.29.3–7; her death shown as grief, ours]
2. The brothers beg to their maternal grandfather's village, Yajñasthala; the uncles shelter them, then grow contemptuous. [KSS 8–11]
3. The eldest tells how he tried to hang himself in a cremation ground; the rope broke; a stranger revived him and said suicide leads to hell. [KSS 12–23]
4. The others argue wealth is fickle and they should win a skill (*vijñāna*). [KSS 24–28]
5. They part in four directions, meet again: one can grow flesh on a bone, one skin and hair, one limbs, one give life. [KSS 29–35]
6. In a forest they find a lion's bone, not knowing what animal it is; each applies his skill in turn; the lion rises, kills all four, goes off satisfied. [KSS 36–41]
7. Narrator on fate and skill gained with effort. [KSS 42–44; may be cut as duplicating the riddle, ours]
8. Betal asks; Vikram, thinking he can fetch Betal back, answers. [KSS 45–50]

Variants: Ś 21 and Hindi XXI ask "who is the fool?" (*mūrkha*) not "who is guilty?"; same answer; maxim "intelligence is better than learning". Animal: lion (KSS, Ś), tiger (J, Hindi). Hindi: bones from a "Kanjar" (not used). J 19 unread.

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question: which of the four was guilty (*aparādhyati*) in making the lion that killed them? [KSS 12.29.45–46]
- Source answer and reasoning: the one who gave it life. The others acted in ignorance; he, seeing the lion's form, did it out of eagerness to show his science and "committed the brahman-murders". [KSS 48–50]
- Our answer: same answer (the life-giver); reasoning edited lightly: drop *brahmahatyā* as a worth-weighted sin (SLATE_AUDIT §5, §6 item 7), keep the vanity-of-skill reasoning. Not a D-041 dilemma change, but the removed phrase should be stated if the telling is questioned. Choice between "guilty" and "fool" is open (bucket §8); recommend "guilty" (KSS, our base), since "fool" turns a moral question into one of wisdom.
- Honest-difficulty: all four shared the plan, the forest and the risk; a reader may blame the one who first refused to ask what the bone was, or all four equally.

## 4. Charter points
- *brahmahatyā* (§3.2.5b, principle 7): the life-giver's fault is vanity, not a hierarchy of lives lost; no sin-weighing by birth.
- "Kanjar" label (§3.2.5b, §3.2.8): not used; no substitute community. Bones simply found.
- Mother's death (§3.4.3): grief, not sati-as-virtue.
- Suicide attempt (eldest): restraint; the stranger's warning is a character's view; do not make suicide a punchline or the rope's breaking a miracle.
- Contemptuous uncles: kept as relatives' neglect of orphans (§3.4.1); not generalised.
- Brothers' fault is one man's (§3.2.8, pattern §5.3: brahmans recur as vain in 008, 018, 019, 020).

## 5. Voice plan
- Hidden layer: a story where nobody does anything wrong until the very last step; heard slowly, about what you do when you know and do it anyway.
- "And you too": (a) at the bone: what have you helped shape without asking what it was; (b) at the life-giver: when did you act because someone was watching.
- Odd details: why they do not ask what the bone is (each trusts the next skill, pride in his own part); why show it at all (to prove their skills to each other, a poor man's need to be believed).
- Plot object: the bone; the sequence of four hands.
- Vikram's aside (KSS 47): he answers knowing it frees Betal; a gift for the frame (SLATE_AUDIT), fits D-056's growing recognition. Use only as source, no invented friendship (D-056 "Not invented").
- Callbacks: none; possibly a light link to TC-002 (revival) only if order puts them near; recap in one clause.
