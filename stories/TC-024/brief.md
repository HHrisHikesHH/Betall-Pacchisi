## 1. Source beats
1. The Vetāla, on the king's shoulder, announces "one great question". [KSS 12.31.1–4]
2. Dharma, a southern feudal king, his queen Candravatī (of Mālava) and their only daughter Lāvaṇyavatī; as the daughter comes of marriageable age, kinsmen combine and uproot him. [KSS 5–8]
3. He flees at night with wife and daughter toward her father in Mālava, and at dawn, on foot, reaches a forest settlement. The narrator (not a character) calls its men robbers shunned by the righteous. [KSS 9–14]
4. Many armed *śabara* run to rob him; he says "lest the *mlecchas* touch you, enter the forest"; the women flee into the trees; he kills many, and the whole village kills him alone. [KSS 15–20]
5. The women sit weeping, exhausted, by a lotus pond under an aśoka. [KSS 21–23]
6. Caṇḍasiṃha, hunting with his son Siṃhaparākrama, sees two lines of footprints. The father: take the woman you like. The son: the slender-footed is young and suits me, the large-footed suits you. The father objects (the son's mother has only just died); the son answers with a verse and swears him by his life. [KSS 24–34]
7. They find the women. The daughter (the only woman's speech in the tale) says the strangers are not thieves. The chief questions them; the queen, in shame and sorrow, tells her story (reported, not quoted). [KSS 35–47]
8. The chief, "thinking her ownerless", takes her in with sweet words; both are put on horses and taken home. "She too, as if in another birth, helplessly accepted him" (narrator). [KSS 48–50]
9. The footprints were inverted: the slender-footed is the mother, the large-footed the daughter. The son takes the mother, the father the daughter, to keep their word. Mother and daughter become mother-in-law and daughter-in-law. [KSS 51–54]
10. Both couples have children, and they have children. [KSS 55–56]
11. Betal's question: what are these children to one another? [KSS 57–59]
12. The king thinks it over in many ways, does not know (*nājñāsīt*), walks on in silence. [KSS 60]
13. Betal laughs inwardly, decides he cannot deceive this man further, praises his courage, gives the warning about the monk and the plan. [KSS 61–73, frame, see Order data]
Variants: Ś (Uhle prose XXIV) is a different story (kinsmen's raid, no *śabara*, the two children marry each other); Uhle's manuscript B XXV follows KSS. Hindi XXV: Bhils demand he lay down arms (no robbery stated), the father proposes the rule, the larger foot goes to the son, the queen goes to the prince. J 25: the king flees alive, two sons, and never says which man took which woman. Pairing and silence are fixed in KSS, Ś, Hindi; J differs.

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question (source): the children born on both sides, and their descendants, what are they to one another? (KSS 58–59).
- Source answer and reasoning: none. The king "did not know" and is silent (60); no reasoning recorded. Hindi: "through ignorance"; J: no fault in silence if one does not know; Ś prose: no reason stated.
- Our answer: same; no answer (D-041 not triggered). The derived relation (sources §6.3: each child is both half-uncle/aunt and half-nephew/niece of each child on the other side) is an AI derivation, not the text's; the tale must not state it as Betal's or Vikram's answer. Whether the question stays unanswerable after the women's treatment is for TC-024's own A3 (D-058 item 6 and frame blueprint §6).
- Honest-difficulty: a reader may think Vikram should have answered, or that the silence is tact or shame about the unions rather than ignorance; the text gives ignorance only (60, 62).

## 4. Charter points
- Coerced remarriage (§3.3.3, §3.3.6; D-058 item 6): mother and daughter taken in after the king's death, with an oath sworn between men over their feet. No speech of theirs ratifies it; no romance, escape or later life invented. KSS 50's second sentence is the narrator's rhetorical question, not the queen's speech (sources §1 item 4, which conflicts with SLATE_AUDIT §5 TC-024 and N1, which call it hers; the A1 reading governs).
- No invented "decisive moment" (§3.3.6; audit): Lāvaṇyavatī's consent is unrecorded and that silence stays felt; her one speech (38) is kept.
- *Śabara* (§3.2.5, 3.2.8): the audit says make them armed robbers or people with a reason, with no ethnic label (the narrator's hostile verse 14 and the king's *mleccha* word are not carried as narration); no community collectively villainous; the Hindi has Bhils demanding surrender instead (no robbery stated).
- The women's cost weighs on the silence through events (flight, dead king, the oath), without making the pairing comic irony (§3.5).
- Kin puzzle is not incest: the women are unrelated to the men (bucket §7); state relations precisely.
- Sensitivity reader recommended (audit): part of the frame climax.

## 5. Voice plan
- Hidden layer: the tale seems a comic word-puzzle; heard slowly, it is about a family broken by kinsmen, then bound by men's promise, and a question the reader cannot cleanly name.
- "And you too": (a) at the footprints, how easily we decide from a trace who a person is and what is owed; (b) at the question, the reader tries to name the relation and fails (device 10); (c) none after the silence.
- Odd details with a human motive: why a father who has just buried his wife follows a son's oath; why the son judges by foot size (a young man's presumption that misfires).
- Plot object: the two lines of footprints.
- Callbacks: none; the frame carries the hand-over (B15–B18). The tale must not carry the frame's irony.
