## 0. Reading conventions

**Evidence labels** (used in every table; never merged). The first five are those of [TC-001's A1](../../TC-001/research/sources.md) §0; the sixth is added here because KSS 12.31 uses it at the points that matter most.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **SRC** | The source text itself says it, in the narrator's voice or as plain narration of an event. |
| **NAR-U** | The narrator *reports* something (usually through a character) **without confirming** it. |
| **CHAR** | A character says, alleges or proposes it. Not a source fact about the world of the tale. |
| **INF** | The researcher's inference. Never a source fact. **DER** = an INF that is a strict logical consequence of cited SRC statements (used in the kinship table, §6). |
| **UNV** | Unverified, or not checked in this session. |
| **NAR-G** | The narrator's *general* or rhetorical remark (a maxim, or a rhetorical question), not a report of what a named person said or felt. |

## 1. Material findings in brief (findings, not decisions)

Everything here is argued in the sections cited. None of it changes a decision, the dossier's assessment or an open question.

1. **The marriage structure the riddle needs is fixed in four of the six texts read, and not stated in two.** KSS, Ś (Uhle's prose tale XXIV), the Hindi (Barker/Platts) and Uhle's anonymous recension f all pair **the son with the mother and the father with the daughter**. **J does not say which woman each man took**: it says the father took "the long-footed" and the son "the short-footed", but never says which of Candrāvatī and Vilāsavatī is which (Emeneau p. 144, Sanskrit; p. 145, translation). Emeneau's own note (p. 145 n. 7) says that "in the other versions" the long-footed is the daughter. **That note does not hold for the Hindi as read** (§5, §8): Barker gives the larger foot to the son, and the queen goes to the prince, so by the text's own terms the queen had the larger foot. **Uhle's manuscript B (verse XXV)** also does not say who took whom (§7.2). §6.4 gives the J consequence: the two possible pairings give **different** kin results. The dossier's "J omits the key marriage detail" is **confirmed and made exact**; it does not rest on a secondary report any more.
2. **Śivadāsa's tale is a different story from KSS in several respects, and "Ś 24" names more than one text.** Uhle's constituted prose tale XXIV (p. 61) has a different royal house (Pradyumna of Prabhāvatī; his daughter Candraprabhā married to Vijayabala of the south; **their daughter** Lāvaṇyavatī), a night raid by **kinsmen** and **no *śabara* or Bhils**, an unnamed king of Kusumapura, a father who proposes the footprint rule, and a **marriage of the two children to each other** (*ubhayor vivāhaḥ kṛtaḥ*). A **bracketed verse on women's chastity** follows the allocation (§7.1). Separately, **Uhle prints from manuscript B a metrical XXV** (pp. 64–66) that matches KSS closely (Dharma, Candravatī, Lāvaṇyavatī, a Bhilla village, *śabara*, Candrasiṃha) and that Uhle argues is **Kṣemendra's text transmitted under Śivadāsa's name** (Vorwort pp. XI–XII). The dossier's Ś row ("question; 'gave no reply'") is correct for the prose text; **Ś's narrative body is now read** (§7).
3. **The women's words in KSS are few and are not about the allocation.** The only direct speech by a woman in 12.31.1–75 is **the daughter's reassurance to her mother at 38**. The queen's account of herself is **reported, not quoted** (47). **No woman's consent, refusal, wish or reaction is narrated for either marriage** (51–54). The record of what the text does say about choice is in §4.5.
4. **KSS 12.31.50 is not the queen's own speech.** Its second half (*anāthā kṛcchrapatitā videśe strī karoti kim*) has no quotation marker and is a **NAR-G rhetorical question in the narrator's voice**. N1 quotes it as the queen's, and [SLATE_AUDIT.md](../../../docs/production/SLATE_AUDIT.md) §5 TC-024 says "Line 50 is already the queen's own speech in the source". **The passage governs** (protocol §A): that statement is not confirmed. For a Sanskritist (§11). *No existing file is edited here; the lead should record this.*
5. **Verse 50's object and 48's verb need care.** At 48 the narrator says that Caṇḍasiṃha, "thinking her ownerless" (*niḥsvāmikāṃ matvā*), pleased her with sweet words and *svīcakre* (made her his own / took her in). At 50 she "helplessly accepted *him*" (*avaśāṅgīcakāra tam*). **Both come before the allocation at 51–52, which then gives the queen to the son.** Who "him" is (the chief, or either man) is not stated. For a Sanskritist (§4.4).
6. **The footprint rule is stated differently in every witness, and the "inversion" is real only where the text gives the women's ages.** In KSS the **son** infers from the slender print that its owner is young and suits him (27–28), and the **narrator then states the opposite**: the slender-footed woman is the **mother**, the large-footed the **daughter** (51–52); the narrator names the result *pādaviparyāsa* ("inversion of the feet", 54). In Ś and the Hindi the **father** proposes the rule; in the Hindi the larger foot goes to the **son**; in J the father's rule is the larger foot to himself.
7. **The *śabara* are described by the narrator in one verse of hostile terms in KSS (14) and are not so described elsewhere.** Ś (prose) has none. The Hindi has Bhils who come while the king is away seeking food and demand he put down his arms (no robbery is stated). J has none (a Ceylon king conquers; the king flees alive); Emeneau's footnote on J's king gives only a partial account of the other versions (§9). Only in KSS does a king say "*mleccha*" (16, in his speech).
8. **How each witness hands over to the frame's conclusion differs, and the counts differ.** KSS: tale 5–56, question 57–59, silence 60, warning 61–75, conclusion in the next *taraṅga* (12.32). Ś (prose): XXIV is a **separate numbered tale** and XXV **is** the conclusion (Uhle pp. 61–63). Hindi: tale, warning, killing, boon and gods are all **inside XXV** (Barker pp. 360–369). **J 25: the conclusion is inside the numbered 25th story** (Emeneau pp. 143–155, colophon p. 154), so J's count is **24 tales + (the riddle and the conclusion in one numbered story)**, not "25 + conclusion" as the dossier §6 puts it (§10). Evidence only; nothing is decided.
9. **The king's silence is given a different stated reason or none in each witness.** KSS: he "did not know" (60) and the Vetāla thinks so (62). Ś prose: no reason; the Vetāla's next words are "many times I have deceived you" (*bahuvārair mayā tvaṃ vañcito 'si*, p. 61 l. 37; reading UNV). Hindi: "ignorant" (Barker p. 364); "through ignorance" (Platts p. 179). **J: the king reasons that there is no fault in giving no answer when one does not know** (Emeneau p. 144). Uhle's apparatus records manuscripts in which **the king answers aloud "I do not know this relationship"** (p. 204). **J has the king already resolved to keep silent before the riddle is put** (p. 143).
10. **Coverage.** Not read: Kṣemendra's text, Ryder, Tawney's translation (one download truncated; not needed), the manuscripts behind Uhle's and Emeneau's apparatus beyond what they print, Platts on page images, Barker pp. 365–369 in Devanagari (§3, §11).

## 4. KSS 12.31.1–75, verse by verse (re-read this session)

AI reading of the GRETIL e-text; labels as in §0. Verse numbers are the e-text's. Prior N1 events are marked "N1 agrees" where my reading confirms them.

### 4.1 The frame lead-in (1–4)

The king returns to the *śiṃśapā* tree, shoulders the Vetāla, and sets out (1–3). The Vetāla says he is *udvigna* and announces "this one great question" (*mahāpraśnam*, 4). *aham udvigno na punas tvaṃ gatāgataiḥ* (4ab): **the construction (who is wearied by the comings and goings) is UNV**; N1 recorded only *mahāpraśna*.

### 4.2 The flight and the *śabara* attack (5–20)

| 20 | When the "robber army" (*dasyusainya*) has gone with the ornaments, the queen sees from a thicket that her husband is dead | SRC |

**How exactly the *śabara* are described (the task's point 5).** The terms used are: *bhilla* (13), *śabara* (15, 18), *dasyu* ("robber", 20, in *dasyusainya*), and *mleccha* **only in the king's speech** (16). The **narrator's characterisation of the place and its men is in 14** (men who take lives and property; shunned by the righteous; like Yama's city). They act as a collective: "many … ran to rob" (15), "the whole village, commanded by its lord" (19). **No *śabara* speaks; none is individually named or described; no motive is given other than robbing a richly dressed traveller (15).** The text does not say they pursued the women. N1 and the audit's "*śabara* (Bhils, a forest people) kill the deposed king as faceless 'savages'" is consistent with this reading; "faceless" and "savages" are the audit's characterisation. **The portrayal question is an A2 and A3 matter** (the audit's "drop the ethnic label" is a proposal, not a source fact).

### 4.3 The women's flight and the lake (21–23)

The queen and daughter flee far in confusion (*vihvalā*, 21), and at midday sit **by a lotus pond under an *aśoka* tree, grieving, weeping and tired** (22–23). SRC. N1 agrees.

### 4.4 The footprint dialogue (24–34)

**N1 agrees**: the father tells the son to follow and take the woman he likes; the son claims the small-footed; the father objects that his wife has just died; the son presses him with a verse and makes him swear. **Added precision:** (a) the *son* assigns the footprints (the small-footed is his; the large-footed his father's), not a rule laid down by the father; (b) the oath is by the son's life and is a demand that the father accept "the second" woman; (c) **no woman is present, named or consulted in this exchange** (SRC: she has not been seen).

**What the text does and does not say** (SRC unless marked).
- **Said:** the daughter speaks once (38); the queen tells her story (47, content not given); the chief's thought is that the queen has no owner (48); **the queen "helplessly accepts him"** (50), in the narrator's voice, and the narrator generalises about a woman in her position (50, NAR-G).
- **Not said:** that either woman **chose, wished for, refused or objected to any marriage**; that either woman was **asked**, at 48–52, whether she would marry; **any word of Lāvaṇyavatī's after 38**, or any report of her feelings after 36–38; any reaction of either woman to the allocation (51–54), which is narrated entirely with the men as subjects (*vyadhāt*, 51; *cakāra*, 52). **The text does not say "she was forced"; it also never says she consented.** *Consent is neither narrated nor denied; the word the text uses for the queen's state is *avaśā* ("without will/power over herself", "helpless"), and for the allocation it narrates only the men's agreement* (53).
- **Open to a Sanskritist:** the sense of *svīcakre* (48); the referent of *tam* (50): the nearest masculine antecedent is Caṇḍasiṃha (48), **but the verse comes right before the son takes the queen (51) and does not say which man is meant**; whether *sāpi* ("she too") points back to the queen or to the pair; whether *avaśā* denotes compulsion or only helplessness; *kṛcchrapatitā* ("fallen into distress"). **Whether 50 expresses coercion in any sense the A3 reviewers will apply to D-058 item 6 is not an A1 question.**
- **Also relevant (INF, not source fact):** the pairing is decided between the men before the women are seen (26–34) and the narrator gives it as a binding agreement of the men (53). That the women's lack of choice is "central to events" (dossier §5) is an inference, and a reasonable one; the text itself gives only the facts above.

### 6.3 The children (DER)

Let **a** be any child of S and C (the "son–mother" couple) and **b** any child of P and L (the "father–daughter" couple). The riddle (58) asks about **a** and **b** and, by *kramāt*, their descendants. (No sex or number is given, 55; the relations below hold for sons and daughters alike, with the sex-appropriate term.)

| Of… | **a** (child of S + C) is… | Derivation | **b** (child of P + L) is… | Derivation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| their parents | child of S and C | 55 | child of P and L | 55 |
| **L** | **half-sibling of L** (b: same mother C; L's father is D, a's is S) | 7, 55 | **child of L** | 55 |
| **P** | **grandchild of P** (b: S's child) **and brother-in-law of P** (half-brother of P's wife L) | 55, 25; 52, 7 | **child of P** | 55 |
| **S** | child of S | 55 | **half-sibling of S** (b: same father P; S's mother was M₀, b's mother is L) | 25, 29, 55 |
| **C** | child of C | 55 | **grandchild of C** (child of C's daughter L) **and sister-in-law of C** (half-sibling of C's husband S) | 7, 55; 25, 29 |

**a to b, and b to a (the answer to the task's "what the two sets of children are to each other"):**

- **a is b's half-uncle/half-aunt** (a is a half-sibling of b's mother L: same mother C) **and also b's half-nephew/half-niece** (a's father S is a half-sibling of b: same father P). *b* stands to *a* in the **same two relations** (a's father S is b's half-brother, so b is a's half-uncle; b's mother L is a's half-sister, so b is a's half-nephew).
- So **each child is both half-uncle/aunt and half-nephew/niece of each child of the other couple, by blood, in the same direction on both sides.** They are **not** siblings (no common parent) and **not** cousins (no parent of one is a sibling of a parent of the other).
- **In marriage terms** the two sets are also related through: C (mother of a) is b's grandmother and sister-in-law; S (father of a) is b's half-brother and **step-grandfather** (stepfather of b's mother L); L (mother of b) is a's half-sister and **step-grandmother** (wife of a's paternal grandfather P); P (father of b) is a's grandfather and half-sister's husband.
- **Further generations** (*kramāt*, 58) multiply the same overlap. **They are not enumerated here.**
- The text states none of this (55–59) and gives **no answer** (60).

**What the Hindi says about the women and consent.** No woman speaks, is named, is asked or reacts in the allocation; the women's only reported actions are weeping and beating their breasts (p. 362) and sitting and thinking (p. 362); the men seat each of "his" woman on his horse (p. 364). **The text does not say the women agreed, and does not say they were forced.** The "prize" word is Barker's English (*prize*, free translation); the Hindi is **"conformably to the agreement"** (*muvāfiq karār ke*). **Barker's free translation reads "children of the king and prince"; the Hindi says *laṛkoṃ*** (UNV: "sons" or "children"); Platts has "the children of these two".

**Barker's note 300 (p. 364)**, an editor's comment, says that by the rule on p. 80 the Baitāl returns to the tree whenever the king speaks, and that "on this occasion he remained silent, we should think to the no small relief of the reader." This is **Barker's remark, not the Hindi text.**
