# TC-018 brief: The lost science (Candrasvāmin)

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no (D-081)

## 1. Source beats
Base: KSS 18 (12.25.1–87). Evidence: SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE TC-018.
0. Frame episode: at the tree Vikram finds many identical corpses, each possessed by a Vetāla; he resolves to enter the fire if the night fails, and the Vetāla withdraws the illusion. [source: KSS 3–7] Frame-owned; use only if it serves Vikram's fear (NARRATOR_VOICE #6). Open for the coordinator.
1. Ujjayinī: Candrasvāmin, learned son of the minister-brahman Devasvāmin, is addicted to gambling; he loses everything, is beaten and left for dead. [source: KSS 13–25]
2. Ashamed, he shelters in an empty Śiva temple. A *mahāvratī* finds him; he refuses a beggar's food "as a brahman"; the ascetic summons a science that creates a golden city and women each night, vanishing at dawn. [source: KSS 26–47] **Ours:** drop the nightly woman-as-amenity; keep the purity refusal. [ours, SLATE_AUDIT §5]
3. He begs to be taught. Rules, stated in advance: recite under water, live an illusory life; roused at twenty-four, he must remember and still enter a fire; an unworthy pupil costs the teacher his own science. [source: KSS 48–58]
4. In the river he lives another life: birth, marriage, children. Awakened, he faces the pyre, sees parents, wife, children weeping, wavers, decides to enter. [source: KSS 59–75]
5. The fire feels cold as snow; he comes out of the river. [source: KSS 76]
6. The teacher, puzzled, suspects a fault; the science appears to neither; both leave dejected. [source: KSS 77–81]
7. Betal asks. [source: KSS 82–83]

Variants: Ś 17: a yakṣiṇī does not come; verdict "double-minded" (*dvidhācitta*); closing *karma* verse. Hindi XVII adds fate. J 15 unread (Emeneau calls J's riddle solutions careless). *Vikalpa* may mean doubt, hesitation or alternative thought.

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question: why was the science lost to both, though the rite was done as instructed? (KSS 12.25.83)
- Source answer: not an act merely correct in form; it needs a steady mind free of wavering (*vikalpa*), of pure resolve. This young brahman wavered even after being awakened; the teacher lost his science by giving it to an unworthy recipient (85–86).
- Our answer: same. Avoid Ś's and Hindi's fate/karma close (SLATE_AUDIT §5). "Unworthy" means unsteady, never low-born.
- Honest-difficulty: wavering before one's weeping family, even an illusory one, can seem human or right, and a rule that demands it be refused is arguably unfair. Not a reversal that makes hesitation correct.

## 3. Locked directions
None in D-048, D-051 or D-055 to D-061. Approved audit (SLATE_AUDIT §5): "his identity and the refusal stay visible... the tale notices that without a sermon." "the verdict stays defensible: the rule was stated in advance and he accepted it. *Not* a reversal that makes hesitation the right choice."

## 4. Charter points
- Purity refusal of beggar's food is connected to the plot: keep it, visible as pride that delivers him to the ascetic (§3.2.5a); no collective judgement of brahmans (§3.2.8).
- "Unworthy" never birth-based (§3.2.2).
- Ascetic unmarked by sect, like the frame's (D-055 R1 logic; §3.2.8, SLATE_AUDIT §5.3): no *Pāśupata* label.
- Illusory wife and children given reality, not decoration (§3.3.2); no women as nightly amenity.
- Gambling beating shown as harm without relish (§3.4.1).
- Verdict as maxim is a lecture risk (§3.5): the cold fire does the work.

## 5. Voice plan
- Hidden layer: "a life lived in an instant, and the whole thing turns on one breath."
- "And you too": (a) at the wavering, the thing you refused to give up when it counted; (b) after the cold fire, the instructions you followed to the letter that failed.
- Odd details: why the ascetic offers help (he needs a worthy pupil); why the rules are stated in advance; why he insists (shame and hunger).
- Plot object: the fire, which feels cold only when the choice is already lost or won.
- Callbacks: none.
