# TC-015 brief: The magic pill (Manaḥsvāmin and Śaśiprabhā)

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no per D-081's list, but see §2 (D-041 conflict if the answer is composed)

## 1. Source beats
Base: KSS 15 (12.22.1–115). Evidence: SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE TC-015; N3 §3.1 (J 13 read in full).
1. Śivapura: Princess Śaśiprabhā and the young brahman Manaḥsvāmin fall in love at a spring festival. [source: KSS 3–13]
2. A mad elephant breaks loose; he carries her to safety. She pines for him and does not know his name. [source: KSS 13–19]
3. He goes to his teacher, the master-rogue Mūladeva, who is always with his friend Śaśin. Mūladeva takes a pill and becomes an old brahman; he gives Manaḥsvāmin a pill that turns him into a girl. [source: KSS 20–26]
4. The "old brahman" asks the king to guard his "son's betrothed"; fearing a brahman's curse, the king places the "girl" with the princess. [source: KSS 27–33]
5. She confides her lovesickness; he reveals himself; they marry by the *gāndharva* rite; girl by day, man by night. Her shame and wonder at the revelation are recorded. [source: KSS 34–56, 54]
6. At her cousin Mṛgāṅkavatī's wedding the minister's son falls lovesick for the "girl"; the king, to save the minister, gives her to him. [source: KSS 57–74]
7. She sets a six-month delay; he goes on pilgrimage. Manaḥsvāmin tells Mṛgāṅkavatī the Ilā and Budha tale, reveals himself and seduces her; they flee before the husband returns. Her fate is never told. [source: KSS 75–99]
8. Mūladeva returns as the old brahman with Śaśin as the "son" and demands his daughter-in-law; the king, afraid of the curse, gives Śaśiprabhā to Śaśin with proper rites. [source: KSS 100–105]
9. At Mūladeva's house the two men dispute: first marriage by the teacher's favour, or the father's gift before the fire. [source: KSS 106–110]
10. Betal asks. [source: KSS 111]
May alter (audit): the elephant rescue, the Ilā sub-tale, the length. [ours]

Variants: Ś 14, Hindi XIV: she is pregnant by the lover, the child will perform Śaśin's rites; Ś reasons by deceit (*chadmanā praviṣṭaḥ*) (OCR, Q&A only). J 13: lover a bard's son, charm not pill, Śaśideva, minister's son Vijayasena, same answer, "forbidden in this world and the next" (Tr p. 87).

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question: whose wife is she properly (*kasyopapadyate bhāryā sā*)? (KSS 12.22.111)
- Source answer: Śaśin's, since her father gave her openly by the proper rite; Manaḥsvāmin took her by stealth through the *gāndharva* form, and "a thief can never have rightful ownership of what belongs to another" (113–114). Her wishes are never consulted.
- Our answer: PROPOSED, needs the user's approval. SLATE_AUDIT §5 does not pre-pick it. Recommendation: keep the answer (Śaśin; same in KSS, Ś, Hindi, J) but replace the property reasoning with Ś's attested deceit reasoning, so the reasoning is attested, not ours. That fixes Vikram's verdict but not the problem: it overrides the one consent she gave. Any other answer, or reasoning of our own, is a D-041 composition. **Conflict:** D-081 excludes TC-015 from the user's look, yet the audit says a changed answer goes to the user; resolve before drafting.
- Honest-difficulty: both claims rest on Mūladeva's fraud; a reader can defensibly say Manaḥsvāmin (her only chosen union) or neither.

## 4. Charter points
- Property verdict: the "thief of property" reasoning treats a woman as property (§3.3.2); replace it.
- Consent per D-051; no laundering of deception into choice (§3.3.6); do not write "she chooses to run" for Mṛgāṅkavatī.
- Seduction of Mṛgāṅkavatī by deception: shown as harm, not farce (§3.3.3).
- King's curse-fear is his weakness, not legitimate brahman power (§3.2.1, 3.2.6, SLATE_AUDIT caste note); the brahman label is otherwise an idle tag (J 13: bard's son), decided by the pattern audit (§3.2.5c).
- Mūladeva's trickery must carry visible costs (§3.4.4).
- Keep the Ś pregnancy out: it adds a child as ritual device.
