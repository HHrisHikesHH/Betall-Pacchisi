# FRAME brief: The night of the corpse (the Vikram–Betal frame)

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no (D-081). Flags for the user's attention are in §2 ("not yet approved").

## 1. Source beats
Base: KSS 12.8 and 12.31–32 (Somadeva; Sanskrit read by AI only, no specialist). Beat IDs are blueprint.md (rev. 2, D-058 corrections applied).

**A. Court to first tale**
1. B01 An ordinary court day: a mendicant brings one fruit daily, the king hands it unlooked-at to the treasurer; ten years. [KSS 12.8.21–27]
2. B02 A pet monkey breaks a fruit, a gem falls out; the store has rotted fruit and heaped gems; the king rewards the treasurer. [KSS 12.8.28–33]
3. B03 The king refuses more until told the reason. [KSS 12.8.34]
4. B04 The man asks for a brave helper for a rite (*mantra-sādhana*), undescribed; the king agrees at once. [KSS 12.8.35–36]
5. B05 One appointed night: the dark-fortnight fourteenth, great cremation ground, under a banyan. [KSS 12.8.37–39]
6. B06 The king goes out alone at dusk, dark clothes, sword. [KSS 12.8.40–41]
7. B07 The cremation ground; the man drawing a diagram. No other human figure added (D-058 item 5). [KSS 12.8.42–46]
8. B08 He is told to fetch a hanged corpse from the *śiṃśapā* tree to the south. No terms, no silence rule, no danger named. [KSS 12.8.47–48]
9. B09 The corpse cries, then laughs; the king speaks to it ("Why do you laugh? Come, let us go"); as he speaks it is back on the tree. Causation neither stated nor denied. [KSS 12.8.49–55]
10. B10 The king climbs, carries it silently (the silence is his own; KSS states no rule). [KSS 12.8.56–57]
11. B10a Frame-only loop: Betal provokes the king to speak; he speaks; Betal returns to the tree; the king retrieves him. No tale, not one of the 25. [ours, D-057A; purpose from J p. 13]
12. B10b On the next carry Betal says he will tell a tale to ease the road. [KSS 12.8.58]
13. B11 (when a tale ships first) the question; the penalty is stated with it; the king answers; Betal departs; the king fetches him again. [KSS 12.8.188–196, 12.9.1; "returns to tree" is our staging]
14. B12–B14 Movement families, elided middle. [ours; no seeds about the monk, D-058 item 1]

**B. Ending (after TC-024)**
15. B15–B16 Betal announces "one great question"; after TC-024 the penalty is recalled (or stated, if TC-024 is first shipped); the king does not know (*nājñāsīt*), walks on in silence. [KSS 12.31.4, 57–60]
16. B17–B18 The loop breaks: Betal does not return; the king is glad; Betal laughs privately. [KSS 12.31.61–62]
17. B19 Betal's warning: the man means to sacrifice the king; the ruse (prostration, beheading) and a prize. All on Betal's word only. [KSS 12.31.65–73]
18. B20–B21 Betal leaves the body; the king goes to the man as to an enemy. [KSS 12.31.74–75]
19. B22–B24 The rite staged with restraint; Betal summoned into the body; the man tells the king to prostrate. [KSS 12.32.1–16]
20. B25–B27 The king says he does not know how; the man prostrates; the king beheads him, tears out the heart, gives head and heart to Betal. Betal's instruction and the king's own acts are kept distinct (D-058 item 3). [KSS 12.32.17–19]
21. B28–B31 Betal acknowledges the trouble, offers a boon; the king asks that the tales be famous; Betal adds a blessing for hearers; he departs by his own power. [KSS 12.32.20–30]
22. B32–B35 No divine coda; dawn; the king returns alone; no sovereignty shown; last image left to drafting. [KSS 12.32.31–41, reduced; R6]

Variants: Ś, J, H give the villain a sect or trade, a prehistory for Betal (oilman, potter), and a servitude ending; none is used.

## 2. Frame mechanics
- **First loop (D-057A):** B09 → B10 → B10a → B10b, then the first tale. Shown by use; no rule stated at the opening. Blueprint assumes B10a is permanent (G14); user has not confirmed.
- **Where the frame ends for the first release (D-054):** "a release must include the frame, completed through its ending, and TC-024". Minimum cut: B09, B10, B10a, B10b, B15 (fused with end of B10b), TC-024, B16, then B17–B35.
- **Penalty placement (D-057B):** with the first shipped tale's question; if TC-024 is alone, with its question, before the king falls silent. Draft it as its own short segment (G15), since it migrates when a tale ships earlier.
- **Our answer to Betal's ending:** the source has no riddle here; the frame's moral question is the killing, left open (D-055 R3).
- **Not yet approved (our composition):** B10a's staging and its permanence; the staged form of B09's non-causal return; the rite's kit and menace (B22); the blessing's tone (B30); the closing image (B35); the release-invariant wording of B15, B19, B28, B29 (G17). Voice A's closing line ("माँग छोटी नहीं होती") stays: the source does have him make a big request (B04), so it is supported, not the invented foreshadowing D-058 item 1 bars. (Coordinator correction, 2026-11-27.)
- **Honest-difficulty:** a reader may think the king right to kill a man who plotted his death, or wrong to kill a host on a spirit's word alone.

## 4. Charter points
- Ascetic by function only, no sect marker or tantrik/Kāpālika imagery (3.2.5); his act is not a community's character (3.2.8).
- The killing is not a triumph (3.4.3, 3.4.4; principle 6 applied to Vikram): no applause, no throne.
- Betal is not the charter's mouthpiece; no servitude or liberation (3.4.3).
- Treasurer and every incidental person are people, not scenery (3.4.1); none invented for the ground.
- No woman added; no coerced remarriage as irony (3.3.6).
- Debt-as-loyalty is examined, not assumed (3.4.5).

## 5. Voice plan
- Hidden layer (promise at the start): heard fast it is a ghost story, heard slowly it is about what we stop looking at (voice-test A).
- "And you too" turns: (1) the daily fruit nobody opens (B02); (2) the king who never asked why (B01/B03); (3) fear at the cremation ground, people hum when afraid (B07, device 8).
- Odd details needing a motive: why the treasurer kept, not threw away, the fruit; why the king never asked; why he says yes blind.
- Plot object: the fruit; the corpse as burden, answer-and-fetch.
- Callbacks: none in the frame source. Call-forward candidates: B09's laugh (to B18); B10a (to B17).
