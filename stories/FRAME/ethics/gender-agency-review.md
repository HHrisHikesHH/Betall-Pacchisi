# A3 ethics review B: gender, agency and representation (FRAME blueprint)

Reviewer B | model Sonnet 5.5 | **AI review only, not human review** | 2026-11-21

Scope: charter §3.3/§3.4/§3.6, blueprint, approval packet, A1 research, D-043/053/054/055. Assumptions: R1-R8 are decided; TC-024's tale text was not available to me, so claims about it rest on the blueprint's own summaries. I recommend reopening none of R1-R8.

## Findings (most severe first)

**1. MAJOR | B15-B18, §6 | ethical risk**
Problem: the frame's one gender-relevant event, TC-024's coerced remarriage (KSS 12.31.50), appears only as an "echo" in §6. The blueprint does not say whether the unanswered question is moral (about her situation) or a kinship puzzle. B17 then plays the king's ignorance as "glad", "the sharpest irony... played straight". If the question is a kinship puzzle, the woman's coercion is backdrop to a game and charter §3.3.2/§3.3.6 (woman as device; coercion visible) is at risk. If it is moral, the king's gladness sits on top of a coerced woman's fate. The §6 flag for the TC-024 session covers only the forest robbers, not this.
Opposing: the source states ignorance, not a moral stance, and adding commentary would breach "storytelling, not lecture".
Remedy: add a B16-B17 drafting constraint: the frame's tone does not treat the content of the final question as comic or merely mechanical; no beat resolves or comments on the coercion; the TC-024 session decides whether the question centres her. Specialist: Hindi literary reader plus a gender-studies sensitivity reader, after reading the tale.

**2. MAJOR | §12.1, §8.4 row "Women in the frame" | narrative weakness / process gap**
Problem: proposal §8.4 says "TC-024's women weigh on the climax through events". No beat B15-B18 carries any such event; all of it is Vikram and Betal. The §12.1 test table has no gender row at all, although charter §3.3 binds the frame (§3.1). The promised mitigation exists on paper only.
Opposing: §12.5 and the tale files carry gender; the frame need not.
Remedy: add a §3.3 row to §12.1 stating plainly that the frame's answer is absence plus tale-level handling, and delete or qualify the "weigh through events" claim. Specialist: none to fix the paperwork; a human to judge sufficiency.

**3. MODERATE | §1.5 "Women", R-list | ethical risk / process**
Problem: the all-male frame is the most consequential gender choice and was never put to the user as an R-question; it rode in on D-043's "working base". The user's D-055 approval of R1-R8 therefore does not cover it.
Would inserting a woman be justified? No. KSS has none; one night, one cremation ground, three male parties. Any woman would be ADAPT with no causal function in the chain of §1.2, which is the definition of a token. The opposing case (absence is itself a message, and "no women" can be defended only by fidelity) has force, but fidelity is the better ground than representation.
Remedy: record in DECISIONS.md that the absence is a decided fidelity-based choice, with the reasons above, so it is not silently inherited. Specialist: human reviewer to confirm.

**4. MODERATE | §4.1, B12, B14 | ethical risk**
Problem: the frame's mitigation for male-only judging is that verdicts are "judgments under pressure" and, later, "strategic" (B14). Applied to tales about women, this makes their situations stakes in a survival game: the king answers to save his head, not on the merits. B12 also plays his fast, sure answers as competence, which rewards confident verdicts exactly where the collection is most likely to have gendered ones.
Opposing: undermining the king's authority is the charter's goal (D-041).
Remedy: in the Contract movement the king's confidence must not be validated on tales about women; Betal's objections stay at full strength there. Specialist: sensitivity reader on tale-level pairings once Q-413 is settled.

**5. MODERATE | §4.1, B04 | ethical risk**
Problem: "three coercions (debt, penalty, steel), none clean" and the king "consents blind, out of obligation" give Vikram the frame's strongest sympathy as a constrained party. Beside a tale of a woman's coerced remarriage this can flatten the difference between obligation and forced marriage and recast the powerful man as the pressured one (§3.3.6; §3.4.4).
Opposing: the parallel is unstated, as §3.5 requires.
Remedy: keep the king's pressures social and consequential, not physical or sexual in vocabulary; avoid "forced", "no choice" language. Specialist: Hindi register check.

**6. MODERATE | §4.3 "Rules for the middle", B14, §5.3 | source-fidelity / honest-difficulty**
Problem: R3 promises uncertainty without invented evidence, but "Betal never lies about the rules" and B14's "deniable oddities" are ADAPT evidence of Betal's reliability and the monk's purpose, built to make the warning credible. They tilt toward justification, while B22's "unforced warmth" tilts the other way. A reader can question the killing (B25 lie, B27 heart, no dying words, the debt irony), so the test mostly passes, but the structure still rewards him (he lives; B33 "no one asks").
Opposing: without seeds the warning is unfair, and §12.5(4) records this.
Remedy: balance seeds with equally deniable counter-seeds, or cut the "never lies" rule to "keeps the penalty". Specialist: none; an editorial call.

**7. MODERATE | B07, B13, §12.3 | ethical risk**
Problem: "burning-ground people seen in passing" are caste-coded (the blueprint says so) and unspecified by gender. The easy way to add women to an otherwise male frame is here, as atmosphere or the stock witch/mourner. That would be the tokenism this review rejects, with caste-coding on top.
Opposing: the blueprint already says "cut them if you cannot do it without a marker".
Remedy: state that no unnamed woman is added as scenery or as dread. Specialist: caste and gender sensitivity reader.

**8. MINOR | §1.5, §1.4 (R8) | narrative weakness**
Problem: Hindi forces a grammatical gender on the treasurer, monkey, mendicant, hanged man and passers-by. The blueprint is in neutral English, so the default will be male by silent grammar, not decision. The treasurer is "Incidental, replaceable" (§3.6), so the gender is a free parameter that changes no plot.
Opposing: changing it for representation alone is the pattern the brief warns against.
Remedy: record the choice for each incidental role instead of inheriting it. Specialist: Hindi literary reader.

**9. MINOR | B33, B35 | subjective preference**
Problem: the last page is the king remembering his own request, so the collection closes in the ruler's perspective with no counter-voice, and B33's "no one asks" means no character, including anyone in his household, can hold him to account (§3.4.4). Accountability rests on the reader.
Opposing: the open ending is the R3 decision.
Remedy: none required; flag for the drafting session. Specialist: none.

## What I could not establish
- Whether TC-024's final question is moral or genealogical, and what KSS 12.31.1-56 says about the woman's agency; A1 read only 12.31.50 onward.
- How Hindi readers associate the opening fruit motif with the Bhartrihari story and the queen (proposal §8.3 says unverified).
- Whether the blueprint's gender handling is sufficient. Only a qualified human specialist (Hindi literary reader, gender and caste sensitivity reader, Sanskritist) can say; this review cannot.

## Honest dissent from the author's choices
- The blueprint treats "no token woman" as settled and its checks omit gender entirely; I think the absence deserves a recorded decision and its own test row.
- I disagree that "judgment under pressure" mitigates gender risk; it may worsen it for tales about women (finding 4).
- B14's seeds and the "never lies" rule contradict the claim that no evidence is invented.
- I agree with the author on not inserting a woman and on the open R3 ending.

## Verdict
The frame's absence of women is faithful to the source and inserting one would be tokenistic, so I support the blueprint on that point. It does not handle the absence well. Its checks have no gender test, the promised "women weigh through events" has no beat to carry it, and the climax's only link to a coerced woman is an unexamined echo played for irony. The honest-difficulty test mostly passes on Vikram's killing, but B14's seeds and the "never lies" rule manufacture a little evidence toward justification. Fix findings 1, 2 and 6 before drafting, record finding 3, and obtain human specialist review before approval. None of this requires reopening R1-R8.
