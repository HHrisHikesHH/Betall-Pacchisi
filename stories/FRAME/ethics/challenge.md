## Findings (most severe first)

### 1. BLOCKER: the spine does not work in the D-054 minimum release
**Where:** B10, B11, B15, B17, B19, §5.3, §11.2, §11.4, §13 G1. **Type:** narrative weakness; source fidelity.
**Problem:** the mitigation in §11.2 is not written into B10. §11.4 points to §11.2, and §11.2 points to a B10 that does not contain it. With only TC-024 shipped, B15 ("not *another* tale"), B17 ("the loop breaks"), B19 ("obstructions *for so long*") and §5.3 ("kept his terms *all night*") each assume earlier loops the reader has not seen. The beats cannot be drafted until this is settled. **Opposing argument:** D-054 sets a minimum, not an expected release, so this case may never ship. **Remedy:** choose one of the options in the minimum-release section below and write it into the spine before drafting begins. **Specialist:** a Hindi literary reader, to judge whether a one-tale frame reads as complete.

### 2. MAJOR: "Betal has kept his terms" is a convenient invention that contradicts the source and the blueprint
**Where:** §5.3, §4.3 (rule 2), §1.1. **Type:** source-fidelity error; narrative weakness.
**Problem:** in KSS, Betal's own reasoning (12.31.61–64) is that he can no longer go on *deceiving* the king, and he says the night's obstacles were deliberate (12.31.73). Ś's "deceived you many times" (OCR, provisional) has the same shape. The blueprint's §1.1 itself says "Betal, who has deceived him all night". §5.3's evidence is also empty: the penalty "applied the same way each time" was never applied, because the king always answered, so the king has no proof it is real. The blueprint says the frame's emotional logic leans on this invention "hardest", and it is the weakest support in the design. **Opposing argument:** an adversary who follows the rules is a familiar and believable reason to trust one. **Remedy:** rest the trust on what the source actually provides. (a) The warning makes predictions the king can check before he acts: the monk calls Betal into the corpse, honours him, and asks for the prostration (B23–B24 match 12.31.67–70). The blueprint undercounts this ("matches at one point"). The king acts only after three predictions come true, and none of them confirms the lethal part. That is a sharper, honest irony. (b) Betal pays a visible price: he gives up the return to the tree. Delete rule 2 of §4.3, or mark it as not load-bearing, and correct §1.1 or §5.3 so they agree. **Specialist:** a Sanskritist, on the scope of the "deceiving" in 12.31.61–64.

### 3. MAJOR: "laugh only" (R2 sub-question) plus §5.3 makes Betal read as a benevolent protector
**Where:** R2, B18, B19, §1.4. **Type:** ethical risk (charter §3.5, the mouthpiece rule); source fidelity.
**Problem:** with the narration withholding Betal's mind, the reader hears only what Betal says aloud: admiration (12.31.66) and "I obstructed you for this" (12.31.73). Both flatter him. The self-interested part of his motive, *tricking the monk*, appears only in the private reasoning that R2 withholds. The decision meant to keep him unknowable therefore removes the source's own evidence against his benevolence. **Opposing argument:** unverifiable speech is exactly what keeps him a trickster, and readers distrust tricksters by default. **Remedy, within R2:** make the cornered-trickster logic visible through events. Because the king did not know, he stays silent; because he stays silent, Betal has no way back to the tree and is being carried to a man who can summon him by spells (12.32.12, shown at B23). Pace B16–B18 so the reader feels Betal has run out of moves before he laughs. **Otherwise I recommend the user REOPEN the R2 sub-question** and allow one compact private beat from 12.31.63–64, the deceiving and the thwarting only, without the admiration. **Specialist:** a Sanskritist on 12.31.63 (A1 D4).

### 4. MAJOR: the ending vindicates Vikram by placement
**Where:** B28, B29 ("the thematic turn"), B34, B35. **Type:** ethical risk (charter principle 6, D-043).
**Problem:** R6 cut the gods because "approval by placement" is still approval. The same mechanism now works through the boon. Betal promises sovereignty, the king asks for the tales, sovereignty is never shown, and the last image is the king holding his request. That reads as a king who chose stories over power. KSS does not say this: he does not refuse the promise, and he rules afterwards. The blueprint admits as much in a parenthesis but frames the beat as the turn anyway. **Opposing argument:** removing the throne is reduction, not invention, and the request is attested. **Remedy:** have the king accept or let stand Betal's promise in B28, with no refusal. Keep the source's double edge: the request includes "the 25th" (12.32.22–29), the story of the king's own night, so he is also asking that his own deed be remembered. Drop "the thematic turn" from the guidance. Do not close B35 on the king's request; close on dawn, the body, or the city that does not know. **Specialist:** a Sanskritist, on whether "the 25th" means the frame.

### 5. MAJOR: B27 tilts the open killing, and the blueprint does not admit it
**Where:** B26, B27, R3, R4. **Type:** narrative weakness; honesty of labelling.
**Problem:** the beheading (B26) can be read as survival. The heart-and-head offering (B27) comes after the danger has passed. Betal tied the prize to "that offering" (12.31.72), so B27 reads as the king taking the prize, which is the opposite of "evidence both ways". R4 is right to keep it. Calling the act "open" afterwards is not accurate. **Opposing argument:** a summoned spirit left unappeased is a danger, so propitiation is also survival. **Remedy:** state in the blueprint that B27 can be read two ways, acquisitive or propitiatory, and forbid the narration from choosing between them. Revise R3's wording to say the beheading is open and the offering leans toward the prize, with no verdict given. **Specialist:** a Sanskritist, on whether Betal instructs the heart-tearing or the king does it unprompted (12.31.65–73 against 12.32.19). I could not settle this.

### 6. MODERATE: the "open" balance is managed in both directions
**Where:** B21, B22, B25, §8 ("unspoken debt"), §12.2. **Type:** narrative weakness; honest difficulty.
**Problem:** the evidence that the rite is menacing is softened: the inventory of human remains is dropped, the skull-cup is cut, and the monk's warmth is "unforced". The guidance is also loaded against Vikram: "a lie told to a trusting man", "kills his donor". Meanwhile B14's seeds add invented support for the warning (finding 7). The design is less open than it claims. **Opposing argument:** the charter (principle 6) requires that Vikram not be vindicated, so some asymmetry is mandated. **Remedy:** draw up an evidence ledger in which every pull in either direction has a carrier in the events. Keep the menace of the rite in non-sectarian dread: fire, a corpse bathed and garlanded, offerings described as dreadful. Change "unforced" to "unreadable". Remove the evaluative glosses from the drafting notes. **Specialist:** a sensitivity reader, on how much dread can stay without the stock-villain trope.

### 7. MODERATE: B14's seeds are manufactured evidence
**Where:** B14, §12.5 item 4. **Type:** ethical risk (honest-difficulty test).
**Problem:** the seeds (Betal slowing down, his interest in the monk's gifts) are ADAPT and exist to make Betal's retrospective claim look true. That settles in advance something the source leaves as Betal's word alone (G5). It also asks how Betal knows about the gifts. The seeds cannot appear in the minimum release. **Opposing argument:** without them the warning feels arbitrary. **Remedy:** cut them, or make each one readable equally as Betal's self-interest. Fairness to the warning comes from finding 2(a), not from seeding.

### 8. MODERATE: the climax depends on TC-024 surviving its own review
**Where:** §6, B15–B16; D-054. **Type:** narrative and ethical risk.
**Problem:** the "great question" is a kinship puzzle about wives allotted by footprint, with a coerced remarriage (12.31.50), and charter §3.3 applies to it. A rewrite that gives the women a choice could make the question answerable, or change it, and the climax depends on it staying unanswerable. Readers who solve it may take the king's not-knowing as dimness. That is source-faithful, but it undercuts "great". **Opposing argument:** the reader solving what the king cannot is good irony. **Remedy:** make "the question stays unanswerable within the world" a fixed constraint in the TC-024 brief. Sell B15's weight through Betal's manner, not grandeur. Settle G12 (tale numbering), because it reveals that this is the last tale. **Specialist:** a Hindi literary reader.

## Minimum-release mechanics

**Verified against the text.** §11.2: "Betal states both rules at B10, in speech". B10 itself says only "it will tell a tale to ease the road", and its basis note says "the silence is not a rule anyone states". §13 G1's default is the king's own silence, with the return-to-tree rule "shown by use (B11)". §11.4 puts the penalty "after the first shipped tale" and, if TC-024 is first, refers back to §11.2. §11.1 makes B11 a slot that exists only when a tale ships. D-054 allows a release of the frame plus TC-024 alone. **The inconsistency is real and circular.**

**What a minimum-release reader sees.** In B09 the corpse flees once, but nothing the king says causes it. No penalty is stated before TC-024. B16's silence carries no stakes. B17 breaks a pattern that does not exist. B19's "so long" is false. §5.3's grounds for trust are empty, and B29's "tales" is a single tale.

**Options (not ranked; combinations are possible):**

- **O1. Betal voices both rules at B10, in every release.** *Source:* the return rule spoken by Betal comes from H (Platts p. 14), not KSS. The penalty moves earlier than KSS 12.8.188. *Literary:* the rules are told rather than felt, and B17 breaks a stated rule, not a lived pattern. B19 and §5.3 remain hollow. B10 never changes when tales are added (consistent with §8.5). It changes G1's default; that is a drafting choice.
- **O2. B10 voices the rules only in the minimum release, then reverts to the KSS form.** *Source:* as O1 while active. *Literary:* readers of different releases read different openings. *Needs the user's decision:* a spine segment changing after launch goes through the frame revision cycle (§8.5).
- **O3. The penalty is stated with TC-024's question.** This is KSS's own position, after the first tale. *Source:* faithful in position. The return rule is either voiced (H) or dropped. *Literary:* a compact, tense single-question story, in which the king survives his silence and that proves he did not know. The "virtue undoes the errand" engine is lost. B17 and B19's "so long" must be cut, or B19 left as a claim the reader can see is false, which moves Betal toward the deceiver reading.
- **O4. A frame-native first loop with no tale.** Betal provokes the king into speaking and goes back to the tree. *Source:* J's purpose "to break the king's silence" (p. 13). The content is ADAPT, and it must not become an invented tale (CLAUDE.md). *Literary:* one shown loop gives B17 something to break. It can stay as a permanent spine beat before the B11 slot. The penalty still needs O1 or O3.
- **O5. A summary beat for the elided night** (many tales, each answer sending Betal back), with no tale content. *Source:* the 24 loops are KSS-attested. *Literary:* restores B17, B19 and the basis for trust, but by telling. In later releases it has to coexist with the tales that are shown, which implies a voice that admits it selected them. That touches Q-005 and R8's no-narratorial-wisdom rule. *Needs the user's decision.* Interface copy about missing tales would also fall under the charter.

## Honest dissent from the author's choices
I agree with the late warning, no dying words, departure, and the cut coda. I dissent on four points: §5.3 swaps the source's better trust mechanism (predictions verified before the king's eyes) for a weaker invented one; "laugh only" removes the one passage that stops Betal becoming a protector; the boon now does the vindicating work the gods were cut for; and "open" is treated as achieved while being steered both ways. The minimum-release case should have been designed before the spine was called fixed.

## Verdict
The spine is source-faithful, causally tight and suspenseful in its main arc (debt, pattern, failure, a warning from the least trustworthy voice), and the source already supplies the honest difficulty the charter wants. The weaknesses are the blueprint's own: an invented trust basis that contradicts KSS, withheld motive that tilts Betal toward benevolence, a boon ending that vindicates by placement, an offering labelled "open" that leans toward the prize, and a circular, undrafted minimum release. Resolve findings 1–5 before drafting the spine; none requires changing a D-055 decision except the optional reopening of R2's sub-question. With them resolved, a thoughtful reader can reasonably question Vikram without manufactured doubt; without them the frame either fails (minimum release) or quietly acquits him.
