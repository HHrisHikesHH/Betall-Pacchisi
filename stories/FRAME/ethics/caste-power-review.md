# A3 ethics review: caste, power and religious representation (FRAME blueprint)

Reviewer A | model Sonnet 5.5 | **AI review only, not human review** | 2026-11-21

Read: charter, FRAME_PROPOSAL 8.2-8.6, D-043/053/054/055, research files, blueprint, approval packet. R1-R8 are treated as decided. Statements about Sanskrit, Buddhist terms and Hindi usage are from my general knowledge, unverified. Specialist needs are listed per finding and again below.

## Findings (most severe first)

**1. MAJOR | R1, B01, B04, B07, B22-B26, blueprint 12.1 | ethical risk.** The name Kṣāntiśīla is two words that, to my knowledge, are among the Buddhist perfections (*kṣānti*, *śīla*); the sources file itself says *kṣānti* is a "Buddhist virtue-word". The proposal prizes the "fine irony" of a patient man. That irony only works if the reader hears the name, so the irony *is* a villain with a Buddhist-sounding virtue name. The blueprint also drifts back into sect-adjacent English: "mendicant" (B01, B04), "monk" (B24-B26, sections 7 and 8), which a Hindi or English reader hears as *bhikṣu*. Charter 3.2.8 singles out a Buddhist *bhikṣu* villain. R1 is provisional "until a sensitivity read", but Hindi drafting will bake the name in. *Opposing:* all four witnesses share the name, and no sect word is used. *Remedy:* **I recommend the user reopen R1** and choose a new name now (the loss is source recognisability only); ban "monk" in beats and drafts. *Specialist:* Hindi reader from the Buddhist/Ambedkarite community; Buddhist studies scholar.

**2. MAJOR | B22, B23, 12.1, 12.2, D-043/D-053 | ethical risk.** "Unmarked" is not neutral. A Hindi reader supplies a default, and the proposal itself says the stock figure is the "tantrik" of film and TV. Beats keep ash, bone-dust, blood, fat-lamps, corpse-garments, cremation ground, corpse-spell, "dreadful" offerings. Removing the skull-cup removes one object while the whole kit still signals the figure. Practical consequence: a living tradition is hit anyway, by an unlabeled stand-in. *Opposing:* the source offers no other rite, and a craft-less villain is flatter (12.2's own point). *Remedy:* reduce the kit, not one item: drop blood and fat-lamps; keep one sober element (fire, circle, ash). Give the monk non-sinister craft seen earlier (B07: patient, careful, alone, unarmed). Consider one non-villainous ritual practitioner, even in passing (ADAPT). *Specialist:* scholar of Tantra and Shaiva traditions; Hindi sensitivity reader.

**3. MAJOR | 12.2, B08, B22-B24, G5 | narrative weakness and ethical risk.** The rite-versus-intent line is the "central safeguard" but is not credible as built. By Betal's account the *rite is* the sacrifice, so plan and practice cannot be separated. The staging (dread register from B07, macabre circle, a corpse called by spell) tells the reader the rite is dangerous before any intent is reported, and then the blueprint says the monk's intent is unconfirmed (G5). Evidence is tilted by the visuals before it is weighed. *Opposing:* the monk's courtesy (B22, B24) is real counter-evidence. *Remedy:* stage the rite plainly and quietly so that its danger is only Betal's claim; move dread to the walk and the dark. Do not let any narration use "terrible" for the monk himself (B07's compliance with KSS 12.8.44 helps, keep it). *Specialist:* sensitivity reader; Sanskritist on 12.32.12-14.

**4. MAJOR | B08, B14, 5.3, 4.3 rule 2, 1.2, R3 | narrative weakness, ethical risk.** Can a reader reasonably question Vikram? Yes, but the blueprint adds weight to one side without saying so. (a) "The thinness [of terms] is itself a clue" (B08) points the reader at the monk. (b) B14's seeds ("the obstacles had a purpose") are invented corroboration of Betal's claim; the blueprint admits they give "retrospective fairness", i.e. they confirm the warning. (c) ADAPT "Betal never lies about the rules" (4.3, 5.3) is the invention the emotional logic leans on hardest, and it builds Betal's credibility. (d) The ten years of gifts are read as "cultivation of obligation" (A1) and "shown to have worked" (B04): the monk's generosity is cast as grooming, which is itself villain-coding. On the other side stands courtesy only. And the reader's schema (the Hindi Vikram wins, the tantrik loses) resolves the question for them; withheld verdicts will not register. *Opposing:* KSS itself is built on Betal's word and the monk's silence. *Remedy:* give the monk-innocent reading one concrete, attested-adjacent fact the reader can hold: alone, unarmed, the king armed (KSS has him alone, no weapon named). Make B14 seeds symmetric (one oddity favours the monk's version) or cut them. Keep the gifts ambiguous: generosity or grooming. *Specialist:* none required; a human reader test of "was the king right?" would settle whether open is open.

**5. MAJOR | B27, B28, B29, B30, B34, R3, R4, R6 | ethical risk.** "No applause" is cosmetic. After the offering, Betal is "shown pleased", promises sovereignty and grants a boon (B28): the efficacy of the sacrifice is the applause. B29 calls the king's request a "thematic turn" (he "does not ask for power", though the source does not say he refused): that is a virtue gloss. B30 then has Betal declare that hearers are "freed from sin", directly after a lie and a killing: placement reads as absolution, aimed at the reader. Charter 3.4.3 and principle 6. *Opposing:* B28-B30 are attested and the only tale-linked coda content. *Remedy:* make Betal's boon unconfirmed and cold, strike the "thematic turn" note, move B30 earlier or cut it; let Betal's response to the offering be neither pleased nor reproachful. *Specialist:* Hindi reader on how the blessing sounds in this position.

**6. MAJOR | B07, B13, 12.3 | ethical risk.** Optional "people of the ground" are historically caste-coded (cremation-ground workers) and are placed as a *glimpse*, in a dread landscape, at night, "unharried". Even unmarked, a Hindi reader will place them. Charter 3.2.4 and 3.2.5(b) forbid occupation-by-birth coding. A passing appearance inside horror staging is scenery with a dignity label. The blueprint's own test ("cut them if a marker is needed") is one the drafter cannot judge. *Opposing:* cutting them leaves the ground empty and treats it as pure horror, which is also disrespect to real workers. *Remedy:* default to cut. Retain only if a person from the relevant community reads and approves, and give them an event (they see the king and carry on, working, not afraid). *Specialist:* reader from the communities concerned; essential, not optional.

**7. MODERATE | 1.1, 1.2, 4.1, B33 | ethical risk.** Power is framed as debt: "the monk's leverage is the debt". The king, the powerful party who received ten years of treasure, is cast as the ensnared one. This softens his accountability (charter 3.4.4) and sharpens the scheming-ascetic coding. In-world consequence is nil: B33 "no one asks", the treasury keeps the gems, the household never knows. Accountability then rests only on the reader's inference, which finding 4 shows is weak. *Opposing:* the ten-year debt is KSS's own device. *Remedy:* one small physical residue at dawn (the treasury, the treasurer who handled the fruit) with no statement. *Specialist:* none.

**8. MODERATE | B20, B23, B27, B31, optional B31 gesture | ethical risk.** Dignity of the dead. The hanged man becomes "cargo" (B20), "only a body" (B31); the monk's body, after the heart is torn out, is never addressed; the one dignity gesture is optional. A hanged man in this setting may be read as an executed outcaste, so his anonymity plus instrumental use matters. *Opposing:* anonymity avoids a caste-coded backstory. *Remedy:* make the plain gesture mandatory for both bodies; keep B27 graphic level minimal. *Specialist:* none; audience-setting decision from the user.

**9. MODERATE | B23, R5, 5.4 | source-fidelity error (risk) and ethical risk.** B23 has the monk call Betal "by spell" and Betal fall silent, "whether the spell binds him is shown, not explained". KSS shows a summons (12.32.12), not a binding (A1 3.1). The scene reintroduces captivity by the monk, which feeds the monk-as-enslaver reading and contradicts R5's reason. *Remedy:* show a summons, not compulsion. *Specialist:* Sanskritist on 12.32.12.

**10. MINOR | B24-B26 | narrative weakness.** The killing happens at the moment of religious deference (the prostration, with the king having asked to be "shown"). The frame can read as "ritual devotion is a trap". *Remedy:* keep the monk's bow plain and brief; do not stage it as worship. *Specialist:* none.

## Honest dissent from the author's choices
- I disagree with provisional R1. The risk falls on a living community and the fix is cheap now.
- I disagree that 12.2's rite-versus-intent safeguard is "central"; I think it is a stated intention the staging contradicts.
- B14 seeds and "Betal never lies about the rules" are invention that tilts the question, though the blueprint calls open "default".
- R4 and R6=C do not stop vindication while B28-B30 stay as written.
- I agree with R3 (open), R5, R6=C and R7 in principle.

## Verdict
The blueprint is honest about its risks and source-faithful, but it does not yet deliver what it claims: an unmarked, rite-performing ascetic who is not the stock villain, and a killing the reader can fairly doubt. The kit, the dread staging, the Buddhist-sounding name and the invented support for Betal's credibility all lean one way; the boon and blessing then pay the king. These are fixable at blueprint level. Recommend the user reopen R1, cut or gate the burning-ground people, and revise B14, B28-B30 before drafting. No human specialist has read any of this; a Buddhist-community and a Tantra-studies reader are needed before approval, and a reader from the communities concerned before any burning-ground figure is kept.
