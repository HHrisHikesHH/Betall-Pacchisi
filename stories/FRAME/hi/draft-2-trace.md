## 2. Source check (FR-01, FR-13)

**What was read.** Tawney's English translation of KSS, ch. LXXV, the opening of the Vetāla frame (the Trivikramasena–Kṣāntiśīla passage, up to the appointment and the king's night departure). It was read on the Wikisource mirror the critique cites ([kwx.liehendr.com, The_Katha_Sarit_Sagara/Chapter_75](https://kwx.liehendr.com/content/wikisource_en_all_maxi_2022-09/A/The_Katha_Sarit_Sagara/Chapter_75)), through a web-fetch tool that returns a model's extract, not the page itself. Two fetches were made; the second asked for verbatim sentences.

**Findings (translation only, wording as the tool returned it):**
- Storage: the treasurer says "I used to throw them into the treasury from the window without opening the door." → **FR-01 confirmed**; A2, A7 and A9 changed.
- Monkey: "the king gave it to a young pet monkey, that had escaped from the hands of its keepers." → **FR-13 confirmed**: the escape and the king giving it the fruit are in the translation, not ADD as Draft 1's trace said. The first fetch's summary also placed this "after the mendicant had left". That came from the summary, not from a verbatim sentence, so A4's «क्षान्तिशील के जाने के बाद» carries **medium** confidence.
- Jewel: "it burst open, and there came out of it a splendid priceless jewel"; "he took up the jewel" (A6 now «राजा ने रत्न उठाया»).
- Reward: "he was pleased, and gave those jewels to the treasurer." → A10 now gives the treasurer the jewels themselves (line note A10).
- Request: "the mendicant said to him in private, 'I have an incantation to perform which requires the aid of a brave man'". → A14 adds «अकेले में».
- Night departure: "he enveloped his head in a black cloth, and left the palace unperceived, sword in hand". → A19 now has the black cloth round the head (blueprint B06 "dark clothes" is a paraphrase of this).

**FR-13 record (for the research owner; research files not edited):** chronology.md row 4 should add the window storage (door unopened), the monkey's escape from its keepers, the king giving it the fruit, and the jewels given to the treasurer. Row 7 could note the black head-cloth. These are translation-level only, pending the Sanskrit (12.8.24–33, 12.8.40–41).
