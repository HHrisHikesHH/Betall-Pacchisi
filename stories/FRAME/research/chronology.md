> **Erratum to row 9 (first added 2026-11-22; expanded and corrected 2026-11-23; AI re-reading of the GRETIL Sanskrit and a cross-check against Tawney, not a specialist reading).** Row 9 as first written omitted that **the king speaks to the laughing corpse**. The Sanskrit (GRETIL `SoKs_12.8.54–55`, Durgaprasad–Parab numbering; sandhi as printed there):
>
> - 12.8.54: *tataḥ sa rājā matvā taṃ vetālādhiṣṭhitaṃ tadā / kiṃ hasasy ehi gacchāva iti yāvad akampitaḥ //* (*hasasy ehi* is *hasasi ehi* by sandhi.)
> - 12.8.55: *vakti tāvan na bhūmau savetālaṃ śavam aikṣata / aikṣatātraiva vṛkṣe tu lambamānaṃ sthitaṃ punaḥ //*
>
> **Reading.** "Then the king, thinking it possessed by a Vetāla, undaunted, [said] 'Why do you laugh? Come, let us go'; as he speaks (*yāvat … vakti tāvat*) he did not see the corpse with the Vetāla on the ground; he saw it hanging again on that very tree." (*na bhūmau … aikṣata*: not seen on the ground; *aikṣata … vṛkṣe tu lambamānaṃ sthitaṃ punaḥ*: seen again hanging on the tree. The text reports what the king sees, not how the corpse moved.) Tawney (II, OCR) renders the link as "And immediately he missed from the ground the corpse … and perceived that it was once more suspended on that very tree."
>
> **Established:** the king speaks to the corpse (12.8.54); the corpse is back on the tree in the same narrated moment (12.8.55). The correlative *yāvat … tāvat* is temporal; it can be read "while he speaks" or "just as he speaks / immediately", and this record does not choose between those two. **Not established:** whether the speech causes the return. The text states no cause and gives no reason. Neither "the speech caused it" nor "the speech was irrelevant" is a source fact, and KSS contains no formal silence rule here (the silence at 12.8.57, *maunena*, is the king's manner of carrying). Rows 9 and 11 otherwise stand. The omission was found by the focused validation of the frame blueprint revision 2 ([../ethics/focused-validation.md](../ethics/focused-validation.md)); this fuller wording replaces the first erratum, which said "while he is still speaking" with less qualification.

> **Supplement to row 9: the speech–departure pattern elsewhere in KSS (2026-11-23; AI reading of the GRETIL Sanskrit, not a specialist reading).** It does not bear on whether the speech causes the first return at 12.8.55, which stays unstated.
>
> | Verse | Who speaks | What it shows |
> |---|---|---|
> | 12.8.196 *vimuktamaunam* | Narrator | After the king's answer, with his silence broken, the Vetāla goes by *māyā* "to gauge his firmness" (narrator's gloss). |
> | 12.9.41 *muktamaunāt* | Narrator | The Vetāla leaves the shoulder of the king whose silence is released. |
> | 12.14.114 *nirastamaunasya* | Narrator | The same pattern, "as before" (*pūrvavat*). |
> | 12.16.34 *maunaṃ tyājayati prāyaḥ kālakṣepāya māṃ bhavān* | The king, to Betal | "You probably / usually make me give up silence, to pass the time" (*prāyaḥ* can mean either; Tawney: "repeatedly"). The king names a purpose, passing time, aloud to Betal. |
> | 12.29.47 *icchati gantum ayaṃ me* … *vimaunasya* | The king, to himself | *vimaunasya* is read with the king ("of me, now out of silence"): this one wants to go from me; "let him go, I shall bring him back." Tawney recasts it as "This demon wishes me to break silence, and so to escape from me", adding a causal "and so" and "escape". |
>
> **Editorial formulation (the user's, 2026-11-23).** KSS does not formulate a general opening rule stating that any speech sends the corpse back. However, it repeatedly narrates Betal's departure after Vikram breaks silence, and Vikram explicitly recognises Betal's strategy of making him break silence. The specific opening frame-only loop and its placement before the first tale remain adaptation decisions.
>
> *Related, not used as pattern evidence:* at 12.27.2 Betal speaks to the silently walking king between tales (*rājan kas te 'nubandho 'yaṃ gaccha rātrisukhaṃ bhaja*, "King, what is this persistence of yours? Go, enjoy the night"). It is not a provocation of speech, so B10a remains adaptation.

**Material conclusions for the frame:**
1. The **late warning with a retrospective reason** is KSS-specific. Only H puts a warning early.
2. **Servitude is absent only in KSS** (existing finding confirmed; J's *tāla-vetāla* siddhi is its closest equivalent).
3. J and H give Betal a **victim backstory** (executed or killed by the ascetic's circle). KSS gives none. These strands are caste-coded or Bengali-only and are already excluded by FRAME_PROPOSAL §8.2(2).
4. J's "Betal and the ascetic share a teacher" backstory shows Betal and the ascetic as **linked**; if the blueprint wants the two to have a past, that is **J-sourced** and interpolated. A KSS-based frame would be **our invention**.

## 3. Betal: what is directly attested, and what is not

### 3.1 Circumstances and captivity
- **KSS:** Betal is **in a hanged corpse** in the cremation ground. The king infers he is there (12.8.54 *vetālādhiṣṭhita*). He appears from the corpse by speaking at 12.8.58. **Nothing says Betal is bound, imprisoned or suffering**; he returns to the tree by his own illusion-power (12.8.196) and leaves at will (12.32.30).
- **Compulsion:** the only compulsion shown is by the ascetic: at the rite he **summons the Vetāla into the corpse by the power of spells** (12.32.12). That says the ascetic can *call* him. It does not say Betal had been his captive earlier. Betal's own wording at 12.31.63–64 may suggest trouble from the monk (D4 in sources.md), but that is a reading.
- **J:** the corpse is a **false-accused oil-miller who died impaled**; the ascetic used the king to seize the Vetāla "by deceit" (pp. 149–152). **H:** the Vetāla is an oilman killed by the potter-devotee and hung head-down.
- **So:** the "captive Betal" of our proposal §8.4 is **attested only in J and H, not in KSS**. Using it with a KSS base would be an **addition from other witnesses** or an **invention**; record it as such.

### 3.2 Cooperation and resistance
- Resistance is shown physically (he flees to the tree, laughs, cries) in KSS, H and J. **He cooperates verbally**: he opens the first tale and keeps asking (12.8.58).
- **Purposes the sources give for the tales and returns:** "to ease the road" (KSS 12.8.58, Betal's words); "to gauge the king's firmness" (12.8.196, narrator); "to break the king's silence" (J p. 13); "ease the road" (H p. 14). These are different **stated** purposes and can coexist.

### 3.3 The warning and its motive
| Statement | Where | Type |
|---|---|---|
| The king **does not know** the answer, so he walks gladly in silence. Betal **cannot go on deceiving this "treasure-house of courage"** and the monk's trickery is not exhausted by this. He will therefore **trick the monk** and **give his earned success to the king**, who has a good future. | 12.31.61–64 | Betal's **private reasoning**, as narrated (the narrator is entering Betal's mind). |
| "I am pleased with this courage"; the king has been troubled by many comings and goings and has not wavered. | 12.31.66 | Betal's **spoken** reason. |
| "**For this purpose I put this obstruction in your way for so long.**" | 12.31.73 | Betal's **retrospective claim**. It reframes the whole night as a delaying action, but contradicts nothing explicit; the tales were *also* presented as entertainment and a test (12.8.58, 12.8.196). |
| He advises the king to take the Vidyādhara prize through the monk's offering. | 12.31.72 | **Instigation of the killing** with a reward attached. |
| The Betal of Ś: "pleased by your daring", "deceived you many times" *(OCR)*. | Ś p. 61–62 | Betal's spoken reason; **same shape** as KSS, a second witness. |

**What KSS does *not* say:** it does not say Betal hates the ascetic (it says only that the king, afterwards, regards him as an enemy: 12.31.75), that Betal is trying to free himself, or that Betal was moved by sympathy. These would be **inference**. The motive is best described as: **admiration for the king's courage + a resolve to thwart the monk**, with Betal's own retrospective claim that the obstacles were meant to protect the king. The warning's **timing is tied to the king's failure to answer tale 24**: that is the trigger, directly stated (12.31.62).

## 4. Ethical and narrative implications (checks, not solutions)

1. **The king learns the plan only from Betal, in every witness.** The monk **never says** he will kill the king: in KSS he says only that prostration will win the king "your desired success" (12.32.15–16). So the killing is a **pre-emptive act on a mischievous spirit's word**. This is the strongest source-based way to **keep Vikram morally examinable** without invention. The text also has the monk **praise the king's steadfastness** (12.32.6–8) and the king **lie** ("I do not know how", 12.32.17).
2. **The king's own acts at the rite** (beheading, tearing out the heart, offering head and heart to the Vetāla) are the very sacrifice the monk intended, with the roles reversed. The existing frame documents mention the beheading but not the offering. **Flag for the blueprint:** whether to keep the offering. Cutting it removes a source-given morally charged beat.
3. **Betal incites the killing** and ties it to a reward (12.31.72). Betal is therefore **not a moral authority** in the source either; the frame can keep him mischievous and self-interested without our adding anything.
4. **Romanticising captivity:** KSS gives Betal **no suffering and no captivity**. A pathos-of-captivity reading would come from **H and J**, not from the base. The **KSS-faithful ending is Betal departing by choice** (12.32.30).
5. **Reward of domination:** KSS's coda gives the king the **Vidyādhara empire**, a sword and union with Śiva (12.32.31–41). The **only part tied to the stories** is the king's request that the tales be famous (12.32.22–29), which is also where the **blessing for hearers** lives. Everything else is power. The proposal's plan (keep the request, shrink the throne) is source-compatible.
6. **Outgroup language:** 12.32.33 speaks of *asuras born as mlecchas*. Not to be carried into adaptation.
7. **Suspense:** the **late warning is also the source's central suspense device** (the king walks on in ignorance, Betal reveals the plot at the end). The ethical questions above do not require moving the warning.

**Genuine blockers: none.** Items for the blueprint or A3 to decide: (a) whether the king's knowledge of the plan stays Betal-only (recommended by the source); (b) whether the heart-offering stays; (c) Betal's motive within the range in §3.3; (d) "departs" versus "freed" (only "departs" has a source); (e) Q-005.
