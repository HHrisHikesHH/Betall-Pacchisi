The villain's affiliation is **not stable across the tradition**. That is positive support for D-043, which keeps him unmarked. It is evidence, not a proof of what an "original" said.

### 2.3 Occupation or ritual role

| Witness | Role as stated |
|---|---|
| KSS | Brings the daily fruit as courtesy and "service" (*sevārtham*, 12.8.23). Asks for a *vīra* as helper in a ***mantra-sādhana*** (a rite, 12.8.35). Performs a night rite in a great cremation ground: circle of bone-powder, blood, fat-lamps, ash on the body, corpse-garments; summons the Vetāla into the corpse **by the power of spells** (12.32.2–14). |
| Ś | *mantra-sādhana* on the Godāvarī-bank cremation ground; promises **eight siddhis** (p. 6, vv. 15–17); the king is to be his ***uttara-sādhaka*** (assistant). Rite: Vetāla-invocation (*vetālāhvāna*, p. 62). |
| J | *Mṛtaka-siddhi* (success through a corpse); the king is to be his ***uttara-sādhaka***; the king is to be offered to **Devī** (Betal's disclosure). |
| H | A meditant whose practice has made a demon of the oilman; wants to kill Vikram (pp. 7–8). |

**Important correction to our own wording.** In Ś and J the word *sādhaka* appears only in the compound ***uttara-sādhaka***, the **king's** role (the helper). The ascetic is the one who performs the *sādhana*. So "sādhak" for the ascetic is a defensible plain reading ("one who performs a rite"), but it is **ours**. No witness uses it as his title.

### 2.5 Q-414: finding (not a decision)

1. **Name:** Kṣāntiśīla is attested in **all four witnesses**; variants exist in manuscripts only. It is not itself a sect-label in any text. The "Buddhist" reading is an inference.
2. **Role words:** the texts describe a **rite-performer** (*mantra-sādhana*, *siddhi*) in a **cremation ground** (*śmaśāna*), and offer **no stable sect**. "Sādhak" is not a title in the sources and, in Ś and J, names the *king's* role.
3. **Source-faithful options** for the user's later decision (the user decides after review; this session does not choose): (a) keep the name and describe the role by function only ("the man who performs the rite"); (b) keep the name plus *bhikṣu* or *yogī* as a generic mendicant word, accepting that *bhikṣu* may be read as Buddhist; (c) change or drop the name. The sources do not force one. Option (a) is closest to the KSS Sanskrit, which gives no sect.
4. **Sensitivity input needed:** whether the name Kṣāntiśīla reads as Buddhist to Hindi readers today. The repo has no evidence. This belongs to the sensitivity reader (status.md).

## 4. Discrepancies and open questions

- **D1: Uhle versus Emeneau on J's backstory.** Uhle (Vorwort p. XXII) describes J's Vetāla as an oil-miller's **son** (*Oelmüllerssohn*) and Kṣāntiśīla as the **king's brother**. Emeneau's printed Bengali text says the Vetāla was himself an **oil-miller householder**, impaled after a false theft charge by a king's officer, and that Kṣāntiśīla was a **disciple of the teacher who had lodged with the oil-miller**; "he shall be your brother" is the teacher's phrase to the oil-miller, so the "brother" is Betal's, not the king's. Uhle did not have Emeneau's edition and may have used the Calcutta printing. **Unresolved, but low materiality:** Emeneau argues this backstory is a Bengali **interpolation** (absent from the Nepali recension and the Newari) and the adaptation does not use it (FRAME_PROPOSAL §8.2(2)).
- **D2: J's ending and Betal.** In J the king receives *tāla-vetāla-siddhi* (power over those beings), and Betal's own fate is **not stated**. This is the ending that fits our "no servitude" rule worst. J's penalty also differs: the Bengali text makes the king a *mahāpātakī* (great sinner) if he knows and stays silent (Emeneau p. 14, variant V); there is **no bursting body part**. Ś has the **heart** bursting (Uhle p. 12; Emeneau note), KSS the **head** (12.8.188). I found **no further internal contradiction** in J's ending beyond D1.
- **D3: Tawney versus the Sanskrit at 12.32.28.** The Sanskrit says reciter and listener become *muktapāpau* (**freed from sin**). Tawney writes "freed from their **curse**". The change adds a captivity or curse reading to the coda that the Sanskrit does not state. Do not cite Tawney for this line.
- **D4: KSS 12.31.63.** *krīḍan bhikṣuḥ sa cāsmābhir iyataiva na śāmyati* is compact. Tawney reads it as: this much is not enough to make the mendicant stop playing tricks with *me*. The sense that the monk is **toying with** Betal is a **reading**, not a clear statement. Needs a specialist before it is used as evidence of Betal's grievance.
- **D5: Ś p. 6 silence clause** is in square brackets in Uhle's text; its manuscript status was not checked.
- **D6: Ś 25 wording (*OCR*).** "bahuvāraiḥ mayā tvaṃ vañcito 'si" is readable ("you have been deceived by me many times"); the following clause is garbled. Do not build on it without a page image.
- **Not read:** KSS 12.8.1–20 in Sanskrit (the old brahman's embedding), Hindi original, Ś manuscript A/other recensions beyond Uhle's printed text, page images for any OCR passage.
