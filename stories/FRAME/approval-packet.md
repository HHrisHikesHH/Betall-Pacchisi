## R1. Q-414: the ascetic's name and role words
*Evidence:* Kṣāntiśīla is in all four witnesses (KSS, Ś, J, H). No witness gives him a sect in KSS; Ś, J and H give him different ones (*digambara* yogī, *kāpālika*, a potter-devotee). "Buddhist" is our reading, not the Sanskrit. *Sādhaka* is **not** his title in any witness (in Ś and J it names the king's helper role).

| Option | What | S / A |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Keep the name. Describe him **by function** only (the man who performs the rite), no sect word. | **S:** name in all four witnesses; *mantra-sādhana* (KSS 12.8.35) names the rite. **A:** the Hindi phrasing for the function. |
| **B** | Keep the name and add a generic ascetic word. | **S:** *bhikṣu* and *śramaṇa* throughout KSS (but readable as Buddhist); *tāpasa* only in Śiva's speech (12.32.32). **A:** which Hindi noun, and its connotation, which nobody on the project has checked. |
| **C** | Change or drop the name; function only. | **S:** none. **A:** all of it. Loses the one point on which all four witnesses agree and the "patience" irony of ten years of gifts. A new name needs its own check. |

**Recommendation: A, provisionally.** Use the name through one replaceable placeholder in drafts; drop the phrase "sādhak of the burning ground" (it is ours and clashes with Ś/J usage). Ask a Hindi sensitivity reader whether the name reads as Buddhist; if yes, switch to C. I recommend A because it is closest to KSS and the name's irony is load-bearing for the opening.
**Disagreement:** I cannot judge how Hindi readers hear *kṣānti-śīla*. A reviewer may say the name alone is enough to mark him. If so, C is right even at some cost.

## R2. What moves Betal to intervene
*Evidence:* The trigger is directly attested: the king fails tale 24, so Betal decides he can no longer deceive him and will deceive the monk and give the monk's "earned success" to the king (12.31.61–64). He gives two spoken reasons: pleased by the king's courage (12.31.66) and "for this purpose I put obstructions in your way" (12.31.73). He also instigates the killing with a prize (12.31.72). KSS says nothing of sympathy, hatred of the monk, or captivity.

| Option | What | S / A |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Layered and unresolved.** He says he admires the king and that the obstacles were for this; the frame also shows he is thwarting the monk; the narration confirms none of it. | **S:** every stated piece. **A:** the layering; any grievance against the monk (a *reading* of 12.31.63, A1 D4). |
| **B** | **Reward.** Pure admiration: the king earned it. | **S:** 12.31.66. **A:** making it the whole motive. Risk: Betal as examiner granting a prize to a deserving king (moral-authority and vindication risk). |
| **C** | **Self-interest.** The game is spent; he swaps the victim or ends the monk's hold on him. | **S:** partial (monk "won't stop", spells call him in, 12.32.12). **A:** the swap and the grievance are inference. Ś's "deceived you many times" is OCR and provisional. |
| **D** | **Compassion.** He wants to save the king. | **S:** none. **A:** invention. Risk: Betal becomes the charter's mouthpiece. Not recommended. |

**Sub-question:** does the narration show his reasons (KSS does) or only his laugh and his speech? Recommend the latter, because the reasons then stay unverifiable.
**Recommendation: A**, laugh only. **Disagreement:** showing his mind (KSS does) would be clearer for readers; withholding it adds mystery but may read as evasive. Both are defensible.

## R3. Does Vikram's killing stay morally ambiguous?
*Evidence:* The king learns the plan **only from Betal** in all four witnesses. The monk's own words describe prostration as the way to the king's "success" (12.32.15–16). The king then lies ("I do not know how"), kills a man who has just praised him, and gives his heart and head to Betal. Betal instructs the prostration and beheading ruse with a reward attached; the heart-tearing and the offering are the king's own further acts (D-056, D-058 item 3).

| Option | What | S / A |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Open.** Evidence both ways, no verdict from Betal, the narration, or any god. Betal's prize stays in the warning (attested) but is never shown delivered. | **S:** the whole structure. **A:** withholding every verdict; the prize never delivered. |
| **B** | **Lean justified.** A confirming act or line from the monk. | **S:** none (KSS gives the monk no such line). **A:** invention. Contradicts D-043 ("do not clean the act"). |
| **C** | **Lean condemned.** Counter-evidence: the rite was harmless, or Betal was lying. | **S:** none. **A:** invention. Makes the king a murderer on a trickster's word, which the source does not claim. |

**Recommendation: A.** It follows the source's own shape and D-043.
**Disagreement:** (i) The plot rewards him whatever the narration withholds: he lives, the monk dies. Neutrality in verdict does not neutralise that. (ii) A reader can say "open" is the king's own account winning by default. I think the risk is real and is best met by R4 and R6 (no applause, no throne), not by changing the act.

## R4. Keep the heart-and-head offering?
*Evidence:* KSS 12.32.19: after the beheading the king tears out the monk's heart and gives head and heart to Betal, completing the monk's own sacrifice with the roles reversed. Earlier frame documents mention only the beheading.

| Option | What | S / A |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Keep**, restrained, no applause. | **S:** 12.32.19. **A:** the restraint and the cut of the cheering host (12.32.20). |
| **B** | **Cut the offering**, keep the beheading. Betal receives nothing. | **S:** the beheading only. **A:** the omission. The killing reads as cleaner. |
| **C** | **Offering refused or reversed** (the king stops, or Betal declines). | **S:** none. **A:** invention. Cleans the king and moralises Betal. |

**Recommendation: A.** It is the most charged beat in the frame and the one that most prevents Vikram being vindicated. It also shows Betal as a recipient, not an authority.
**Disagreement:** A may reinforce the "tantric human-sacrifice" trope, ironically by putting the rite in the king's hands. Graphic level follows whatever audience setting the project holds (not re-checked here). If the sensitivity reader flags the trope, B is the fallback and the frame loses some moral weight.

## R5. Betal departs freely: no liberation language?
*Evidence:* KSS 12.32.30: he leaves "to a dwelling he likes" by his own power. No servitude, no release. "Freed from the tree" is attested in **no witness**. KSS shows no captivity; the captive Betal is J/H only. (Tawney's "freed from their curse" at 12.32.28 mistranslates *muktapāpau* "freed from sin" and should not be cited.)

| Option | What | S / A |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Departs** by his own act. The word "free" is not used. | **S:** 12.32.30. **A:** the restraint. |
| **B** | **Freed** (from the tree, a curse, the king). | **S:** none. **A:** invention; needs a captive Betal earlier (J/H strand), and romanticises captivity (§3.4.3). |
| **C** | **Ambiguous:** he leaves, and may return next dark fortnight. | **S:** none. **A:** invention; makes him a recurring figure. |

**Recommendation: A.** **Disagreement:** a reader who sympathised with Betal may feel it is ungenerous not to call it freedom. The cost of calling it that is an invented captivity; I think it is worth refusing.

## R6. How much of the divine coda stays?
*Evidence:* KSS 12.32.31–41: Śiva and the gods praise the king for killing a *kūṭatāpasa* greedy for the Vidyādhara empire; he is told of his earlier birth to destroy asuras born as *mlecchas*; he receives the sword Aparājita, then sovereignty over earth and the Vidyādharas, then union with Śiva. In Ś the gods are Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Maheśvara; in H, Indra and the gods.

| Option | What | S / A |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Full KSS coda.** | **S:** all of it. **A:** none. Includes a divine verdict, a throne, a sectarian deity, and R7. |
| **B** | **One reduced image:** unnamed gods or a light at dawn; no verdict, no sword, no throne. | **S:** the appearance (reduced). **A:** the unnamed deity and the omissions. Meets a Hindi reader's expectation of divine presence (H has it). |
| **C** | **None.** The night closes on Betal's boon and dawn. | **S:** a reduction (the boon and the departure are attested). **A:** the closing image (B35). |

**Recommendation: C.** Any divine arrival after the killing reads as approval by placement, even wordless. The only non-power content in the coda is the blessing for hearers, and that sits in Betal's mouth (B30), not the gods'.
**Disagreement:** a case for B: the myth register and the Hindi expectation are real, and C loses them. If you want B, it must follow Betal's departure, carry no judgement, and pay the king nothing.

## R7. The *mleccha*/*asura* passage
*Evidence:* KSS 12.32.33: Śiva says the king was born as Vikramāditya to destroy asuras born as *mlecchas*. A1 flags it as outgroup-demonising. It is also the only place KSS links Trivikramasena to the name "Vikram".

| Option | What | S / A |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Cut** the line and the earlier-birth framing. | **S:** reduction. **A:** none. |
| **B** | **Keep a past-birth identification** without outgroup language (to put down evil). | **S:** the earlier birth (12.32.33), the evildoer (within 12.32.31–38). **A:** the rewording. |
| **C** | **Keep verbatim** in divine speech. | **S:** 12.32.33. **A:** none. Fails charter §3.2.8 and §3.2.5(b): a divine voice cannot be framed as "a character's belief". |

**Recommendation: A.** **Moot if R6 = C.** **Disagreement:** a Sanskritist may say the sense of *mleccha* here is more nuanced than "outgroup"; that is unverified. Cutting is safe either way.

## R8. Q-005: the outer narrator and the embedded frame
*Evidence:* KSS tells this frame **inside** Mṛgāṅkadatta's story, relayed by Vikramakeśarin from an old brahman (12.8 intro; Tawney only, Sanskrit unread). D-035: Betal tells the tales **within the story world**; the project's own voice is Q-005. D-043 withdrew "an unnamed narrator tells the frame" because it half-decided Q-005.

| Option | What | S / A |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Unmarked voice as a drafting rule only**: no first person, no reader address, no persona or gender, no "it is said". KSS's embedding dropped. Q-005 stays open. | **S:** a reduction of the KSS embedding. **A:** the rule. |
| **B** | **Keep a thin transmission trace** (someone told it). | **S:** KSS's embedding (medium confidence). **A:** the teller's identity; an "old brahman" authority figure needs charter care. |
| **C** | **Betal narrates the frame as well.** | **S:** none (KSS does not). **A:** invention; collides with the suspense (he knows the ending) and with D-035 only partly. |
| **D** | **A new persona** (human or AI) frames the book. | **S:** none. **A:** invention; decides Q-005. |

**Recommendation: A, as a working rule, with Q-005 still open.** The blueprint lists the **six places** where a persona would change the text (blueprint §1.4); each other option touches only those.
**Disagreement:** drafting in Hindi will fix register choices that are expensive to reverse, so "open" costs more than it looks. The alternative is to decide Q-005 **before drafting**; I would not decide it here, but you may prefer to.
