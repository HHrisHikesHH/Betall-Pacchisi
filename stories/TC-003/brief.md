## 1. Source beats
1. In Pāṭaliputra, Prince Vikramakesarin owns a learned parrot, a cursed celestial; on its advice he marries the Magadha princess Candraprabhā, who owns an equally learned mynah [KSS 12.10.4–9].
2. The parrot proposes union to the mynah; she refuses because men are wicked and ungrateful; he retorts that women are wicked. They wager (slavery or wifehood) and go to the prince [KSS 10–14]. **Ours:** make the wager symmetric (service for a season).
3. Mynah's tale: Dhanadatta, a merchant's son, gambles away his inheritance; a merchant takes him in and gives him his daughter Ratnāvalī [KSS 16–22].
4. He leads her and an old woman into a forest and throws both into a pit, taking the jewels [KSS 23–28]. The old woman dies; Ratnāvalī climbs out and tells her parents robbers did it [29–35].
5. When he returns for money she protects him with the cover story; one night he kills her in his lap and flees [KSS 36–45].
6. Parrot's tale: Vasudattā, a merchant's daughter, takes a lover; on the night her husband arrives she goes out to meet him and finds him hanged as a suspected thief [KSS 48–64].
7. She kisses the corpse; a Vetāla in it bites off her nose [KSS 65–68]. She cries that her husband did it; he is bound and silent [74–78].
8. A thief who saw everything tells the king; the king frees the husband, cuts off her ears and banishes her, and makes the thief chief of police [KSS 79–88]. **Ours:** leave the unequal punishment visible, without comment.
9. The birds are freed from their curse; the dispute is left undecided [KSS 89–91].
10. Betal asks [KSS 92]; Vikram answers; Betal returns [93–95].

Variants: Ś 3 (different names; question "whose sin is greater?"), Hindi IV (same form, with a stated reason): both answer "women". J 3 unread; BKM may have two related embedded tales (unestablished).
