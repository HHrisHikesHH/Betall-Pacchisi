# TC-026 brief: Mūladeva wins a bride for Śaśideva (J 22)

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: yes (D-081; answer kept, reasoning is composed, and a rival answer exists)

## 1. Source beats
All [source: J 22, Tr pp. 125–133; P and V only] unless marked.
1. In Kuṇḍinapura the merchant Śaṅkaradatta, friend of King Śvetaketu, leaves wife Anaṅgasenā under guard and goes abroad; twelve years pass; she wastes away.
2. At a festival the rogue Śaśideva sees her and falls in love; his friend Mūladeva proposes the bawd Kāmikalpalatā, who can "disturb even Arundhatī".
3. The bawd poses as an all-knowing ascetic (Śiva worship, show of power). Consulted by the mother, she falsely says the husband has married abroad.
4. The terrified parents beg a remedy; she prescribes a "charm": in a hidden bower the daughter must look at a man brought to her and make love with him daily until he takes the husband's form. The father builds the bower.
5. The man is Śaśideva; the two become lovers; she forgets her husband.
6. Mūladeva, in Śaśideva's guise, seizes her at the king's gate as his runaway wife; the king threatens to kill him; he says he mistook her and promises to produce his wife.
7. A woman's corpse in her ornaments is laid in her house and the house burned; the lovers take her away. Parents grieve; the king sends "her" bones to pilgrimage places.
8. Later Mūladeva presents Anaṅgasenā as his wife who "looks like" her; the king exclaims she is Anaṅgasenā and lets them go; her parents give her the jewels and weep. Whether they knew her is not stated.
Gaps: the king's oath and Mūladeva's excuse rest on a passage Emeneau thinks corrupt (Tr p. 131 nn. 11–13); the bucket cites "T p. 130", N3 cites Tr p. 131: files disagree on the page, same passage. Husband's fate is untold; why Mūladeva takes Śaśideva's guise is not stated.
Variant: Newari 22 (German only) makes them brothers, has Śaśideva plan the escape, a brahman at the gate, a bawd preaching the Prajñāpāramitā, and **reverses the answer**.

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question (source): "Who showed the greater intelligence (*mahatī buddhiḥ*): Kāmikalpalatā or Mūladeva?" (T p. 132; Tr p. 133, "more ready wit").
- Source answer (verbatim as the files give it): "Mūladeva, the great rogue (*mahān dhūrtaḥ*), because his wit was ready on every occasion (*sarvatrotpannabuddhitvāt*) (T p. 132; Tr p. 133). No further reasoning." Newari 22 (Jørgensen p. 236): the bawd, "because she was the occasion of all the cleverness".
- Our answer: **same** (Mūladeva), per D-048. Audit: ADAPT (light), L–M; first draft that grafted the Newari answer and invented complicity was withdrawn after the challenger's review. The reasoning beyond the source's one phrase is our composition.
  - **PROPOSED A (needs the user's approval):** Vikram keeps the source phrase and adds only what the plot shows: the bawd's scheme worked on a prepared ground (fearful parents); Mūladeva's wit met what no one planned (the king's threat at the gate, the need to erase her). Betal argues the bawd's case at full strength (Newari reading: every later step stands on her opening).
  - **PROPOSED B (alternative):** Vikram gives the source phrase alone, no added reasoning; Betal's rival case stays unanswered by argument, leaving the weighing to the reader (closest to source, thinnest).
- Honest difficulty: cleverness in starting a scheme versus finishing it; two traditions gave opposite answers.
