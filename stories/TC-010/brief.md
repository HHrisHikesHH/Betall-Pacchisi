# TC-010 brief: Madanasenā and her rash promise

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no (D-081)

## 1. Source beats
Base: KSS 10 (12.17.1–68). Evidence: SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE TC-010; SLATE_AUDIT §5 TC-010.
1. Merchant's daughter Madanasenā, betrothed to the merchant Samudradatta, is seen in her garden by her brother's friend Dharmadatta, who falls sleeplessly in love. [source: KSS 12.17.3–16]
2. He pleads; she says she is betrothed and asks him to leave. He says he cannot live without her. [source: KSS 17–21]
3. Fearing force (*balātkāra-bhaya*), she proposes: let the wedding happen first, so her father gains the merit of giving a maiden. He refuses a woman who has belonged to another, so she promises to come to him, already married, before going to her husband, and he makes her swear. [source: KSS 22–26]
4. Wedding night: she turns away and weeps. He says that if she does not love him she may go to whoever she loves. She asks his promise of safety and tells him of her vow, which she cannot break because she has kept truth since childhood. [source: KSS 27–38]
5. Stunned, he reflects that she loves another, and releases her. [source: KSS 39–41]
6. On the night road a thief seizes her garment; she offers her jewels, he refuses them and will not let her go; she tells him her story. Trusting her truthfulness, he lets her go and waits. [source: KSS 42–52]
7. Dharmadatta asks what happened, is pleased with her truthfulness, wants nothing to do with another man's wife, and sends her back. [source: KSS 53–54]
8. The thief releases her again, with her jewels, and she reaches home "protected" by him. [source: KSS 55–58]
9. She tells her husband everything. KSS: seeing her face unchanged and no sign of intercourse, he honours her. [source: KSS 59–61] **Ours:** he takes her word; no inspection. [ours, SLATE_AUDIT §5]
10. Betal asks. [source: KSS 62–63]

Variants (not used): Ś 9, Hindi IX add the lover's verses on women's untrustworthiness; Hindi IX husband "showed her no affection"; J 10 adds a brahman-ogre, the husband rejects her as defiled and wins the verdict (Emeneau pp. 71, 73). KSS 12.17.58 (*anuyāyinā*): escort or merely following is uncertain (N1).

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question: of the thief and the two merchants, who was truly generous (*kas tyāgī*)? (KSS 12.17.62–63)
- Source answer: the thief (64). The husband could not keep a wife known to be attached to another (65); the lover's ardour was "worn by time" (*kālajīrṇasaṃvegaḥ*, 66) and he feared the husband would report him to the king (66); the thief, with no such constraint, gave up a "jewel of a woman" (67).
- Our answer: same. Reasoning stays KSS's, reframed: the husband's "could hardly keep her" is a description of the social pressure on him, not a norm (SLATE_AUDIT §5). The thief renounces power over her, not a "jewel". If drafting rewrites the reasoning beyond this reframing, it becomes our composition and needs the user's approval (D-041); PROPOSED reframing only.
- Honest-difficulty: the husband gave up a wife he wanted, unasked, on his wedding night, at a cost the thief never paid; J 10 answers "the husband". The thief's risk-free restraint is also arguable.

## 4. Charter points
- Oath under threat: keep the fear of force visible so the promise reads as rational, not romantic (§3.3.3, §3.3.6).
- No inspection of her body; no verses on women (§3.3.1, §3.3.4). Her truthfulness is the engine.
- Sexual-honour logic in the verdict must not become the tale's norm (§3.3.4, §3.5); Betal may contest it at full strength.
- Thief as restraint, not glamour (§3.2.9, SLATE_AUDIT §5.3). Merchants are incidental (caste L).
- She is not a prize passed between three men (§3.3.2): her choices lead each scene.

## 5. Voice plan
- Hidden layer: "a promise is easy to make and costly to keep, and the tale asks who really paid."
- "And you too": (a) after the oath, the promise you made in fear that you still owe; (b) at the thief's release, who you trust most when it costs you nothing.
- Odd details: why she asks to be married first (her father's merit); why the husband listens (he sees her tears first); why the thief waits.
- Plot object: the jewels she offers and is given back; generosity grows from what each man holds and returns.
- Callbacks: none.
