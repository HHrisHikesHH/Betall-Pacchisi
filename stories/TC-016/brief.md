# TC-016 brief: The sacrifice of Jīmūtavāhana

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no (D-081)

## 1. Source beats
Base: KSS 16 (12.23.1–207), the longest tale; compress the romance. Evidence: SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE TC-016.
1. Kāñcanapura: Vidyādhara prince Jīmūtavāhana remembers former births, is "born of a portion of a Bodhisattva" (*bodhisattvāṃśa*), compassionate to all. [source: KSS 3–10]
2. He asks the family wishing-tree to rid the earth of poverty; it rains wealth. [source: KSS 11–29]
3. Envious kin prepare war; he will not kill kin for a kingdom and leaves with his parents for Malaya. [source: KSS 30–38]
4. He befriends Mitrāvasu, loves his sister Malayavatī (garlands, lovesick night; her attempted hanging, a heavenly voice), and marries her. [source: KSS 39–93] Compress; keep her reproach to Gaurī (181–190), shorten the noose. [ours, SLATE_AUDIT §5]
5. On the shore he sees mountains of bones: by Vāsuki's pact Garuḍa eats one Nāga a day (feud from Kadrū and Vinatā). He blames Vāsuki and Garuḍa and resolves to give his body for one. [source: KSS 94–112] **Ours:** make Vāsuki's pact visible as a ruler's bargain paid in ordinary lives, through events, without speeches. [ours]
6. He hears young Śaṅkhacūḍa and his old mother. He offers himself; the mother refuses; Śaṅkhacūḍa refuses to be saved at another's cost and goes to worship Śiva. [source: KSS 113–145]
7. Jīmūtavāhana mounts the rock; Garuḍa seizes him, tears out his crest-jewel and eats him while he prays; his joy turns Garuḍa's perception. [source: KSS 146–153, 164–166]
8. The jewel falls before Malayavatī; Śaṅkhacūḍa returns, follows the blood and cries out that he is the Nāga. Garuḍa is stricken; Jīmūtavāhana nearly dies as his family arrives. [source: KSS 154–180]
9. Gaurī revives him with nectar and makes him emperor; Garuḍa offers a boon; he asks that no more Nāgas be eaten and the bones revive. [source: KSS 181–201]

Variants: Ś 15 gives practice across births (Vetāla objects first); Hindi XV grounds practice in Kshatriya caste (AI reading, not specialist); J 24 unread (opens with a weeping serpent-mother, Tr p. 141).

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question: who was greater in courage (*sattva*), Śaṅkhacūḍa or Jīmūtavāhana? (KSS 12.23.203)
- Source answer: Śaṅkhacūḍa. Jīmūtavāhana's act was "perfected over many births" (*bahujanmasiddham*); Śaṅkhacūḍa, though he had escaped death, ran after Garuḍa and offered his body insistently (205–206).
- Our answer: same, KSS and Ś births/practice reasoning. The Hindi caste reasoning is rejected (SLATE_AUDIT §5).
- Honest-difficulty: a man who gives his life for a stranger could be called the greater, and habit is not the same as ease; the late evidence (his return) may be thin against a whole tale of giving.

## 3. Locked directions
None in D-048, D-051 or D-055 to D-061. Approved audit (SLATE_AUDIT §5): "The Hindi caste reasoning is rejected (§3.2.2); KSS and Ś's births or practice reasoning is kept." "Vāsuki ... buys his own safety by feeding one subject a day to Garuḍa. The source does not judge this; the adaptation should make it visible ... **without** speeches."

## 4. Charter points
- Caste: no Kshatriya-courage reasoning (§3.2.2, 3.2.4); courage is habit and choice.
- Sacrifice examined: who benefits, who pays, was it free (§3.4.3, 3.4.5): the mother's grief and Malayavatī's loss stay on the page; the miraculous revival must not simply reward the sacrifice.
- Ruler accountability (§3.4.4): Vāsuki's bargain shown by events (Vāsuki stays one ruler, not "all rulers", §5.3).
- Nāgas are people, not disposable tribute (§3.4.1).
- Religious colouring (Bodhisattva within Śaiva frame): handled without sect claims (§3.2.8).
- Malayavatī: keep her reproach; do not frame the noose as romance (§3.3.2).

## 5. Voice plan
- Hidden layer: "two ways of giving your life, and the tale asks which one cost more."
- "And you too": (a) at Śaṅkhacūḍa's refusal, the good deed you would let someone else do; (b) before the question, what you have practised until it no longer feels brave.
- Odd details: why the mother refuses; why Śaṅkhacūḍa goes to Śiva first; why Garuḍa stops.
- Plot object: the crest-jewel, which carries the news to Malayavatī and measures the cost.
- Callbacks: none. J 23 (TC-027) shares the tribute motif only.
