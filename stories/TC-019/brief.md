# TC-019 brief: The thief's son and the three hands

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no (D-081 list). **But** if Vikram's reasoning is rebuilt (see §2) it is our composition and goes to the user under D-041 and SLATE_AUDIT §5/§7.

## 1. Source beats
1. King Sūryaprabha of Vakrolaka has everything but a son. [KSS 12.26.3–6]
2. In Tāmraliptī the merchant Dhanapāla dies; kinsmen seize his wealth "with royal backing"; his widow Hiraṇyavatī and daughter Dhanavatī flee at night with hidden jewels. [KSS 7–12]
3. Outside the city the mother's shoulder strikes a thief impaled on a stake, still alive. [KSS 13–16]
4. At moonrise he sees the girl and offers 1,000 gold pieces for her as wife: he is sonless, and a sonless man misses the higher worlds; any son she bears by another at his bidding will be his *kṣetraja*. [KSS 17–22]
5. The greedy mother agrees and pours water into his hand (the bride-gift). He names the buried gold, asks for cremation and his bones in a holy river, drinks water, dies. They do as asked and settle in Vakrolaka. [KSS 22–31]
6. The young brahman Manaḥsvāmin longs for the courtesan Haṃsāvalī, who wants 500 dīnāras. Dhanavatī sees him from her roof, remembers the thief's permission; her mother sends a maid; he comes for one night for 500 dīnāras and is paid. [KSS 32–46]
7. She bears a son. Śiva in dreams tells her to leave the baby with 1,000 gold at the king's gate, and tells the king to take him. The king names him Candraprabha. [KSS 47–58]
8. Grown, he is made king; Sūryaprabha retires to Vārāṇasī and dies. [KSS 59–62]
9. Against his ministers' advice Candraprabha goes on pilgrimage to do his father's rites in person (Ganges, Prayāga, Vārāṇasī, Gayā). [KSS 63–87; long travelogue, compress, ours]
10. At the well at Gayā, as he offers the *piṇḍa*, three hands rise: a thief's with an iron spike, a brahman's with a kuśa ring, a king's with a signet ring. No one can say whose it is. [KSS 88–92]
11. Betal asks; Vikram answers. [KSS 93–101]

Variants: Hindi XVIII puts the hands in the River Phalgu, not a well. Ś 18 has Betal object to the answer (blood and rearing); the reply is that both were bought. J 16 unread (Emeneau calls its riddle solution "careless"). No difference of answer. Ś/Hindi narrative bodies not read in full (N1).

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question: into whose hand should the offering go? [KSS 12.26.94]
- Source answer and reasoning: the thief's. The begetter "sold himself" for a night; the king's rearing was paid for by the gold at the child's head; so the son is the *kṣetraja* of the man to whom the mother was given by water-gift, by whose leave she conceived, whose wealth it all was. [KSS 96–101]
- Our answer: **PROPOSED, needs the user's approval.** SLATE_AUDIT §5 (TC-019): the purchase rule has a hole, because the thief also paid (1,000 gold for the girl). Betal presses this (as Ś already presses blood and rearing); Vikram's answer must survive it, or A3 rebuilds the reasoning (D-041). Nothing is composed yet; the draft carries the source verdict with the objection argued at full strength, and any rebuilt reasoning is labelled ours.
- Honest-difficulty: the begetter gave the body, the king gave the years, the thief gave only a name and a permission, and all three were paid; a reader can fairly answer "the one who raised him" or "none of the three".

## 4. Charter points
- Daughter sold by her mother (§3.3.3, §3.3.6): keep it the mother's act, visible as coercion; do not turn it into Dhanavatī's bargain. Her agency stays where the source puts it: she picks Manaḥsvāmin and decides about the child.
- "Field" doctrine treats a woman as land (§3.3.2, principle 7): voiced by the thief and others, never the narrator's authority.
- Caste label test (§3.2.5a): thief, brahman and king are connected; the verdict runs against the brahman and the king. Keep the three emblems. Brahman "sold himself": one man's act, not a type (§3.2.8; slate pattern §5.3).
- Impalement and kin seizing the estate are the state's cruelty and injustice, shown as such, not as law.
- The thief wins here and in TC-010: place apart in the order; no glamour.
- Sensitivity reader recommended (D-046).

## 5. Voice plan
- Hidden layer: a ghost-light tale about who a person belongs to; heard slowly, about what a name, a body and a childhood each count for.
- "And you too": (a) at the three hands: who in your life gave you your body, your name, your years, and which do you call father; (b) at the thief's bargain: what do you bargain for when you are afraid of dying with nothing behind you.
- Odd details: why a dying man wants a son; why the mother says yes (flight, hunger, fear, not a villain); why Dhanavatī, not the mother, picks the brahman; why Śiva's dream (the family cannot keep the child).
- Plot object: the water poured into a hand, the same hand that rises at Gayā.
- Callbacks: none. (TC-010 also has a winning thief; no use in referring to it.)
