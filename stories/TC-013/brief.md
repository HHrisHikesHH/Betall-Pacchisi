# TC-013 brief: Harisvāmin, who died of poisoned food

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no (D-081)

## 1. Source beats
Base: KSS 13 (12.20.1–60), a "short" tale (2). Evidence: SOMADEVA_TALE_EVIDENCE TC-013 (AI reading of GRETIL, not collated).
1. Vārāṇasī: Harisvāmin, son of the honoured brahman Devasvāmin, has a wife of matchless beauty, Lāvaṇyavatī. [source: KSS 3–5]
2. Asleep on a moonlit roof, she is carried off through the air by the Vidyādhara prince Madanavega. [source: KSS 6–9]
3. He searches all night, laments, is barely kept from suicide by kin, gives away his wealth and wanders to holy places. [source: KSS 10–28]
4. Starving in the hot season, he stands silent at the door of the brahman Padmanābha. The host's wife pities him and gives rice cooked in milk, ghee and sugar. [source: KSS 29–40]
5. He leaves the bowl under a banyan to wash. A hawk with a black snake in its claws settles; poison drips from the dead snake into the bowl; he eats it unaware. [source: KSS 41–46]
6. In agony he tells her the food has poisoned him, says she will bear the sin of killing a brahman unless she cures him, and dies. [source: KSS 47–50] **Ours:** rendered as a dying man's belief: "she will carry my death", no caste weight. [ours, SLATE_AUDIT §5]
7. Padmanābha, angry for his guest, drives out his innocent wife; she goes to a holy place for penance. [source: KSS 51–52] **Ours:** she is named, with an interior. [ours]
8. In Dharmarāja's court the question of whose the sin is goes undecided. [source: KSS 53] The abducted wife is never mentioned again. [source: KSS 9, 53]
9. Betal asks Vikram. [source: KSS 53–54]

Variants: Ś 12: poison from a snake in a hollow of the banyan, no hawk (OCR; poisoning passage only). Hindi XII (Platts, answer only): wife acts "at the bidding of her husband"; narrative unread, hawk unchecked. J 12 and BKM 13 unread (title-level). Hawk or snake alone is open (D-034).

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question: whose is the brahman-murder (*kasya sā brahmahatyā*), among snake, hawk, food-givers? (KSS 12.20.53–54)
- Source answer: not the snake (helpless, eaten by its enemy, 56); not the hawk (hungry, 57); not the couple (blameless, acting from virtue, 58); the sin is the dull-witted person's who, "without reflection" (*avicāryaiva*), would say it belongs to any of them (59).
- Our answer: same. Question reworded without the worth-weighted category: whose is the guilt of this man's death (SLATE_AUDIT §5, approved). Dilemma unchanged.
- Honest-difficulty: someone must answer for a death, and refusing every candidate can feel evasive; the husband who expelled her may seem the plain culprit. The text does not say whether the hasty speaker belongs to the court (N1); do not pin him.

## 4. Charter points
- *Brahmahatyā* is worth-weighted: remove from the narrated question; keep both households' brahman identity as sympathetic (§3.2.5a/b, §5.3 counterweight).
- Expelled wife: injustice shown by events, not narration (§3.3.3, §3.5); do not invent her consent or later life (§3.3.6).
- Abducted wife is felt as loss, not a trigger dropped without trace (§3.3.2); no rescue.
- Verdict must not read as a lecture; avoid Vikram stating a thesis (§3.5).
- Question form shared with TC-001, 022, 027: keep the plot distinct.

## 5. Voice plan
- Hidden layer: "a tale with no villain, and the only guilty party may be the listener."
- "And you too": (a) at the dying accusation, the reader's first reach for someone to blame; (b) at the end, the haste with which they judged.
- Odd details: why she gives food (pity); why he leaves the bowl (washing); why the husband expels her (grief and anger for a guest).
- Plot object: the bowl, left open under a tree; wisdom grows from what lands unseen in what we hold.
