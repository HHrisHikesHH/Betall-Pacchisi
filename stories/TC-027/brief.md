# TC-027 brief: The ogre and Arimaulimaṇi's kingdom (J 23)

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: yes (D-081: the answer has no source reasoning; ours is composed, D-041) · **Settled by the user: D-091 (2026-11-27).**

## 1. Source beats
All [source: J 23, Tr pp. 133–139; P and V only] unless marked.
1. In Ekacakravarti on the Narmadā townspeople kill a river creature and find alive in it a girl, Śobhāvatī, whose father once proposed her to King Arimaulimaṇi; he resolves to marry her.
2. A disguised brahman-ogre warns him that he and his father mean to eat her. The king insults the ogre's father and dares him; "guard your whole kingdom."
3. Tālajaṅgha gathers the ogres from Laṅkā; they eat every man they catch. His mother Kumbhodarī takes a courtesan's form and eats the men who visit.
4. She takes Vidyādhara as lover; jealous, she comes as his mother, then as a bee, kills him in his sleep; her husband eats him.
5. The king accuses her; she names herself and says the killings avenge his insult. He agrees to give the ogre one man a day.
6. The lot falls on a weeping mother's son. The foreign king Mahābala promises to kill the ogres; the king promises him "this kingdom". Mahābala beats Kharjūrajaṅgha, makes him swear no violence in the city, releases him.
7. The king treats Mahābala with little regard. Angered, Mahābala mocks the king's name, releases the ogre from his oath, and leaves; the ogres eat the citizens.
8. The king flees with Śobhāvatī to Mahābala's country, cannot explain himself; Mahābala smiles, makes the ogre swear again, gives troops and restores him.
Gaps: Tālajaṅgha in the question rests on P alone (V: *atha vā rākṣasajaṅghaḥ*, T p. 138 n. 38); the deaths are told through several ogres and the Sanskrit attaches the citizens' deaths after the release to "that ogre" (*tena rākṣasena*, T p. 138; N3 §4(a), unresolved); the text never says in so many words the king broke his promise; careless grammar at the end (Tr p. 139 n. 18). No Newari parallel; W gives names only. J places TC-016 (J 24) right after it.

## 2. Riddle and verdict
