# TC-002 brief: Mandāravatī and the three suitors

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no (D-081). Note: D-051 is provisional, so the wish in beat 2 is flagged for the drafter and critic.

## 1. Source beats
(Frame: the corpse is recaptured, KSS 12.9.1–5, then the tale.)
1. At the brahman settlement Brahmasthala on the Yamunā, the learned Agnisvāmin has a very beautiful daughter, Mandāravatī [KSS 5–7].
2. Three equally accomplished youths from Kānyakubja each ask for her; each would sooner die than see her given to another. Her father, fearing two would die, gives her to none; the three stay on, gazing at her [KSS 8–11]. **Ours (D-051):** before this, she has and voices a wish of her own, which the father overrules.
3. She dies suddenly of a burning fever [KSS 12]. The three cremate her [13].
4. One builds a hut on the cremation ground and sleeps on her ashes, living on alms; one takes her bones to the Ganges; one becomes a wandering ascetic [KSS 14–15].
5. At Vakrolaka the ascetic is a guest in a brahman's house. A child cries without stopping; the mistress, in anger (*krudhā*), throws it into the fire [KSS 16–19].
6. The guest, horrified, refuses to eat there; the householder revives the child from the ashes with a spell from a book [KSS 20–23].
7. That night the ascetic steals the book and returns to the cremation ground, where the bone-bearer has also just arrived [KSS 24–28].
8. He reads the spell over the ashes and Mandāravatī rises alive, "as if made of gold" [KSS 29–32]. **Ours (D-051):** this moment is told from where she stands: she wakes and perceives.
9. All three, overcome with desire, quarrel: spell, bathing-place, or guarded ashes [KSS 33–35].
10. Betal asks; Vikram answers; Betal returns [KSS 36–41].

Variants: Ś 2 differs materially (snakebite; a suitor dies on the pyre and is revived; roles father/brother/husband; no bone-bearer). Hindi II follows KSS. J 2 unread. Not used.

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question: whose wife does she properly become (KSS 12.9.36)?
- Source answer: the reviver is her father (a father's work); the bone-bearer "is counted" her son; the ash-keeper "is called" her husband, acting as a devoted lover (12.9.38–40).
- Our answer: **same** (no D-041 change). The wish and her presence are additions around the verdict, not a changed verdict.
- Honest-difficulty: the reviver gave life and did the hardest thing; the bone-bearer's labour and the ash-keeper's devotion are both real. Classifying the claimants by the relationship their act resembles is itself arguable, and the text gives no reaction from her.

## 4. Charter points
- She does not speak or choose in the source (N1). Agency stays voice, wish, presence; it must not decide the outcome (§3.3.6, no "agency the plot cannot honour").
- Threats of death are the suitors'; withholding her was the father's decision. Assign each to the person who made it (D-048; §3.3.3, victims not blamed). Her wish is her own and is not a reply to the threats.
- No speech of hers ratifies the verdict, and she does not choose a husband afterwards (D-051). That would collapse the riddle.
- The child in the fire: keep it; do not make it a statement about women (§3.3.1). The mistress is one angry person.
- Brahman identity kept (§3.2.5a, connected to who holds learning and spells); suitors and household drawn as devoted, learned, grieving individuals, not a collective type (§3.2.8, SLATE_AUDIT §5.3).
- The ascetic steals from a host who revived a child; show it as theft driven by grief, not as clever virtue (§3.4.4).

## 5. Voice plan
- Hidden layer: heard fast it is a ghost story about three lovers; heard slowly it is about who gets to name what she is.
- "And you too": (a) at the father's refusal, how often someone has decided for someone out of fear of what a third person might do; (b) at the quarrel, after something precious comes back, who do we see first, the person or our own claim on them.
- Odd details needing a human motive: why the father gives her to none (fear, KSS 11); why the guest refuses food (he thinks it a brahman-demon's house, 20); why a householder keeps a spell book (source silent; do not invent a reason).
- Plot object: the ashes (a bed, a thing guarded) and the bones carried away: what is left of a person that others carry.
- Callbacks: none.
