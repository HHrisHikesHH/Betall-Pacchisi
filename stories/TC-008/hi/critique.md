## Riddle and verdict

The question and the answer match the brief: the bed-connoisseur, with the evidential reason from KSS 12.15.50–51. Betal's objection in 80 is argued at full strength and has three prongs: suspicion is not proof, a sense that detects a hidden cause is no less delicate, and the mark was seen by others' eyes. That meets the D-041 honest-difficulty test, and Vikram does not state a thesis. The weak points are Vikram's reply in 82 (#2), the overclaim in 78 (#4) and the pause in 75, which gives the answer away (#3).

## Verdict

Strong grip in the turtle and farmer scenes. The courtesan's scene is the best-written part: she is allowed the joke and her own pace. The draft is weakest at its two thinking points, the pause (75) and Vikram's rebuttal (82), and in the thesis restated in too many places. All three must findings (#1–#3) can be fixed with local rewrites, and no restructuring is needed.

The 3 changes that most raise grip:
1. Rewrite 75 so it asks and does not give the answer, and make 82 a real answer to Betal's three objections.
2. Cut the restated thesis (#5): 83, the last sentence of 4, the pebble gloss and the last two sentences of 92.
3. Compress the opening exposition (5–10) and the coda (83–93), and end on a spoken Betal line.
