# TC-007 brief: Sattvaśīla and the Daitya princess

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no (D-081)

## 1. Source beats
1. In Tāmraliptī a king is served for years, unrewarded, by Sattvaśīla, a poor attendant (*kārpaṭika*) [KSS 12.14.3–9]. **Ours:** he is a genuine commoner (see §4).
2. Hunting a boar, the king is lost in a forest; only Sattvaśīla follows, on foot [KSS 10–14].
3. He gives the king two āmalaka fruits, saying he has lived on such fruit for ten years; the king, ashamed, rewards him but does not feel the debt repaid [KSS 15–31].
4. Sent by sea to ask for a princess for the king, Sattvaśīla sees a golden banner rise from the ocean; a storm drives the ship at it and it begins to sink [KSS 32–38]. The brahmans aboard cry out; he leaps in, sword in hand [39–41]. The ship breaks up [42].
5. Underwater: a divine city, a temple of Kātyāyanī; a maiden goes into the goddess's halo and he follows into a richer city and falls in love [KSS 43–54]. He bathes, surfaces in the king's garden tank, lovesick [55–63].
6. He tells the king; the two sail to the spot and the king leaps in too [KSS 64–74].
7. The maiden, a Daitya princess, is drawn to the king; he declines her coolly, then visits her second city [KSS 75–98].
8. She says she is Kālanemi's daughter and, "now you are my father" (102), places herself and her cities in his power [KSS 99–102]. The king gives her to Sattvaśīla; she agrees [103–104]. He says one fruit is repaid, one still owed (106).
9. She gives the king a sword and an age-removing fruit; Sattvaśīla rules her cities [KSS 108–110].
10. Betal asks (110); Vikram answers (112–113); Betal returns (114).

Variants: Ś 8 and Hindi VIII: same answer, different reasoning (first to do a kindness; service beyond one's office); not used. J 7's answer is reported, not documented. The e-text calls the king Caṇḍasena at 12.14.3 and Caṇḍasiṃha afterwards; pick one.

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question: which of the two showed greater courage in leaping (KSS 12.14.110)?
- Source answer and reasoning: Sattvaśīla; he leapt without knowing and without expectation (*nirāsthaḥ*); the king knew and entered with expectation (113a). Verse 113b adds the king did not desire her, thinking her not to be won by desire.
- Our answer: **same**, with the KSS reasoning and the ambiguity of 113b left intact (SLATE_AUDIT §5). The commoner change does not alter the dilemma.
- Honest-difficulty: the ship was already sinking when Sattvaśīla leapt, and the king risked a king's life, knowingly, for a debt; a reader could call that the braver act.

## 4. Charter points
- Class change, recorded (§3.6, SLATE_AUDIT §5). Original: a poor prince serving as a menial. Proposed: a commoner attendant. Effect: his service is no longer honoured because of royal birth; the reward becomes mobility, not restoration. The class invisibility, the king never noticing ten years of hunger, is the tale's quiet subject: show it through events (§3.5).
- The princess is given away. Source records her assent (103–104); give it weight as her own judgement and invent no choice scene (§3.3.6). Her shift from husband-seeking to "father" is source (87, 102): keep it.
- The king's "one fruit repaid" is his line; let the reader feel the coldness without comment.
- Daitya and Asura are mythological; do not treat them as a collective type of villain (§3.2.8).
- The brahmans aboard are passengers only; no stereotype.
- The king is held to account without becoming a villain; he is the "king who repays" counterweight (SLATE_AUDIT §5.3).

## 5. Voice plan
- Hidden layer: heard fast, a sea marvel; heard slowly, how a debt can be repaid only by someone who feels it.
- "And you too": (a) at the two fruits, who has been serving you for years that you never asked how they eat; (b) at the king's cool refusal, what the unreachable thing is in your own life.
- Odd details: why the king never asked (shame, 24–25); why Sattvaśīla carries the fruit; why he leaps (source: to find the cause, a duty to the ship); compress the slow opening (hunt) and keep the fruits.
- Plot object: the two āmalaka fruits as a measure of debt (106).
- Callbacks: optional echo of the frame's fruit gifts, only after D-086 fixes the order and with a one-clause recap; TC-012 shares an undersea journey made twice, so no call-forward without checking it.
