The non-CA additions are: atmosphere ("कमलों से भरी", §3; "जब तक लोहा लाल न हो गया", §12; the market's passers-by, §13; "सिपाही उसे घर से ले गए", §16), and six narrator lines that no source contains (§6, §10 last paragraph, §15 "बेटे की मौत को अब एक वजह मिल गई थी", §16 second sentence, §17 second sentence, §18 last sentence). **3.3 CA-05 (the Hindi sign pun), pending user approval.** What it does, exactly:
- §3 realises KSS's *dantaracanā* as "एक फूल लेकर उसे दाँतों से कुछ गढ़ा" (deliberately vague; CB-01, the bitten lotus, is not used).
- §4 the minister's son decodes it as "फूल को दाँतों से गढ़ा, यानी वह दाँत गढ़ने वाले की ... बेटी है", a Hindi pun (दाँत + गढ़ना) standing for *dantaghāṭaka*, glossed at once.
- The other three decodings (कर्ण पर उत्पल, पद्म, hand on heart) are close renderings of KSS 81–83 with a gloss; they are translation rather than invention, and survive whatever the user decides.

**Can it be removed or swapped cheaply? Yes.** Signs may be reduced to at least one (blueprint §7.2). Dropping the teeth sign costs one sentence in §3 and one clause in §4 (about 25 words), and the ivory-worker origin survives intact through §4's third paragraph, where the minister's son already says what he has heard ("संग्रामवर्धन नाम का एक बड़ा हाथीदाँत का कारीगर", KSS 84–85). A swap to CB-01 is equally cheap but also pending (OQ-19). One caution either way: in everyday Hindi "दाँत गढ़ने वाला" sounds like a maker of teeth, which happens to match Tawney's "dentist" reading of the disputed word; a Hindi reader may smile at the wrong thing (CR-09).

## 4. Ethics (Charter §§3.3–3.5)

**4.1 The three narrator lines the writer flagged** (trace §5 item 2). None is an authorial verdict on a person; all are epistemic fences. Judged against Charter §3.5 ("made visible by what happens, or by what the narrator says?") and North Star §5.1 and §10:
- **§6** "पद्मावती ने सचमुच क्या कहा था, यह बूढ़ी ही जानती थी" does real R8 work in few words. The added clause "और बूढ़ी को उपहार मिले थे" repeats what §5 showed and tips the doubt onto the nurse's honesty, which edges towards the venal-servant figure the blueprint removed (§6 role table, Charter §3.4.1). **Keep the first clause; drop the second.**
- **§17** "इससे आगे उसने क्या समझा, कोई नहीं जानता।" fences CA-02, which is already in its narrowest form; with §18's line it makes two "कोई नहीं जानता" within a few lines. **Cut, or fold the limit into how the supposition is phrased.**
- **§18** "उसने क्या कहा, कोई नहीं जानता।" is the most necessary of the three: without it, "दिलासा दिया, एक घोड़े पर बिठाया" followed by "सुख से रहने लगा" risks reading as acceptance (blueprint §11, "whether the silence in B18 reads as acceptance"). **Keep one line of this kind here, worded so it does not echo §17.**

The same "ledger" voice appears in three lines the writer did not flag (§10 last paragraph, §15 second sentence, §16 second sentence); see CR-06. Of these, §15 "बेटे की मौत को अब एक वजह मिल गई थी" is the best: it is a noticing (grief seizing an explanation), not a ruling, and could stay.

**4.2 The drink and consent.** The instruction (§11) shows the plan clearly and the giving is not narrated (IC-07), as required. The problem is the sequence in §12 (CR-02): he reaches the window, and "inside" she is already motionless. Read literally, the planned incapacitation happened without him, which points to her own drinking or someone else's hand. That is the reading R6 forbids ("never her choice and never her fault") and it also removes the prince's first wrong from the events, though Vikram later names it. A neutral passage of time between his arrival and her state, with her condition tied to the plan, would restore KSS's gap without narrating a cup or any intimacy. §11's "पहले की तरह उसके पास जाना" should name the route (as §12 does), not "going to her as before", which a reader may take as the lovers' usual night.

**4.3 The mother's death (item (h)).** §19 says nothing about manner: "फिर उसकी पत्नी भी नहीं रही।" "फिर" gives only sequence, which KSS 185 has. **Compliant.** Nothing in the draft presupposes the outcome of OQ-20.

**4.4 The dilemma change and Option A.** Compliant with D-060 A–E. Vikram's answer is "इन तीनों में से किसी का नहीं ... राजा कर्णोत्पल का" (Option A); the friends' acts are "उनका अपना पाप" and "दोस्त की सेवा हो या प्रेम" washes none (exemptions removed); scope is stated (C7); Betal speaks KSS 191 only, at full strength in itself. Two cautions:
- **In-world motivation.** §20 (4) rebuts "service or love" though nobody in the tale has argued either; the rebuttal answers KSS 192–193, which the reader never sees. Tying it to the persons ("for his friend's sake", "in his love") would make it an answer to the tale, not to the source (CR-04).
- **Balance.** Betal's objection is 22 words; Vikram's reasoning is 348. Because D-060 E fixes Betal at 191, the only lever left for keeping the objection felt (F5, C4) is Vikram's length and his concessions.

**4.5 Is the reasoning a lecture? Partly (C6).** It is an argument, not a thesis: it concedes ("यह सच है ... ये बातें झूठी नहीं"), it answers the objection, and it does not rank. But it is long for a Betal ending (16% of the tale; §20 as a whole is 426 of 2,183 words), structured like a judgment ("रही पद्मावती ... अब मौतें ..."), and closes by announcing the tale's motif. Where it goes beyond the approved outline it is listed separately (CR-05). A target of roughly 180–230 words looks achievable without losing any approved point: drop §20 (8)'s motif sentence, merge §20 (5)'s two statements that the charge was never put to her, cut "ये बातें झूठी नहीं", and keep one of "वह उसी सज़ा का डर था" and the rhetorical question that follows it.

**4.6 Other Charter checks.** No caste or jāti marker; the craft is a skill (§3.2.4, §3.2.5 (a) connected and kept). No religious community villainised; the ascetic is unmarked (§3.2.8). The dead child is used by the scheme and the king, and §15 makes that use visible (§3.4.2). The ending is not comfortable: contentment is followed by "पर" and the deaths (§19). Padmāvatī is neither flawless nor a villain (§3.3.5). The prince has no hesitation and no interiority; the blueprint permits that, but he risks reading as an instrument rather than a person who chose (MINOR, CR-15).

## 5. Literary notes (pacing, shape, curiosity, dialogue, restraint, Hindi)

**5.1 Shape and pacing.** Sections, in words: opening and household 171 (§1–§2); lake and decoding 309 (§3–§4); courtship 439 (§5–§9); poison and plan 270 (§10–§11); the night, the market, the lie 311 (§12–§14); trial and sentence 133 (§15–§16); forest, removal, deaths 124 (§17–§19); question and reasoning 426 (§20). The middle is well paced and the pivot at §10 lands. The tale's emotional low point after the sentence (forest, removal, the parents' deaths) gets fewer words than any other movement, about a third of the reasoning's 348. Restraint is required there (no invented thoughts), but the Editorial Guide asks grief to be "warm and unhurried"; place, time and observable detail are the tools (CR-08).

**5.2 Curiosity.** The blueprint sets the tale not as a mystery (§11: the reader knows the lie is a lie), so its one true puzzle is the signs. §3 handles them well ("बुद्धिशरीर ने भी देखा था" withholds his understanding), but §2 has already told the reader Karṇotpala, Kaliṅga, ivory, and Padmāvatī, so the withholding is wasted and §4 repeats facts the reader has had twice (CR-01). The blueprint allows CA-01 "before or alongside the lake"; placing it at the arrival in Kaliṅga (§5, where they "ask for" the house) keeps the parents early and lets the decoding land.

**5.3 Dialogue.** Buddhiśarīra is crisp and plausible; Padmāvatī's §9 speech is natural and alive; the prince's lines are few and right for him. Vikram's speech is written prose, not speech to a corpse on one's back: long relative clauses ("जिस बात के लिए मैं राजा को दोष दे रहा हूँ, कि ..."), enumerations, connectives. Shorter spoken sentences would also shorten it.

**5.4 Restraint (D-044).** The marking (§12) is restrained and correct in its facts. It is also three procedural sentences for the tale's central wrong; one observable detail that is not about her body (the room, the iron, his hand) could give it weight without spectacle (optional, CR-15).

**5.5 Register (Q-402 open; provisional plain Hindustani-leaning default).** Mostly consistent: everyday narration (दोस्त, शहर, ख़बर, ज़हर, बाज़ार) with tatsama names and a few needed terms (गांधर्व, त्रिशूल, तपस्वी, युक्ति). Character register differences work: the ascetic says "शराब" and "हृदय", the narrator "मदिरा" and "दिल". The Persianate administrative words (कोतवाल, सिपाही, फ़ैसला, सज़ा) in an ancient Kaliṅga are not wrong for this tradition (the Hindi witness itself has a kotwāl, blueprint §2.1) but are the main point where Q-402 will bite; noted, not a defect.
