## 6. Points left unclear or unresolved in this chronology

- **K4 and K25–K26, K28–K29, K36**: the readings that depend on the Sanskrit (sources.md §11.2). Treat the rows as the reading of this session and N1, not as specialist-confirmed.
- **Ś7, Ś23**: whether the printed text's "king" and "father" are one man (INF), and what "the earlier account" is. The Ś apparatus shows manuscript variation at Ś5 and Ś7.
- **Hindi rows**: all read from the Devanagari; the residual doubts are translation issues and word-sense questions for a Hindi specialist (*raṇḍī*, *khiṛkī*, *kutarnā*, *rānī*; sources.md §11.4). The Persian-script line and Forbes's Hindi were not read.
- **J16**: the ascetic's account is uncertain in the Sanskrit (Emeneau's note), and Emeneau's English fills it with an emendation.
- **Poison, by witness (not collapsed):** **KSS:** the dog dies; poison and motive are alleged by the minister's son and are not confirmed by the narrator. **Ś and Hindi:** the narrators identify the food as poisoned; neither reports her thought. **J:** her reported thought presents an apparent intention to kill (*tatas taṃ nihatya kālaṃ nayāmi*), and the next sentence says she sends poisoned food (*viṣasahitadravyam*) to Buddhiśarīra; the apparent sequence is thought, then dispatch. The syntactic referent of *taṃ* is a Sanskritist's question (sources.md §11.5) and specialist confirmation is outstanding. **No witness's account is transferred to another**, and KSS's ambiguity is not resolved by the others.
- **Her reactions:** J14 reports that she wakes, finds the ornaments gone and laments. KSS reports **no speech or reaction from her during the marking and the banishment (K30–K35, K37)**; K36 (182cd) **may** indicate she supposed a stratagem of the minister's son's, scope and syntax unestablished.
- **No row establishes** whether she consented to the union, the rite or her removal; the sources are silent or differ, and this record does not decide.
