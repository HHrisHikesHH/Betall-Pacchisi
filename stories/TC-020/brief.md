# TC-020 brief: The boy who laughed

Status: brief · Written 2026-11-27 · User look required: no (D-081 list). **But** the reasoning is partly reframed and this is a "why" question, so the answer is our composition: it goes to the user under D-041 (SLATE_AUDIT §5, §7: "Yes (the reasoning is the answer)").

## 1. Source beats
1. King Candrāvaloka of Citrakūṭa lacks only a suitable wife; hunting, his horse carries him into a forest. [KSS 12.27.4–14]
2. At a lake he meets Indīvaraprabhā, daughter of sage Kaṇva and the apsaras Menakā; Kaṇva rebukes his hunting, the king renounces it, marries her. [KSS 15–50; compress, ours]
3. Riding home they sleep under a fig tree by a tank. [KSS 51–64]
4. At dawn the *brahmarākṣasa* Jvālāmukha, whose dwelling it is, threatens to tear out the king's heart. The terrified king offers a human victim instead. [KSS 65–73]
5. The demon's condition: within seven days, a brahman boy of about seven, brave and discerning, who gives himself willingly, held by mother (hands) and father (feet), killed by the king's own sword. [KSS 74–82]
6. The king returns in despair. Minister Sumati has a golden statue of such a boy paraded, proclaiming the statue and a hundred villages to the parents. [KSS 83–95]
7. A seven-year-old volunteers. His parents think him mad; he argues the body is impure, perishable, worth only the merit it earns; they accept statue and villages. [KSS 96–110]
8. At the tree he rejoices; gods rain flowers. [KSS 111–122; flowers cut or made the crowd's projection, ours]
9. His mother holds his hands, his father his feet; as the king raises the sword, the boy laughs; all, the demon too, stand amazed. **The tale stops here; the outcome is not narrated.** [KSS 123–125]
10. Betal's question; Vikram's answer. [KSS 126–136]

Variants: Ś 19: the mother refuses one son, the father the eldest; the middle son offers himself, father hands him over for wealth. Hindi XIX: second son, "first laughed, and then wept", the king beheads him, plus a verse that woman is the source of misery. J 17 unread. Answer agrees in Ś and Hindi.

## 2. Riddle and verdict
- Betal's question: why did the boy laugh at the very point of death? [KSS 127–128]
- Source answer and reasoning: a threatened creature cries first to its parents, then the king, then the deity present; here the parents held him for wealth, the king would kill him to save himself, the deity would eat him. Seeing such delusion about the body, and "knowing his own wish accomplished", he laughed in wonder and joy. [KSS 130–136]
- Our answer: **changed (D-041), PROPOSED, needs the user's approval.** Keep the indictment (every protector turned killer). Leave "joy at his wish accomplished" unresolved, not romanticised; show his willingness as manufactured by the proclamation, the reward and the adults (SLATE_AUDIT §5). Vikram's wording is not yet composed.
- Honest-difficulty: a reader may hear the laugh as terror, shock, courage, or release, not understanding; a child's mind at that moment cannot be settled.

## 4. Charter points
- Child sacrifice by consent for money (§3.4.2–3): never virtue. The boy's speech about the body is the real valorisation (SLATE_AUDIT): show it as an idea given to a seven-year-old, not wisdom.
- Brahman boy demanded (§3.2.5b): the demon's condition, visible as "a pure life is a worthier offering"; never shared by the narration. *brahmarākṣasa* stays as folklore, not a comment on brahmans.
- Parents (§3.2.8, pattern §5.3): borrow Ś's refusing mother so the household is divided; no collective greed. No poverty added.
- King and state accountable (§3.4.4): king buys his life with a child; minister's proclamation is complicity.
- Queen not blamed for the trespass (§3.3.3); no woman-as-misery verse.
- Ending: open (KSS); nothing in the telling resolves his death or rescue.

## 5. Voice plan
